# Square Pattern

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/types/square-pattern/profile/square-pattern-image.svg)
A surface pattern built around the square as the primary structural or motif element — the simplest and most fundamental shape in geometric pattern design, capable of producing a wide range of visual effects from minimal to complex.

## Overview

Square-based patterns exploit the square's perfect equality of sides and right-angle corners to create patterns with strong structural clarity. The square is the basis of the most fundamental repeat structure in surface pattern (the block repeat), and as a motif it underpins an enormous range of well-established commercial pattern traditions: checkerboards, tartan checks, gingham, tattersall, windowpane, and others. It also forms the structural unit of granny square crochet textile patterning and tessellating tile designs.

## Visual Characteristics

Square patterns read as highly ordered, structured, and geometrically precise. The 90-degree angle creates a rigid, rectilinear visual rhythm. Depending on scale and colour, square patterns can read as minimal and modern (large-scale two-colour blocks), classic and heritage (small gingham), or complex and maximalist (multi-colour plaid with thin overcheck lines). The equal sides and right angles also enable perfect tessellation — squares tile flat surfaces with no gap or overlap.

## Pattern Structure

The square is both a motif and the fundamental unit of the block repeat. In the block repeat, the design tile is a square that tiles in a simple grid. As a motif, squares can be arranged in:
- **Checkerboard:** two alternating square colours in a grid
- **Gingham:** overlapping woven horizontal and vertical bands in two or three tones producing a small-check effect
- **Tattersall:** narrow coloured overcheck lines on a ground, creating a grid of equal rectangles
- **Windowpane:** widely spaced thin lines creating large, open square cells
- **Block repeat:** any motif arranged on a square grid

## Common Motifs

The square itself is the primary motif. Compound square-based motifs include: checkerboard cells, pinwheel square arrangements, cathedral windows (folded square patchwork), and granny square crochet units. Mathematical tessellations built from squares can include complex secondary patterns formed by colouring individual cells of the grid.

## Colour and Style

Colour treatment transforms the apparent style of square patterns significantly:
- **Two-colour high contrast:** checkerboard, stark and graphic
- **Muted multi-colour:** complex tartan or plaid, heritage and traditional
- **Monochrome:** windowpane, clean and minimal
- **Pastel two-colour:** gingham, domestic and casual

Checkerboard in black and white is one of the most versatile and commercially durable geometric pattern formats.

## History and Context

The square has been a fundamental element of decorative pattern across all cultures and eras. Checkerboard patterns appear in ancient Roman floor mosaics and medieval heraldry. Gingham originated in Southeast Asian textile traditions and was imported into European fashion via trade. The granny square, as a crochet unit, became one of the most popular crafting motifs of the 1970s and has seen multiple revivals. In mathematics, square tessellations are the simplest and most studied form of geometric tiling, directly informing computational and Islamic geometric pattern traditions.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- **Fashion:** gingham, plaid, and check patterns are perennial staples in shirting, casual wear, and accessories
- **Home textiles:** gingham in kitchenware and tablecloths; check patterns in upholstery fabrics
- **Wallpaper:** small gingham and tattersall for domestic interiors; bold checkerboard for statement walls
- **Stationery:** graph paper, grid notebooks, and check-based designs
- **Tile and hard surfaces:** square tile patterns translate directly into ceramic and flooring design
