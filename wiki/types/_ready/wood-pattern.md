# Wood Pattern

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/types/wood-pattern/profile/wood-pattern-image.svg)
A surface pattern or texture that replicates the visual appearance of wood grain — its linear fibre structure, annual ring patterns, and characteristic figure variations — used extensively in interior design, flooring, and printed surface simulation.

## Overview

Wood grain pattern is one of the most commercially significant natural texture categories in surface design. The visual structure of wood — parallel longitudinal fibres producing linear grain, annual growth rings producing concentric patterns in cross-section, and various figure effects (curly grain, bird's eye, tiger grain) produced by unusual growth conditions — provides a rich visual vocabulary for both decorative surface pattern and material simulation. Wood-effect printing on laminates, vinyl flooring, wallpaper, and furniture surfaces is a major commercial application driven by demand for the aesthetic quality of wood at lower cost.

## Visual Characteristics

Wood grain patterns are defined by the direction and character of the wood fibre arrangement:
- **Straight grain:** fibres run parallel to the tree's main axis; produces even, parallel lines in flat-sawn lumber; associated with oak and maple
- **Interlocked grain:** fibres reverse direction in successive growth layers; produces a distinctive ribbon stripe effect visible when lumber is quarter-sawn
- **Wavy grain:** fibres follow a wavy path; produces a rippled, undulating surface pattern
- **Irregular grain:** caused by knots, burls, or crotch wood; produces complex, swirling patterns around interruptions in the growth

Annual growth rings (each representing one year of growth) are visible in cross-section (end-grain) as concentric circles and in flat-sawn boards as the distinctive cathedral arches of grain.

## Pattern Structure

The visual pattern of wood grain changes dramatically based on how the lumber is cut:
- **Flat-sawn (plain-sawn):** boards cut tangentially to the growth rings; produces the broad, arching "cathedral" grain pattern; the most common cut for visual effect
- **Quarter-sawn:** boards cut radially; produces straight, parallel edge grain lines; more stable and less common; reveals ray fleck pattern in oak
- **Rift-sawn:** boards cut at an angle; produces straight, diagonal parallel lines with no ray fleck
- **End-grain:** cross-sectional cut; reveals circular growth ring pattern

## Common Motifs

Named decorative figure patterns found in specific wood species:
- **Bird's eye:** small, swirling, eye-like dots scattered across the grain surface; found in some maple and cherry trees; caused by unusual growth indentations
- **Curly / fiddleback:** wavy, rippled grain producing a light-catching figure across the surface; found in curly maple and cherry; named from its use in violin construction
- **Rift grain:** clean diagonal parallel lines; seen in oak and walnut quarter-cut boards
- **Tiger grain:** an unpredictable aberration in maple producing cross-grain stripes

## Colour and Style

Wood palettes are warm and naturalistic:
- **Light woods:** pale blonde (ash, birch, maple, pine) — associated with Scandinavian and contemporary minimalist interiors
- **Mid-tone woods:** warm oak, teak, walnut honey — the most commercially versatile range
- **Dark woods:** deep walnut, mahogany, ebony — associated with traditional and luxury interior styling
- **Reclaimed/weathered:** greyed, oxidised tones — associated with industrial and rustic aesthetics

Wood grain prints in surface pattern use these colour families and the characteristic line structures of the relevant wood species.

## History and Context

The use of wood-grain printing to simulate expensive or rare wood species on cheaper substrates has a long commercial history. Printed wood-grain paper for furniture, cabinetwork, and architectural surfaces was established by the 19th century. In the 20th century, vinyl laminate and Formica surfaces with printed wood-grain patterns became major commercial categories in residential and commercial fit-out. Contemporary advances in high-definition inkjet printing on vinyl, laminate, and ceramic tile have made wood-grain simulation highly accurate, sustaining its position as one of the dominant decorative surface categories in flooring, worktops, and interior cladding.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- **Flooring:** vinyl plank, laminate, and LVT flooring with printed wood-grain surfaces is one of the largest commercial applications
- **Furniture and cabinetry:** laminate and foil-faced boards with wood-grain print
- **Wallpaper:** wood-effect wallpaper panels for interior feature walls
- **Ceramic and porcelain tile:** wood-look tile printed with high-definition wood grain texture
- **Stationery and packaging:** wood-texture finishes for premium paper goods and boxes
