# Gradient Pattern

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/types/gradient-pattern/profile/gradient-pattern-image.svg)
A colour treatment in which a smooth continuous transition between two or more colours is used as the primary surface design element — a technique rather than a motif-based pattern type.

## Overview

A gradient (also called a colour gradation or degradé) is a continuous tonal or chromatic transition between two or more colours across a surface. In surface pattern design, gradients are used both as a background technique behind motif-based compositions and as the primary visual element in their own right — gradient-wash backgrounds, ombré textiles, and gradient-fill geometric patterns. The gradient technique has been used in print and textile production since the 19th century and is now a core tool in digital design software, particularly Adobe Illustrator.

## Visual Characteristics

A gradient is defined by its smooth, uninterrupted colour transition — the visual absence of a clear boundary between colours. The blending can be subtle (tonal variation within a single hue) or dramatic (full hue shift from one colour to another). When gradient is applied to a fabric or surface, the resulting ombré or wash effect gives a sense of light, atmosphere, or movement that flat colour cannot achieve. In contemporary digital design, gradient effects are used in flat vector compositions to add depth and visual interest without additional motif elements.

## Pattern Structure

Gradient patterns are classified by their geometry — the axis or form along which the colour transition occurs:

- **Linear gradient** — colour transitions along a straight axis (left to right, top to bottom, or at any angle); the most common type
- **Radial gradient** — colour radiates outward from a central point; produces circular or oval banding effects
- **Angular (conic) gradient** — colour transitions rotate around a central point; produces a swept or conical effect
- **Diamond gradient** — a rectangular variant of radial; colour radiates outward from centre in a diamond form
- **Mesh gradient** — a multi-point grid-based gradient (Adobe Illustrator Mesh Tool) allowing complex non-linear colour transitions across a surface
- **Freeform gradient** — colour points placed freely across a surface, creating an organic, non-geometric gradation (Adobe Illustrator Freeform Gradient)
- **Shape blur gradient** — gradient applied to the shape of a defined object rather than the background
- **Aurora gradient** — a soft, multi-colour blend imitating the aurora borealis; used in digital art and contemporary surface design

## Common Motifs

Gradient is a colour technique rather than a motif-based category — it applies to shapes and backgrounds rather than generating its own motif vocabulary. Common applications include: ombré stripe (a striped layout where colour shifts progressively along the stripe width), gradient geometric (flat geometric shapes filled with gradient colour), gradient wash (a background gradation over which motifs are layered), and degradé weave (a woven textile version of the gradient using progressive yarn colour changes).

## Colour and Style

Gradient can use any colour combination. Current commercial applications favour:
- Monochromatic gradients (light to dark within one hue) for sophisticated, minimal compositions
- Complementary or analogous colour gradients (e.g., pink to orange, blue to purple) for contemporary fashion and branding contexts
- High-saturation multi-colour gradients ("aurora" or "holographic" style) for digital-native, trend-forward design
- Pastel soft-wash gradients for children's textiles and stationery

High-profile contemporary logo gradients include Instagram (purple/pink/orange/yellow), Microsoft Edge (blue/green), and Tinder (orange/pink) — demonstrating the association of gradient treatment with digital-native brand identity.

## History and Context

The gradient technique in print production dates to the 1880s — Jules Chéret's three-stone lithography process allowed smooth chromatic transitions in colour printing, producing the characteristic warm tonal ranges of his Belle Époque poster work. In textile production, the degradé weave (warp yarn progressively shifted in colour) created ombré effects on handwoven fabrics long before digital reproduction. The introduction of digital gradient tools in Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop from the late 1980s made precise gradient production accessible to surface designers. The gradient has seen commercial revival as a dominant surface design trend through the 2010s and 2020s, driven by digital brand identity and social media aesthetics.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- **Fashion textiles** — ombré fabric (dye-gradated yarn or printing) is commercially active in womenswear and swimwear
- **Digital print fabric** — gradient printing is well-suited to digital textile printing, enabling smooth tonal transitions not achievable in screen printing
- **Wallpaper** — gradient wash wallpaper is commercially active as a contemporary minimalist interior option
- **Branding and licensing** — gradient is widely used in product packaging, stationery, and licensed print design
- **Swimwear and activewear** — ombré and gradient colour effects are commercially active in performance and fashion swimwear
