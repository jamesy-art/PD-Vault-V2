# puzzle-pattern

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/types/puzzle-pattern/profile/puzzle-pattern-image.svg)
A novelty geometric motif derived from the interlocking, tab-and-blank shapes of jigsaw and mechanical puzzles, used to suggest connection, problem-solving, and play.

## Overview

The puzzle pattern draws on the visual vocabulary of physical puzzles — objects designed to be taken apart or reassembled, most recognisably the jigsaw puzzle. ## Visual Characteristics

The defining visual unit is the interlocking jigsaw piece: an irregular tile with projecting "tabs" and receiving "blanks" that lock into neighbouring pieces, originating from the practice of sawing a mounted map or picture along the outlines of its sections. Related puzzle forms referenced in the source contribute distinct silhouettes if drawn on: the grid-based sliding puzzle (e.g. the 15 Puzzle), the interlaced rings of the nine linked-rings puzzle, and the flat geometric pieces of tiling puzzles such as Tangram.

## History and Context

Jigsaw puzzles were invented around 1760 by British engraver and cartographer John Spilsbury, who mounted a map on wood and sawed it along country outlines as a geography teaching aid; this remained the primary use of jigsaw puzzles until about 1820. Puzzles as a broader category are much older — the nine linked-rings puzzle originated in China during the Warring States period (475–221 BCE), and riddles, the earliest documented puzzle form, appear in Greek mythology (e.g. the riddle of the Sphinx) and throughout the Middle Ages.
