# Concrete Pattern

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/types/concrete-pattern/profile/concrete-pattern-image.svg)
A texture-led surface pattern referencing the visual character of architectural concrete — including the raw cast surface of poured concrete, decorative stamped and patterned concrete, and formwork-textured concrete — used in interior design, surface materials, and digitally produced backgrounds.

## Overview

Concrete pattern in surface design draws on the aesthetic of exposed concrete architecture — a visual language associated with Brutalism, modernism, urban environments, and industrial material honesty. As a texture reference, concrete provides a neutral, granular surface that functions as a background or structural element in surface design rather than as a decorative motif. Its commercial applications are primarily in interior materials (wallpaper, tile, printed flooring) and as a background texture in graphic design and stationery. The source available covers architectural and construction-focused concrete patterning rather than directly SPD-specific applications.

## Visual Characteristics

Concrete surface texture ranges from the fine, near-smooth character of polished or ground concrete through the coarse aggregate surface of raw poured concrete to the deeply imprinted texture of formwork-marked or stamped concrete. Colour is inherently neutral — grey tones from near-white to dark charcoal, sometimes with warm or cool undertones depending on aggregate composition. Decorative concrete surfaces can imitate other materials: stamped concrete replicates cobblestone, brick, slate, and wood plank textures. Graphic concrete uses photographic or geometric imagery embedded into the formwork surface, transferring to the cast concrete face.

## Pattern Structure

Concrete-derived patterns appear in several structural formats:
- **Texture all-over** — fine granular or aggregate concrete surface repeated as an all-over tonal texture
- **Stamped/imitation patterns** — concrete stamping replicates cobblestone (running bond layouts), brick (running bond, herringbone), slate (irregular rectilinear forms), and wood plank (parallel linear forms) patterns — these use the structural logic of the imitated material
- **Graphic concrete** — geometric or photographic imagery applied to concrete surface at architectural scale; translatable into surface pattern as geometric overlay on textured ground
- **European Fan and Ashlar Cut Slate** — named decorative concrete stamp patterns that generate specific geometric surface structures

## Common Motifs

Concrete pattern has no figurative motif. Visual interest is derived from:
- Aggregate texture and surface grain
- Formwork board or plank impressions (producing linear marks)
- Stamped imitation of cobblestone, brick, slate, wood
- Geometric overlay from graphic concrete applications
- Joint lines and seam patterns from panel systems

## Colour and Style

Concrete's natural colour range covers neutral greys — from near-white through mid-grey to dark charcoal — with subtle warm or cool tonality. In surface design applications this palette reads as modern, minimal, industrial, and urban. The aesthetic is associated with contemporary architecture, Scandinavian minimalism, and the broader "raw material" design trend. Coloured or pigmented concrete expands beyond grey but is less common as a surface design reference point.

## History and Context

Concrete as a construction material has ancient origins — Roman opus caementicium is an early form. Decorative concrete patterning as documented in the available source focuses on architectural stamped concrete — a 20th-century technique producing decorative surface textures in poured concrete floors, driveways, and patios. As a surface design aesthetic, concrete texture entered mainstream interior design via the influence of Brutalist architecture in the mid-20th century and its subsequent reassessment as a design influence from the 2000s onwards.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- **Wallpaper and wall covering** — concrete texture wallpaper is an active commercial category in interior design, particularly in contemporary and Scandi-minimal aesthetics
- **Tile and surface materials** — concrete-look porcelain and ceramic tile is a major commercial category in floor and wall tile markets
- **Digital backgrounds** — concrete texture is widely used as a digital background element in graphic design and social media
- **Stationery** — concrete texture appears as a background element in minimal and modernist stationery design
- **Printed flooring** — vinyl and laminate flooring uses concrete texture prints as a commercial product category
