Growing up in a small city in India, where a career in art was not exactly the expected path, I was naturally and stubbornly drawn to colour, detail, and the quiet pleasure of making things beautiful. Much of that came from home. My mother was a textile enthusiast and a cutting and tailoring instructor, and watching her embroider fabrics, stitch my clothes, and design them with her own hands was my first design education. She dressed me like her doll, and I sat beside her making my own dolls from scratch, dressing them with her leftover fabrics, running my own quiet little experiments with thread and textile. I did not know it then, but I was already a designer. My father brought a different kind of knowing. His family were farmers, and he carried that deep connection to the land with him always, tending to every native flower and vegetable in our garden and narrating to me the stories of plants and birds with the patience of someone who understood that nature has a language of its own. He taught me about the Neel-kanth, the Indian Roller bird, its cultural meaning and sacred symbolism in India, considered auspicious and deeply connected to the divine. He came from a community where nothing was wasted, where every scrap was made into something useful. From him I inherited my instinct for functionality, for sustainability, and for finding beauty in the overlooked. My love of botanicals, birds, and nature in my work is not just aesthetic. It is memory. Moving to New Delhi for NIFT (National Institute of Fashion Technology) was its own kind of awakening. Suddenly I was surrounded by people from every corner of India, each carrying their own visual culture, textiles, and aesthetic. It was competitive, humbling, and completely exhilarating. I had to prove myself and I decided to do it head on. NIFT taught me how to channel raw creativity into purposeful, market-ready design, and my favourite corner of it was always Print Design. My print faculty, who I suspect still remembers every one of my more adventurous experiments with a mix of fondness and mild horror, shaped the way I think about surface and pattern to this day. ## Overview

Arti Rajput is a textile and surface pattern designer with over 14 years of industry experience, based in Oregon. Working through Artily Designs, she creates original, handcrafted print and pattern collections available for licensing, commercial collaboration, and freelance projects. Her designs have appeared in products sold through national and international retailers (developed during prior industry roles). She has been recognised by Kravet, Rubelli, ITA, SCAD, and IDA.

## Style and Aesthetic

Vibrant, layered, and nature-kissed with a folkloric undercurrent. Arti's work draws on botanicals, birds, and cultural motifs rooted in Indian heritage and everyday nature observation. The self-described aesthetic is "vibrant, bohemian, whimsical, nature-kissed." Selected Instagram images (8 patterns) suggest rich, hand-painted watercolour and gouache compositions — florals, botanical subjects, and folk-influenced pattern work.

## Techniques and Tools

All work begins by hand — watercolour and gouache are the primary documented media, developed through sketchbook into production-ready repeat patterns. NIFT (National Institute of Fashion Technology, New Delhi) trained, with eight subsequent years in the Indian textile industry. No digital software is specified; production-ready repeat outputs are documented.

## Markets and Clients

Services offered: Collection Development, Custom Design, and Art Licensing (exclusive and non-exclusive). Target markets include home décor, stationery, fashion, and gifting. Products developed during industry roles were sold through national and international retailers (specific retailers not disclosed). Open to licensing enquiries, freelance projects, and collaborations worldwide. Also open to full-time roles.

## Career Path

Arti grew up in India, where her mother was a textile enthusiast and tailoring instructor. She trained at NIFT (National Institute of Fashion Technology, New Delhi) and spent eight years in the Indian textile industry working with brands and export houses. She later relocated to the United States (Oregon) and established Artily Designs. Awards include: Design of Distinction, Most Beautiful Collection (ITA Cover Competition), Outstanding Achievement, and IDA Honourable Mention — from institutions including Kravet, Rubelli, ITA, SCAD, and IDA.

## Pattern Focus

All eight selected Instagram images are patterns. Documented influences and subject matter: botanicals, birds (including the Indian Roller / Neel-kanth bird), nature motifs, folk traditions, and cultural heritage.
