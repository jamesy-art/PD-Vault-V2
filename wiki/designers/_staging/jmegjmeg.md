## Overview

Jamie Girouard is a Vancouver-based primitive rug hooking artist and fiber artist. He builds hand-made functional rugs that explore themes of human emotion, science, technology, and his Acadian heritage. Working within the unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations, his practice integrates zero-waste principles and a commitment to reclaimed materials. He holds a Masters in Educational Technology.

## Style and Aesthetic

Contemporary primitive rug hooking with a conceptual edge. Selected images suggest bold, graphic rug compositions — simplified forms with strong colour fields, characteristic of the primitive hooking tradition. Work explores human emotion, science, and technology as recurring themes, grounding traditional craft in contemporary subject matter.

## Techniques and Tools

Hand-built rug hooking from start to finish: building frames, stretching burlap bases, pulling loops by hand. Materials include reclaimed furniture and repurposed coffee bean burlap sacks. Zero-waste production commitment throughout.

## Markets and Clients

Art and craft market. Work produced as individual functional art objects — decorative rugs rather than mass-market or licensed designs. No commercial licensing clients documented.

## Career Path

Based in Vancouver, drawing on Acadian heritage and the landscapes of Canada's west coast. Holds a Masters in Educational Technology alongside his fiber art practice. Each pulled loop described as "a moment of reflection," connecting traditional craft with personal and cultural introspection.

## Pattern Focus

Selected images suggest graphic rug surface compositions with bold structural arrangements and simplified figurative or abstract motifs, consistent with the primitive rug hooking tradition. Work is three-dimensional textile art; design decisions are embedded in the hand-building process.
