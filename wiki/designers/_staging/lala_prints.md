  [ENG]  Laura Layana (1991) is an Argentine textile designer and photographer
  working remotely. Her work focuses on printed materials, analog processes, and
  imagery for the creative field, collaborating primarily with fashion brands,
  designers, artisans, and artists.


  She studied Fashion and Textile Design at the Faculty of Architecture, Design,
  and Urbanism (FADU) at the University of Buenos Aires, where she graduated in
  2016. She also taught at the same institution. After graduating, she was
  awarded the prize for the Terma Sustainability Contest and presented a
  collection at Buenos Aires Fashion Week.


  For six years, she worked as a print designer for the brand Jazmín Chebar, a
  high-end Argentine brand where she illustrated artworks to be printed on
  exclusive womenswear and accessories. She developed fabrics, jacquards, laces,
  embroideries, and embellishments. During that time, Laura gained expertise in
  researching fresh ideas and new materials for the collection, working
  alongside the creative director, the design team as well as local and
  international suppliers.


  Alongside her professional work, Laura was an active participant in various
  artistic workshops. Among them, she studied photographic editing under the
  guidance of Guillermo Ueno and took part in a photography clinic led by
  Alberto Goldenstein. She also attended PAN, a black-and-white photographic
  development and printing lab run by Belén Messina. Additionally, she was part
  of 'Leer Para Diseño' (LPD), a reading group focused on exploring
  architecture, design, and art. Finally, she took painting classes with Lola
  González.


  Currently, she works as an independent print designer in her textile studio
  based in Buenos Aires focusing on her personal project, Layana . She also
  teaches the Design and Cultural Management course at Universidad Torcuato Di
  Tella, coordinated by Laura Escobar.


  Please get in touch for more information, new projects, commissions or
  collaborations at: infolauralayana@gmail.com

  [ENG] Laura Layana (1991) is an Argentine textile designer and photographer
  who works remotely. Her work focuses on printed materials, analog processes,
  and images for the creative field, primarily collaborating with fashion
  brands, designers, artisans, and artists.


  She studied Clothing and Textile Design at the Faculty of Architecture,
  Design, and Urbanism of the University of Buenos Aires, graduating in 2016.
  She also taught at the same institution. After graduating, she received the
  Terma Sustainability Competition award and presented a collection at Buenos
  Aires Fashion Week.


  For six years, she worked as a print designer for Jazmín Chebar, a prestigious
  Argentine brand, where she created illustrations to be printed on exclusive
  women's clothing and accessories. She developed fabrics, jacquards, laces,
  embroideries, and appliqués. During that time, Laura gained experience
  researching new ideas and materials for each collection, working alongside the
  creative director, the design team, and local and international suppliers.


  Alongside her professional work, she actively participated in various art
  workshops. Among them, she studied photo editing under the direction of
  Guillermo Ueno and participated in a photography workshop led by Alberto
  Goldenstein. She also attended PAN, a black and white photo developing and
  printing lab run by Belén Messina. In addition, she was part of 'Leer Para
  Diseñar' (LPD), a reading group focused on architecture, design, and art.
  Finally, she took painting classes with Lola González.


  Currently, she works as a print designer at her textile studio based in Buenos
  Aires, focusing on her personal project, Layana . She also teaches Design and

## Overview

Laura Layana is an Argentine textile designer and photographer (b. 1991) working remotely from Buenos Aires. She trained at the Faculty of Architecture, Design, and Urbanism (FADU) at the University of Buenos Aires (graduated 2016) and spent six years as a print designer at Jazmín Chebar, a high-end Argentine fashion brand. She now works independently under the project name Layana and teaches at Universidad Torcuato Di Tella.

## Style and Aesthetic

Selected Instagram images suggest a rich, pattern-forward aesthetic with layered motifs and considered colour relationships. The work balances analogue process with commercial print sensibility, drawing on her photography background and photographic editing practice alongside traditional textile design methods. A mix of geometric, botanical, and abstract directions is evident across the selected pattern images.

## Techniques and Tools

Trained in Fashion and Textile Design at FADU. Technical work at Jazmín Chebar covered fabric development, jacquards, laces, embroideries, and embellishments — a broad material vocabulary. Photography is an active parallel practice including analogue darkroom work (black-and-white development at PAN laboratory). Photographic editing, painting, and illustration documented.

## Markets and Clients

Primary market is women's fashion. Six-year tenure at Jazmín Chebar (high-end Argentine brand) delivered seasonal print design for womenswear collections and accessories across SS20 through FW24. Work involved collaboration with creative directors, design teams, and local/international suppliers. Currently works with fashion brands, designers, artisans, and artists as an independent.

## Career Path

Layana studied at FADU, UBA, graduating in 2016. She was awarded the Terma Sustainability Contest prize and showed a collection at Buenos Aires Fashion Week. For six years she worked at Jazmín Chebar, developing expertise in seasonal print and material development. She actively participated in artistic workshops including photography editing, analogue photography, and art-reading circles. She currently runs her independent studio Layana and teaches Design and Cultural Management at Universidad Torcuato Di Tella.

## Pattern Focus

Print output documented across multiple Jazmín Chebar seasons (SS20, FW20, SS21, FW21, SS22, FW22, FW23, SS24, FW24). Eight selected Instagram images suggest original pattern work across geometric, botanical, and abstract directions alongside her personal Layana project.
