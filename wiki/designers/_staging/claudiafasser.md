## Overview

Claudia Fasser is an Italian illustrator and surface pattern designer based in Rotterdam, working across both digital and traditional media. Her practice covers custom illustration, surface pattern design, and branding visuals, offering both a ready-to-license pattern library and bespoke project commissions. She is registered in the Netherlands (KVK 81733259).

## Style and Aesthetic

Fasser's work is defined by its handmade character — the unpredictable line of a dip pen, the vibrant saturation of gouache, the simplified shapes of paper cut-outs. She states a preference for "the quirks and imperfections of anything that is hand-drawn." Selected Instagram pattern images suggest a practice spanning illustrated repeat patterns, character-driven compositions, and textured florals. Selected placement images indicate pattern applied to product and packaging contexts. The work has a distinctive, personal voice that sits apart from cleaner digital illustration conventions.

## Techniques and Tools

Gouache, paper cut-outs, dip pen and ink, digital illustration. Both analogue and digital methods used; stated personal preference for traditional media. Custom projects and production-ready files both available for licensing clients. Pattern library described as ready-for-print and adaptable.

## Markets and Clients

Stationery, clothing (including raincoats), packaging, and branding visuals cited as primary product contexts. Custom illustrations for websites, social media, promotional material, and events also offered. Placement images suggest pattern applied across product and stationery applications.

## Career Path

Italian by origin, relocated to Rotterdam. Home studio practice combining solo illustration work with collaborative client projects. Custom designs and an existing ready-to-sell pattern library both available. Business registration in the Netherlands since active.

## Pattern Focus

Illustrated surface patterns drawing on floral motifs, quirky characters, and mixed-texture compositions. The library spans multiple aesthetic registers — from neat shapes to freely drawn character studies — described as suitable for product, packaging, and stationery applications.
