## Overview

Carol Moreira is a Brazilian designer currently working at Farm Rio (@adorofarm), where she specialises in print engineering — the technical practice of adapting repeating prints for specific garment patterns, creating localised placements and new compositional arrangements from original designs. She also works independently on illustration and pattern commissions.

## Style and Aesthetic

Moreira's personal work is rooted in tropical and botanical illustration. Selected Instagram patterns suggest vibrant, organic motifs consistent with Brazilian fashion aesthetics and the Farm Rio visual language. Her independent pattern work reflects a colourful, nature-driven style suited to women's apparel.

## Techniques and Tools

Primary tool is Procreate for digital illustration and pattern development. Screen printing is documented for client production work (Enseada Rio project). At Farm Rio, she executes print engineering — deconstructing and repositioning original designs within garment shapes to create localised placements, new arrangements, and derived elements.

## Markets and Clients

Womenswear is the primary market. Documented professional client: Farm Rio (Verão 25, Alto Verão 25, Inverno 25 collections — named prints include Borboleta em Flor, Heloisa, Amazônia Ribeirinha, Papillon, Sonho de Coqueiro, Passeio de Rio, Antonia, Relicário Tropical, Lenço Lia). Small brand client: Enseada Rio (localised t-shirt print project). Selected placements (4 images) suggest garment contexts including dresses, tops, kimonos, and rompers.

## Career Path

Active as a pattern designer and illustrator since at least 2020 (evidenced by portfolio project dates). Currently holds a design role at Farm Rio, one of Brazil's leading tropical fashion brands. Her freelance practice pre-dates and continues alongside this role, covering independent pattern commissions and illustration for small brands.

## Pattern Focus

Selected patterns (8 images) indicate tropical and botanical character — consistent with the Farm Rio aesthetic and relevant to her independent illustration practice. Her print engineering work focuses on localised garment placement rather than all-over repeats, requiring precise understanding of both pattern structure and garment construction.
