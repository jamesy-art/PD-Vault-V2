## Overview

Natalia Tello (known as Nati) is an Argentine illustrator and designer based in Madrid, Spain, trained at the University of Buenos Aires. Her practice combines watercolor and digital illustration with surface pattern and stationery design. She is currently studying Art Therapy in Barcelona, and this interest in creativity as a tool for growth and healing informs the emotional register of her personal work alongside her commercial illustration and pattern output.

## Style and Aesthetic

Nature-led, warm, and expressive — botanical motifs, flowers, and organic forms rendered with a watercolor sensibility. Selected Instagram images suggest detailed multi-flower compositions and botanical repeat patterns with a soft, hand-made quality. Palette tends toward warm, earthy, and natural tones with occasional vibrant accents. The aesthetic spans delicate single-flower studies and more complex layered arrangements.

## Techniques and Tools

Specialises in watercolor and digital illustration, blending traditional and digital methods. The hand-painted aesthetic is sustained across selected pattern images, suggesting a layered, textured mark-making approach rather than clean vector construction.

## Markets and Clients

Portfolio covers stationery design, pattern design, and illustration, with particular focus on kidswear and stationery markets. Website project "Whispers of Spring — Textile Graphic Design" (2025) confirms direct surface pattern output. Positioned toward licensing, commissions, and collaborative illustration work. Open to licensing and client collaboration.

## Career Path

Trained at the University of Buenos Aires as an illustrator and designer. Currently based in Madrid, pursuing Art Therapy studies in Barcelona alongside commercial practice. Work spans personal creative projects and commercial illustration, with growing focus on surface pattern design and stationery.

## Pattern Focus

Selected images suggest botanical-focused surface patterns — multi-species flower compositions with delicate linework and layered colour, consistent with a watercolor-influenced approach. The range across selected images (from tightly composed single motifs to denser all-over arrangements) suggests a developing pattern vocabulary suited to fabric, paper goods, and home décor.
