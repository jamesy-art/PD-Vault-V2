## Overview

Lucilla Tubaro is an Italian illustrator based in Rome, working under the identity lucilla.condueelle. Known for a refined graphic style rooted in the aesthetics of subtraction — stark contrasts, deliberate negative space, and a visual tension shaped by East Asian minimalist influence. She works primarily in editorial illustration, publishing, brand identity, and wine label design. Available for commissions.

## Style and Aesthetic

Minimalist and poetic. Tubaro describes her visual language as "gentle, elegant, clean" communication that renders the ordinary in "a subtly ethereal key." Work is characterised by a careful use of contrast and empty space, influenced by Japanese and Oriental aesthetics of subtraction. Series titles — MANIFESTO (negative space), VACANTE (wandering/folk tales), ENCHANTED (decorative), RITUALS / A JAPAN SERIES, MOROCCO / MOROCCO — reflect a conceptual, series-driven practice with strong visual identity per project.

## Techniques and Tools

Graphic illustration with strong sense of line and graphic sign. Works across editorial, branding, children's book, and fine art print contexts. Specific digital or analogue tools not documented.

## Markets and Clients

Editorial, publishing, brand identity, and wine labels. Documented client: DACASTELLO Vini Pregiati (DI.VINO wine label series). Children's book illustration (PUBLISHINGS series). Folk-tale and travel illustration (VACANTE). Additional editorial and brand identity work (ENCHANTED).

## Career Path

Italian illustrator, half Sicilian and half Friulian, born in central Italy and based in Rome. Describes her dual regional background as central to her practice — a "meeting point between two extremes." Works primarily in editorial, publishing, and brand identity, with commissions open.

## Pattern Focus

Not a traditional surface pattern practice. Tubaro's output is series-based conceptual illustration — editorial, folk-tale, and cultural series (Japan, Morocco). Pattern-adjacent work is primarily decorative illustration applied to objects (wine labels, publications) rather than repeat surface pattern. Evidence of surface pattern design in the conventional sense is limited in the documented portfolio.
