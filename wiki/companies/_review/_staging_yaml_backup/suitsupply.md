# Suitsupply

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](438977151_7427487100670196_5788609134434988822_n.jpg)

Dutch vertically integrated men's suiting and fashion brand founded in 2000 by Fokke de Jong in Amsterdam; sources fabric from Italian mills Vitale Barberis Canonico and Reda; global retail across Europe, Asia, and the Americas.

## Overview

Suitsupply is a Dutch men's suit and fashion brand founded in 2000 by Fokke de Jong, who initially ran the company from his dorm room at the University of Amsterdam. The company is vertically integrated and privately held. Suitsupply is a member of the Fair Wear Foundation (FWF) and sources fabrics from Italian mills including Vitale Barberis Canonico and Reda. Suits are manufactured in China. HQ is in Amsterdam with offices in New York City, Dallas, and Shanghai. The company operates brick-and-mortar stores across Europe, Asia, and the Americas alongside an online store. Turnover reached €336 million in 2019; COVID-19 reduced this to €205 million in 2020, with a loss of €109.7 million. GQ Magazine named Suitsupply among the best new menswear stores in 2011.

## Company Profile

Fokke de Jong founded Suitsupply as a direct-to-consumer alternative to department store suiting, positioning Italian fabric construction at accessible price points. The first international stores opened in Antwerp and London (Vigo Street) in 2007. Fashion writer Tim Gunn cited Suitsupply's suits as among his personal essentials in Elle Decor. The company served as official supplier to the Dutch Olympic team for the Beijing 2008, Vancouver 2010, London 2012, and Rio 2016 Games. In 2012, Suitsupply collaborated with Italian shoemaker Antonio Maurizi on a capsule collection of six shoes. The brand is also known for provocative marketing campaigns in partnership with photographer Carli Hermes — including the "Shameless" campaign (images withdrawn from a London store; ruled against by the Belgian advertising standards authority) and the "Toy Boys" spring/summer 2016 campaign.

## Market Position

Suitsupply targets the affordable luxury menswear segment — positioned between mass-market suiting and high-end tailoring. The vertical integration model enables direct price control: Italian mill fabric quality at prices accessible to younger professional consumers. Olympic team outfitting (Dutch national team, 2008–2016) establishes trade credibility for formal and ceremonial suiting.

## Products and Categories

Menswear suiting (jackets, trousers, waistcoats), dress shirts, casual shirts, knitwear, outerwear, footwear, ties, and accessories. Fabric sourcing from Vitale Barberis Canonico and Reda (both premium Italian wool mills) is a documented product differentiator. Suits are constructed in China.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Suitsupply's SPD relevance is concentrated in woven textile pattern — the core design language of formal suiting:

- **Italian mill fabric sourcing**: Sourcing from Vitale Barberis Canonico and Reda places Suitsupply directly in the supply chain of premium Italian woven suiting fabrics. These mills produce chalk stripe, pinstripe, herringbone, windowpane check, Prince of Wales check, and flannel weaves — woven surface pattern design commissioned and distributed commercially through mill relationships.
- **Woven suiting patterns**: The entire Suitsupply product proposition is built on access to premium woven pattern cloths. Suiting pattern type, scale, and colourway selection differentiate product tiers within the range and drive seasonal design decisions.
- **Formal textile heritage**: The brand's positioning as accessible Italian-fabric tailoring makes woven pattern selection central to seasonal product design — engaging directly with the formal menswear pattern tradition.
- **Olympic uniform design**: Formal ceremonial outfitting for the Dutch Olympic team (four Games, 2008–2016) represents commissioned formalwear design at a national ceremonial level, with documented seasonal textile selection.

No in-house textile design or pattern development studio is documented. Pattern decisions are made through mill fabric selection rather than bespoke design commission.

## Design Direction

Suitsupply's design identity is built around the vocabulary of Italian tailoring — clean construction, premium wool fabrics, accessible formal aesthetic — rather than trend-driven fashion. No named creative director is identified in source material. The brand's most publicly discussed design signature is its provocative marketing photography (Carli Hermes), separate from product design.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Suitsupply operates as a direct-to-consumer brand through its own stores and e-commerce. The vertical integration model removes wholesale intermediaries. Fair Wear Foundation membership indicates documented labour standards in the supply chain. The company is privately held with no public licensing programme for the brand or its designs.
