# Rent the Runway

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/rent-the-runway/profile/company-logo.png)

American e-commerce platform for renting, subscribing to, and buying designer apparel and accessories from 700+ brands; founded 2009 as a fashion rental disruptor with a subscription-first business model.

## Overview

Rent the Runway is a New York City-based e-commerce company founded in November 2009 by Jennifer Hyman and Jennifer Fleiss, former Harvard Business School classmates. The platform allows customers to rent, subscribe to, or buy designer clothing and accessories, operating as a rotating digital wardrobe service. The company aggregates inventory from over 700 designer brands across sizes 00 to 22 and built its early model around event dress rental before pivoting to a subscription service for everyday clothing in 2016. Diane von Fürstenberg was among the first designer partners. The company reached a $1 billion unicorn valuation in March 2019 after raising more than $400 million in venture capital from investors including Fidelity, Franklin Templeton, TCV, Bain Capital Ventures, American Express, and T. Rowe Capital. In October 2025, Rent the Runway completed a recapitalisation reducing its outstanding debt to approximately $120 million and extending maturity to 2029.

## Company Profile

The concept originated from Jennifer Hyman observing her sister overspending on a dress for a single occasion. Hyman and Fleiss initially pitched the rental model to Diane von Fürstenberg as a service for her own brand's website; von Fürstenberg redirected them to build a multi-brand platform and became one of the first partners. Jennifer Fleiss left in 2017 to co-found Walmart's Jetblack concierge service; Hyman remained CEO. Physical retail locations in New York, Washington D.C., Chicago, San Francisco, and Los Angeles were all closed in August 2020 as the company shifted focus to digital subscriptions and drop-box logistics. As of 2018, Rent the Runway operated the largest dry cleaning operation in the United States, processing approximately 2,000 items per hour. Distribution warehouses are located in Secaucus, New Jersey and Arlington, Texas.

## Market Position

Rent the Runway pioneered the subscription fashion rental model in the United States, positioning itself as an alternative to ownership across event dressing, everyday workwear, and lifestyle categories. Subscriptions accounted for approximately 75% of revenue as of 2019. The company also introduced secondhand purchase of rented items (from 2021) and a non-membership Reserve programme for 4- or 8-day rentals. After strong early growth, 2023 brought financial difficulty — potential Chapter 11 risk reported by CreditRiskMonitor in October 2023, followed by the 2025 recapitalisation transaction led by Aranda Principal Strategies, STORY3 Capital Partners, and Nexus Capital Management.

## Products and Categories

Designer apparel and accessories available across rental subscription, on-demand rental (Reserve programme), and resale. Categories span event dressing, everyday clothing, children's clothing, ski apparel, home décor, jewellery, and handbags. Designer inventory covers 700+ brands across a wide size range (00 to 22). The home décor rental category extends the platform beyond personal fashion into interior accessories.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Rent the Runway functions as a distribution and circulation platform for designer fashion, not a producer or licensor of surface pattern design. Its relevance to the SPD ecosystem is indirect but commercially meaningful:

- **Aggregation of designer fashion at scale**: The 700+ designer partner network includes brands with significant print and pattern identities. High-volume circulation of patterned and printed garments — event dresses, workwear, casual styles — makes Rent the Runway one of the largest active distributors of designer surface pattern work in the US market.
- **Home décor rental**: The home décor rental category represents the platform's extension into interior pattern and print categories.
- **Fashion consumption model**: The subscription model creates repeat pattern exposure across many consumers — a factor relevant to understanding how designer prints circulate and achieve cultural visibility beyond direct retail.

No documented in-house design function, textile sourcing, or pattern commissioning activity.

## Design Direction

Rent the Runway has no proprietary design function. Creative direction is entirely defined by the 700+ designer partners whose inventory the platform curates. The company's operational innovation is logistical (dry cleaning at scale, inventory management, size availability) rather than design-led.

## Retail and Licensing Context

The platform operates through subscription plans, on-demand rental (Reserve), and secondhand resale — no traditional retail licensing or wholesale operation. Physical retail was abandoned in favour of drop-box and digital-first fulfilment. The company does not produce, manufacture, or license apparel. Designer partnerships are structured as inventory or revenue-sharing arrangements rather than licensing deals.
