# H&M

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](542223077_18570992872050081_1697925153790782073_n.jpg)

Swedish multinational fast fashion retailer. World's second-largest clothing company by store count. Operates H&M, COS, Arket, & Other Stories, Monki, Weekday, and Afound. Annual volume approximately 3 billion garments.

## Overview

Hennes & Mauritz AB (H&M Group) is a Swedish multinational clothing company headquartered in Stockholm, founded in 1947 by Erling Persson in Västerås. Originally a women's clothing store named Hennes, the company acquired Mauritz Widforss (a Swedish hunting and fishing retailer) in 1968, gaining a menswear range and the current name. H&M Group is the world's second-largest clothing retailer by market position, behind Inditex (Zara). As of 2025 it operates 4,101 stores across 81 countries, employs approximately 94,700 people, and generates approximately 228 billion SEK in annual revenue.

## Company Profile

Listed on the Stockholm Stock Exchange in 1974. Expanded to London 1976; entered the United States in 2000. H&M Home launched 2008 as a standalone home textiles and accessories concept. The group has built a portfolio of concept brands targeting distinct market segments: COS (2007, minimalist), Monki, Weekday, & Other Stories (2010), Arket (2017, modern heritage), and Afound (2018, off-price). The designer collaboration programme launched with Karl Lagerfeld in November 2004 and has since run annually, each resulting in a limited-edition collection for the H&M mainline.

## Market Position

Positioned as the leading value-to-mid fast fashion retailer globally, competing primarily with Inditex/Zara and Primark. The group's concept brands address distinct positioning from accessible (Monki, Weekday) through considered minimalist (COS, Arket) to premium-adjacent (& Other Stories). H&M operates 3,660 stores under the core H&M brand. The brand faced significant headwinds in key markets: Russian operations (150+ stores) were suspended March 2022 following the invasion of Ukraine; China operations saw −23% sales decline in Q2 2021 and −40% in Q3 2021 following controversy over the brand's stance on Xinjiang cotton, with a partial recovery from August 2022.

## Products and Categories

Womenswear, menswear, childrenswear, and accessories across the core H&M brand. H&M Home offers textiles (cushions, throws, bedding), curtains, rugs, kitchenware, and decorative items. COS focuses on contemporary wardrobe essentials. Arket positions as a modern European fashion brand with a café concept in some locations. Limited-edition annual designer collaborations sit at the high end of the H&M offering, produced in finite quantities.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

H&M's designer collaboration programme is its most documentable connection to surface pattern and print design. Key collaborations with direct SPD relevance include:

- **Marimekko (spring 2008)**: Partnership with Finland's leading print and pattern studio, bringing Marimekko's bold, graphic-scale floral and geometric prints to the H&M mainline.
- **Roberto Cavalli (November 2007)**: Known for animal print and baroque surface pattern, this collaboration brought Cavalli's signature print language to high-volume fast fashion.
- **Kenzo (November 2016)**: Kenzo's bold graphic prints and pattern vocabulary — tigers, florals, graphic typography — were central to the collaboration's identity.
- **Richard Allan (July 2019)**: Collaboration with the estate of Richard Allan, a 1960s British scarf print designer. Allan's original repeat prints and colourways were adapted directly for an H&M collection, making this one of the few collaborations centred entirely on archival print design.
- **Maison Martin Margiela (November 2012)**: Featured a trompe-l'oeil printed viscose jersey dress as a signature piece — a surface-print technique simulating garment layering.
- **Matthew Williamson (2009)**: Williamson's signature colourful print and pattern language applied across womenswear.
- **Sandra Mansour (August 2020)**: Lebanese designer known for floral embroidered and printed fabrications.

H&M Home operates as a significant volume buyer of home textiles — a sector heavily dependent on surface pattern and repeat design across bedding, cushions, curtains, and rugs.

COS, the group's most design-serious concept brand, explicitly cites "architecture, graphics, and design" as its aesthetic foundation. Operating 197 stores across 34 countries, COS represents a sustained, commercially significant market for restrained surface and graphic design applied to clothing.

The H&M Foundation's Global Change Award (€1 million annually) has funded innovations in textile recycling technology — including bio-fabrication and closed-loop systems — with potential implications for how printed and dyed fabrics are processed at end-of-life.

## Design Direction

The core H&M brand is trend-driven fast fashion with a broad, season-by-season aesthetic range. The concept brands maintain distinct design signatures: COS operates with a restrained, architectural sensibility; Arket draws on a modern European heritage aesthetic. The annual designer collaboration programme introduces one internationally recognised design voice per year to the H&M mainline, spanning luxury fashion, print specialists, and cultural figures.

## Retail and Licensing Context

H&M Group does not operate a licensing-out programme — it is a retailer and brand owner, not a licensor of its own intellectual property for third-party manufacture. Incoming licensing relationships exist through designer collaborations (temporary, fixed-term arrangements). The group's buying scale makes it a significant market participant in sourcing printed and patterned textiles from international mills and design studios.
