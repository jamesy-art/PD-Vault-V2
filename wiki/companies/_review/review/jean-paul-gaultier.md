# Jean Paul Gaultier

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/jean-paul-gaultier/profile/company-logo.svg)

French haute couture and prêt-à-porter fashion house; founded 1982 by Jean Paul Gaultier (born 1952); owned by [[wiki/companies/_staging/trk/puig]] (fashion and fragrance, since January 2016); known for the marinière, cone bra corset, gender-fluid tailoring, and irreverent surface design vocabulary; Gaultier retired from runway shows in January 2020; rotating guest designers each season post-retirement; fragrances: Classique (1993), Le Male (1995), Scandal (2017).

## Overview

Jean Paul Gaultier founded his eponymous fashion label in 1982, having trained as an assistant with Pierre Cardin (1970), Jacques Esterel (1971), and Jean Patou. His first individual collection was released in 1976. Often described as the *enfant terrible* of French fashion, Gaultier built his brand on irreverent subversion of dress codes — relaunching the marinière (Breton stripe top) for men in 1983, introducing women's corsets with cone bra in 1984, presenting kilts and skirts for men in 1984, and making gender fluidity a commercial fashion proposition from the early years. Hermès acquired 35% of the label in 1999 for 150 million francs; Gaultier served as creative director of Hermès from 2003 to 2010, succeeding Martin Margiela. He closed the ready-to-wear label in 2015 to concentrate on haute couture. On 17 January 2020, Gaultier announced his retirement from the runway; the brand moved to a rotating guest designer model, with Chitose Abe (Sacai) as the first. [[wiki/companies/_staging/trk/puig]] holds both the fashion and fragrance divisions since 1 January 2016 (acquiring the fragrance licence from Shiseido/BPI for $79.2M plus $22.6M early-termination fee). As of May 2020, Classique, Le Male, and Scandal lines are in production.

## Market Position

Positioned in French haute couture and high-end fragrance. At its commercial peak (late 1990s–early 2000s), Jean Paul Gaultier operated in a full-price RTW and couture market with global store presence and high-profile celebrity costuming. Closing RTW in 2015 and retiring from the runway in 2020 repositioned the brand as a couture-only house with a rotating creative model and strong fragrance revenue. [[wiki/companies/_staging/trk/puig]]'s full ownership of both fashion and fragrance gives the group cross-category brand control.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Jean Paul Gaultier's work is defined by specific surface and structural motifs that became globally referenced design codes:

- **Marinière (Breton stripe)**: Gaultier's 1983 "Boy Toy" collection relaunched the marinière — horizontal navy/white stripe on jersey — as a men's fashion statement and established it as a signature surface pattern of his brand. The marinière appears across subsequent collections for both men and women and is among the most commercially reproduced patterns associated with any single designer.
- **Cone bra and corset surface**: Introduced 1984; the external corset construction with cone/conical bust became one of the most recognisable structural surface patterns in fashion — deployed in RTW, stage costume (Madonna 1990–2023), and couture across four decades.
- **Tattoo and skin surface motifs**: Heavily tattooed models and tattoo-inspired prints appeared in collections — treating the body surface as a pattern medium and normalising decorative skin-surface motifs in high fashion.
- **Tartan and plaid**: Scottish tartans and plaid patterns appeared in Gaultier's gender-subversive knitwear and tailoring.
- **Fetish and textile surface experimentation**: Leather, PVC, lace, and industrial textile surfaces were deployed in collections from the late 1980s through the 2000s — a systematic exploration of unconventional surface materials in fashion design.
- **Fragrance bottle design**: Classique (women's torso in a corset) and Le Male (male torso in a marinière) are among fashion's most iconic fragrance bottle designs — applying the brand's surface vocabulary to three-dimensional product form.
- **Film costume design**: *The Fifth Element* (1997, dir. Luc Besson) required complete future-world costume and surface design — the white bandage costume and multi-pass costume for Milla Jovovich are widely referenced surface design artefacts in sci-fi fashion.

## Design Direction

Gaultier's visual language centres on the deconstruction of gender conventions through surface and structure: repurposing underwear-as-outerwear, applying feminine surface coding (lace, corsetry, silk) to men's bodies, and masculine surface coding (utilitarian fabrics, tailoring) to women's bodies. After retirement (2020), the rotating guest designer model means surface direction shifts seasonally based on each invited designer's practice.
