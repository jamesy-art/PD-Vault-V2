# Maison Lesage

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/maison-lesage/profile/company-logo.svg)

Parisian haute couture embroidery atelier with origins in 1858; acquired by [[wiki/companies/_staging/chanel]] via subsidiary [[wiki/companies/_review/nologo/paraffection]]; historic supplier to the major French fashion houses across the twentieth century.

## Overview

Maison Lesage is one of the oldest surviving embroidery ateliers in Paris. The Michonet workshop, established in 1858, passed to Albert and Marie-Louise Lesage in 1924, who renamed it Albert Lesage et Cie and developed both embroidered accessories and printed fabrics. François Lesage (31 March 1929 – 1 December 2011) succeeded his father after Albert's death in 1949, expanding the atelier's international reach and collaborations across five decades. ## Company Profile

Maison Lesage (formerly Albert Lesage et Cie; founded on the Michonet workshop est. 1858). Paris. Owner: [[wiki/companies/_staging/chanel]] (via [[wiki/companies/_review/nologo/paraffection]] subsidiary). Website: lesage-paris.com. Haute couture embroidery atelier and embroidery school. The atelier holds one of the most significant archives of embroidery samples in the world — sourced continuously since Albert Lesage's tenure and expanded by François Lesage. Materials used historically include Murano glass, imitation lapis lazuli, jade, turquoise, artificial stones, cabochons, crushed gelatin sequins, chenille, mink, metal blades, and fish scales.

## Market Position

Maison Lesage was for decades the preferred embroidery supplier to the leading Paris couture houses. Under François Lesage the client base extended to American, Italian, and Japanese designers. The atelier's archive became a documented reference source for a new generation of designers working with Lesage after his death; [[wiki/companies/_staging/chanel]]'s acquisition through Paraffection has secured the atelier's continuity within the haute couture supply chain.

## Products and Categories

Bespoke haute couture embroidery produced to commission for fashion houses and individual clients. Special orders have included ecclesiastical vestments (Pope John Paul II's chasuble and mitre, 1997 World Youth Day) and Académie française habits verts (Roman Polanski and Erik Orsenna 1999; Jean-Loup Dabadie 2008; Simone Veil; Dominique Bona). The Albert Lesage et Cie operation also produced embroidered accessories and printed fabrics from its early years. An embroidered accessories line was revived and sold in the Schiaparelli boutique on Place Vendôme in 1987.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Maison Lesage is among the most direct intersections of decorative surface work and haute couture in the world:

- **Embroidery as surface pattern**: Every Lesage commission translates a design brief into applied surface pattern — sequins, beads, threads, and found materials arranged as repeating or pictorial motifs across fabric ground
- **Van Gogh jackets for YSL (SS1988)**: The Irises and Sunflowers jackets required 600 hours of work each; the Irises jacket alone used 250,000 sequins in 22 colours, 200,000 beads, and 250 metres of ribbon — a documented extreme of pictorial surface pattern applied to couture
- **Schiaparelli collaborations (1936–1954)**: Embroideries inspired by circus, astrological signs, and the marine world; the "Chevaux Savants" bolero (summer 1938) with rearing embroidered horses — Surrealist influence translated directly into surface ornament
- **Archive as surface design resource**: The Lesage archive has been drawn upon by Azzedine Alaïa, John Galliano for Dior, and Karl Lagerfeld for Chanel — functioning as a living library of historical embroidery surface patterns
- **Karl Lagerfeld for Chanel (from 1983)**: Inspired by Boulle furniture and Chanel's Coromandel panels — embroidery techniques translating lacquer-inlay decorative surface language into fabric
- **Jean Paul Gaultier (winter 1998)**: Dress with panther-skin effect, embroidered with tubes in colour gradients from beige to brown — embroidery reproducing animal print surface pattern

## Design Direction

Maison Lesage does not produce its own seasonal collection. Its output is always bespoke, realised in response to a couturier's design direction. François Lesage described his philosophy: "Embroidery is to haute couture what fireworks are to Bastille Day." Aesthetic range spans pictorial (Van Gogh for YSL), Surrealist (Schiaparelli collaboration), architectural (Boulle and Coromandel reference for Chanel), and naturalistic (Gaultier panther skin).

## Retail and Licensing Context

Maison Lesage does not operate a retail or licensing model. It functions as a specialist atelier supplying to order. [[wiki/companies/_staging/chanel]]'s acquisition through [[wiki/companies/_review/nologo/paraffection]] ensures exclusivity within the Chanel supply chain while permitting continued work for third-party clients (unverified post-acquisition). The Lesage school (École Lesage) trains embroiderers in the atelier's techniques.
