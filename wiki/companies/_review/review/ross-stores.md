# Ross Stores

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/ross-stores/profile/company-logo.png)

American off-price discount retailer; operating brand name Ross Dress for Less; founded 1950 San Bruno, California; HQ Dublin, California; largest off-price retailer in the US; 1,795 stores in 43 states plus DC, Puerto Rico, and Guam as of July 2024; also operates DD's Discounts (353+ locations); Nasdaq ROST; S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 component.

## Overview

Ross Stores, Inc. is an American discount retail chain operating under the brand name Ross Dress for Less — the largest off-price retailer in the United States. Founded in 1950 by Morris "Morrie" Ross in San Bruno, California, the business was operated as a traditional department store until 1982, when a group of investors (including Mervin Morris, founder of the Mervyn's chain) acquired the six-store Bay Area chain and converted it to an off-price format. Under Stuart Moldaw and Don Rowlett, the chain expanded from 6 to 107 stores within three years. Ross became a public company via IPO on August 8, 1985 (Nasdaq: ROST) at $17.00 per share. By year-end 1995, annual sales had reached $1.4 billion across 292 stores. As of July 2024, Ross operates 1,795 stores in 43 US states, Washington DC, Puerto Rico, and Guam, and an additional 353+ DD's Discounts stores primarily in Sun Belt states. Barbara Rentler served as CEO from June 2014 to February 2025; James Conroy, formerly CEO of Boot Barn, succeeded her effective February 2, 2025.

## Market Position

Ross Stores occupies the off-price discount retail segment — the largest player in that category in the United States by store count and revenue. The off-price model depends on buying excess merchandise, cancelled orders, and liquidation stock from brands and retailers at below-market cost, then selling to consumers at significant discounts versus regular retail. This model makes Ross Stores one of the most commercially significant buyers of overstock and surplus branded fashion merchandise in the US market, including apparel, accessories, home goods, and footwear. Ross's scale ($9.7B+ revenue in 2012; 1,795 stores by 2024) and nationwide distribution position it alongside TJX Companies (TJ Maxx, Marshalls) as a defining channel of the US discount fashion retail market. DD's Discounts operates as a deeper-discount secondary banner, primarily targeting Sun Belt markets and lower-income consumers.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Ross Stores' relevance to surface pattern design is structural rather than creative — it is a commercial channel through which patterned and printed apparel, accessories, and home goods move from manufacturers and brands to consumers at below-market prices.

**Off-price as a surface pattern design market signal** — the off-price model involves buying cancelled orders, surplus stock, and overproduced inventory from manufacturers, brands, and retailers. A significant portion of what moves through off-price channels is printed and patterned apparel that has been overproduced or that failed to sell at full price. Ross Stores' purchasing decisions — what categories of printed and patterned merchandise to buy at volume — reflect commercial demand signals from the mass American consumer market. When a print direction or pattern type appears in volume at Ross, it indicates overproduction at the brand level, which in turn reflects which print trends were widely forecast and widely produced in preceding seasons.

**Home textiles** — off-price retail typically stocks substantial home goods categories including bedding (printed sheets, duvet covers, patterned quilts), towels, and decorative accessories. Patterned home textiles from name-brand and private-label manufacturers move in quantity through off-price channels. Ross's home category is a secondary market for surface-designed home textile products.

**Branded apparel prints** — as a large-scale buyer of overstock from mainstream US apparel brands (not named in the source), Ross carries branded and private-label printed apparel at discount. The print and pattern decisions in those products are made upstream by brands and their design suppliers — Ross's role is as a distribution outlet for the output of those decisions.

**Scale** — with 1,795 stores as of July 2024, Ross Stores is one of the highest-volume retail distribution points for fashion and home goods in the US market, including a large volume of patterned and printed goods across categories.

## Design Direction

Ross Stores does not employ a creative or design direction in the brand/fashion sense. The company is a retail operator — its commercial decisions are purchasing, logistics, and merchandising rather than design. Barbara Rentler, CEO from 2014 to 2025, came from a merchandising background within Ross Stores. James Conroy (CEO from February 2025) previously led Boot Barn, an outdoor and western lifestyle retailer.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Ross Stores' off-price retail model does not involve licensing design rights or brand licences in the standard fashion-industry sense. The company sources merchandise through opportunistic bulk purchasing from brands and manufacturers — surplus inventory rather than contracted design production. Ross is publicly traded (Nasdaq ROST); its stock is a component of both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100, reflecting its scale as one of America's largest retailers. The chain's geographic footprint covers 43 US states with concentration in California and the Sun Belt; there is no presence in Alaska.
