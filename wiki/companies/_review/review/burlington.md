# Burlington

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/burlington/profile/company-logo.png)

American national off-price retailer; formerly Burlington Coat Factory; 1,138+ stores in 46 states and Puerto Rico; HQ Burlington Township, New Jersey; third-largest off-price retailer after TJX and Ross Stores; founded 1972 by the Milstein family from a former factory outlet in Burlington, NJ; purchased by Bain Capital for $2.06 billion in 2006; re-IPO'd 2013 ($4.35B revenue); Monroe Gary Milstein, co-founder, died May 9, 2025.

## Overview

Burlington is an American national off-price retailer operating more than 1,138 stores across 46 states and Puerto Rico, with its corporate headquarters in Burlington Township, New Jersey. Formerly known as Burlington Coat Factory, it is the third-largest off-price retailer in the United States after TJX Companies and Ross Stores. The business originated in 1972 when Monroe Gary Milstein and his family purchased a former factory outlet in Burlington, New Jersey for $675,050 — using savings his wife Henrietta had accumulated from her work as a librarian for the majority of the $75,000 down payment. The company went public in 1983 with 31 locations, was acquired by Bain Capital Partners for $2.06 billion in 2006, and re-entered public markets in October 2013 with a 40% first-day stock gain and approximately $4.35 billion in annual sales.

## Company Profile

The Milstein family's outerwear wholesale and retail background (dating to 1946) defined the company's founding strategy: using the former factory outlet to sell coats and jackets in bulk at discount prices. To reduce seasonal dependence on the coat business, the company gradually added apparel categories, accessories, linens, gift items, a baby department, and shoes. By 1983, with 31 locations, Burlington Coat Factory Warehouse Corporation went public. A second location had opened in 1975 in Copiague, Long Island. After Bain Capital's 2006 acquisition for $2.06 billion, Tom Kingsbury served as CEO from 2008 to 2018. Michael O'Sullivan joined as CEO in September 2019, brought from Ross Stores where he had spent 16 years and risen to President and Chief Operating Officer. In 2013, the company operated 503 stores across 44 states and Puerto Rico under the Burlington Coat Factory, Cohoes Fashions, Baby Depot, and MJM Designer Shoes names. By 2016, Burlington had entered the Fortune 500 for the first time. In 2023 and 2024, Burlington expanded its store network through opportunistic lease acquisitions: 40+ former Bed Bath & Beyond locations (June 2023) and 15 former Conn's locations (2024). Monroe Gary Milstein died on May 9, 2025, at age 98.

## Market Position

Burlington occupies the off-price segment of American fashion retail — the third-largest in the category after TJX Companies (TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods) and Ross Stores. Off-price retail operates by purchasing branded merchandise as overstock, end-of-season, closeout, or cancelled-order inventory at significant discounts from the original wholesale price, then reselling to consumers at 20–60% below traditional retail prices. The model depends on opportunistic buying from a broad base of brand suppliers across apparel, accessories, and home categories. Burlington's historic coat and outerwear identity distinguished it within the off-price space even as its product range expanded substantially beyond coats.

## Products and Categories

Coats and outerwear (founding product category), women's and men's apparel, children's clothing (including Baby Depot format), shoes (including MJM Designer Shoes format), fashion accessories, linens and home textiles, gift items, and accessories. The Cohoes Fashions format operated as a luxury off-price division. The company's product assortment at any given time reflects the opportunistic nature of off-price buying — it is composed of branded and private label product purchased from across the fashion supply chain.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Burlington's surface pattern design relevance is structural rather than creative — it functions as one of the largest secondary distribution channels for designed fashion merchandise in the United States:

- **Off-price distribution of branded fashion**: As the third-largest US off-price retailer, Burlington annually moves billions of dollars of apparel, accessories, and home goods that originated from brands with active in-house or licensed design functions. This merchandise includes a significant volume of print and pattern-designed product — seasonal prints, graphic tees, patterned knitwear, printed home textiles, and designed outerwear — that has been marked down from primary retail channels. Burlington's scale (1,138+ stores) makes it a significant endpoint for design-originated commercial fashion product.
- **Linens and home textiles category**: Burlington's early expansion from coats into linens represents a direct entry into surface-pattern-designed product — bedding, towels, curtains, and home textile accessories are categories defined almost entirely by print, weave pattern, and colour. Burlington's historic and current home textiles assortment involves procurement of SPD-originated product at scale.
- **Outerwear as fabric surface design**: Coats and outerwear — the brand's foundational product — are categories in which fabric surface (tweed weave, check, plaid, wool pattern, printed lining) are primary quality and visual signals. The off-price model for outerwear involves buying end-of-season or overrun designer coats, which carry the original brand's fabric and pattern design decisions into Burlington's retail environment.
- **Burlington Industries trademark disclaimer (1981–2009)**: The company operated for 28 years under a legal obligation to carry the disclaimer "Not Affiliated with Burlington Industries" in all advertising — a consequence of a 1981 trademark settlement with Burlington Industries, the fabric manufacturer. Burlington Industries was a major US textile manufacturer and brand name in fabric; the name confusion between the two companies highlights the proximity of "Burlington" as a commercial name to the fabric and textile trade. The disclaimer was only retired in 2009, five years after Burlington Industries ceased operations in 2004 following buyout.
- **Faux fur controversy (2006)**: Burlington was found to have sold real animal fur products mislabelled as "faux fur" following a Humane Society undercover investigation — including coats containing hair from raccoon dogs. The company pulled the false advertisements after the investigation. This documents Burlington's role in the fur and outerwear materials supply chain and the challenge of surface material provenance verification at off-price retail scale.

## Design Direction

Burlington has no proprietary design direction. As an off-price retailer, the brand does not design product — it buys existing designed merchandise from brand suppliers, licensors, and manufacturers at discount. The buyer function (determining which brands and products to acquire for each selling season) is the commercial equivalent of curation in this model.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Burlington operates 1,138+ off-price stores under the Burlington and The Burlington names, formerly also operating under Burlington Coat Factory, Cohoes Fashions, Baby Depot, and MJM Designer Shoes. The business model is purely retail — no licensing of the Burlington name to manufacturers is documented, and no proprietary product design takes place. The company expanded its physical footprint in 2023–2024 by acquiring leases from bankrupt retailers (Bed Bath & Beyond, Conn's). The Bain Capital ownership period (2006–2013 IPO) restructured the company under a holding entity (Burlington Coat Factory Holdings Inc.) before the 2013 re-IPO.
