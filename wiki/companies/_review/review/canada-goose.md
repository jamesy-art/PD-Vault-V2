# Canada Goose

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/canada-goose/profile/company-logo.png)

Canadian luxury outerwear brand founded in 1957; dual-listed (NYSE/TSX, ticker GOOS); known for the Arctic Program badge logo and a strict "Made in Canada" manufacturing commitment; Haider Ackermann appointed first creative director in May 2024.

## Overview

Canada Goose Inc. is a Canadian luxury outerwear company founded in 1957 in Toronto as Metro Sportswear Ltd by Polish-Jewish immigrant Sam Tick, whose first products were wool vests, raincoats, and snowsuits. Tick's son-in-law David Reiss joined in 1972 and introduced down-filling technology; Dani Reiss (David's son, third generation) became CEO in 2001 and repositioned the brand from a B2B workwear supplier toward premium consumer outerwear. The name changed from Snow Goose to Canada Goose in 2000. Bain Capital acquired a 70% stake in December 2013 at a $250M valuation. The company IPO'd on the NYSE and TSX (ticker GOOS) on March 16, 2017, raising approximately $255M. Revenue grew from $3M in 1991 to $291M in 2016. Manufacturing remains entirely Canadian across eight production facilities. In May 2024, Colombia-born Belgian designer Haider Ackermann was appointed Canada Goose's first-ever creative director.

- **CEO:** Dani Reiss (since 2001; third generation)

## Market Position

Canada Goose sits in the premium-to-luxury outerwear segment, with a $900 parka price point as its flagship product. The brand achieved luxury status through product placement in film (*The Day After Tomorrow*), celebrity adoption, and the premium positioning of its "Made in Canada" badge. Dani Reiss discontinued private-label operations (previously supplying L.L. Bean, Eddie Bauer, and public sector clients under Snow Goose) to focus on the consumer brand. The appointment of Haider Ackermann as creative director in May 2024 signals an intentional shift toward runway-level fashion positioning — analogous to the strategy that elevated Moncler. Counterfeiting is an active challenge: hologram trademarks were introduced in 2011, a verification website was launched, and a successful lawsuit against counterfeiters in Sweden in October 2012 resulted in prison sentences and CAD$105,000 in damages.

## Products and Categories

- **Parkas and outerwear** — flagship product; worn by US Antarctic Program researchers and Iditarod dogsledders; Arctic-grade performance positioning
- **Knitwear** — sweaters and knit accessories
- **Hats and gloves** — cold-weather accessories
- **Footwear** — launched November 2021 (first-ever footwear collection); Baffin bootmaker acquired November 2018
- **Project Atigi** — limited collection produced by Inuit seamstresses across Canada (from 2019); proceeds to Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami
- **Generations** — resale and trade-in platform (launched 2023)

The distinctive Arctic Program badge — resembling an arctic map of the North Pole encircled by "CANADA GOOSE" and "ARCTIC PROGRAM" text — is usually placed on the upper arm and is the brand's primary visual trademark. No brand licensing or outsourcing is permitted; all products carry "Made in Canada" branding.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Canada Goose's surface design relevance is concentrated in brand graphic identity and outerwear surface treatment rather than repeat textile pattern design:

**Arctic Program badge** — the circular arm badge is the brand's most recognisable graphic element. It functions as a placed graphic motif on the outerwear surface — a branded patch design with strong geographic and typographic design content. Its Arctic map imagery is distinctive enough that counterfeiters have specifically targeted it (the 2012 International Clothiers lawsuit centred on replication of the badge's placement and design).

**Outerwear surface treatment** — performance parkas involve surface-relevant design decisions: water-resistant nylon shell treatments, quilted down fill patterns, ribbed knit details, and material finish choices (matte, gloss, embossed, etc.) across seasonal collections.

**Project Atigi** — this programme commissions Inuit seamstresses to design jackets, introducing Indigenous craft and design aesthetics into Canada Goose product. This is the most directly surface-pattern-relevant design programme documented in the source: Inuit textile and garment design traditions involve distinctive decorative and functional surface treatments.

**Haider Ackermann creative director (from May 2024)** — Ackermann's background in high-fashion tailoring and colour-led ready-to-wear design implies a more intentional surface and pattern direction for future collections than the brand's functional outerwear origins would suggest.

## Design Direction

Until the appointment of Haider Ackermann in May 2024, Canada Goose's design was driven primarily by performance and function — parkas optimised for extreme cold, with the Arctic Program badge as the core brand design element. The product placement strategy (film, sports, celebrity) amplified the existing design without requiring fashion-led creative direction. Ackermann's appointment represents a deliberate turn toward fashion credibility — consistent with the luxury outerwear market trajectory that Moncler pioneered. No specific collection creative direction has been documented in the source post-appointment.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Canada Goose does not license its brand to other manufacturers and does not outsource production — all manufacturing is in Canada ("Made in Canada" is a brand commitment that was formalised in the Bain Capital acquisition terms). Retail operates through company-owned stores (first store: Yorkdale Shopping Centre, Toronto, late 2016; second US flagship: Magnificent Mile, Chicago, October 2017; Beijing, December 2018), wholesale accounts, and e-commerce. The Generations platform (from 2023) adds a resale channel.
