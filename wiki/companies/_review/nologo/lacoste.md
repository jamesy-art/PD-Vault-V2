# Lacoste

French sportswear and lifestyle brand founded in 1933 by tennis champion René Lacoste and knitwear manufacturer André Gillier; creator of the first branded polo shirt and the first instance of a brand logo appearing on the exterior of a sports garment; owned 100% by Swiss holding group Maus Frères since November 2012; revenue nearing €3 billion.

## Overview

Lacoste was founded in 1933 by René Lacoste — nicknamed "The Crocodile" by a US journalist in 1923 after a leather crocodile-skin bag wager — and André Gillier, head of France's largest knitwear manufacturer. The brand's founding product, "La Chemise Lacoste," was a short-sleeved polo shirt bearing an embroidered crocodile on the chest, which René Lacoste wore for tennis competitions. This was the first time a brand logo appeared on the exterior of a sports garment — a foundational moment in fashion brand identity and surface design. From a single sportswear product, Lacoste expanded across clothing, footwear, fragrance, eyewear, leather goods, watches, and children's wear, distributed in over 110 countries. Maus Frères (Swiss holding group) completed acquisition of 100% of Lacoste in November 2012 for approximately €1 billion. Revenue reached near €3 billion by January 2025. ## Market Position

Lacoste operates at the accessible premium tier — below full luxury (LVMH/Kering) but above mass fashion — with a 70%+ own retail weighting under CEO Thierry Guibert's strategic shift (from the former inverse 30% own/70% wholesale split). US wholesale: Saks, Nordstrom, Neiman Marcus, Lord & Taylor, Bloomingdale's, Macy's, Belk. UK: JD Sports, John Lewis. Canada: Harry Rosen, Hudson's Bay. Australia: David Jones, Myer. ## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

- **Crocodile logomark as surface design icon**: The embroidered crocodile on the chest of the polo shirt is one of the most widely recognised brand marks in fashion — a single motif applied consistently since 1933, establishing the embroidered chest logo as a foundational genre of sportswear surface design
- **Polo shirt colour as design language**: Lacoste produces the L.12.12 polo in an extensive seasonal solid-colour palette; colourway selection and seasonal colour direction represent the primary surface design decision for the brand's core product
- **Creative direction and print extension**: Creative directors have progressively expanded Lacoste's seasonal collections beyond the core polo into print and graphic pattern work — particularly under Louise Trotter and Pelagia Kolotouros — introducing contemporary surface pattern alongside the heritage crocodile motif
- **Licensing surface translation**: Fragrance bottles, eyewear frames, watch faces, and leather goods carry Lacoste visual codes (crocodile, stripe elements, colour palettes) across diverse product surfaces
- **Supreme x Lacoste**: Three-year collaboration (2017–2019) blending Lacoste's embroidered heritage with Supreme's graphic-heavy streetwear aesthetic — a documented case of cross-market surface design exchange
- **Piqué fabric as surface identity**: The piqué cotton weave of the L.12.12 shirt is a surface design decision in itself — a specific textile construction that has become synonymous with the brand's physical identity

## Design Direction

Core identity: minimalist sportswear anchored to the embroidered crocodile and the piqué polo. Seasonal colour palette across the polo range is the primary design driver. Under Pelagia Kolotouros (from 2023), expanded seasonal runway offerings incorporate pattern and graphics. Return to Paris Fashion Week (2024) signals increased investment in seasonal design storytelling beyond sportswear heritage. The August 2025 GOAT logo limited edition (US Open) demonstrates willingness to introduce new logomark variants for event-specific collections.
