# Paraffection

[[raw/companies processed/Chanel]] subsidiary established 1985 to acquire, preserve, and promote heritage French fashion artisan workshops (Ateliers d'Art); twelve ateliers as of 2015 spanning embroidery, millinery, feathers, buttons, shoemaking, fabric flowers, goldsmithing, knitwear, glovemaking, and pleating; name translates as "for the love of"; director from 2009: Dominique Barbiery.

## Overview

Paraffection S.A. is a subsidiary of [[raw/companies processed/Chanel]], established in 1985 when Chanel acquired Desrues, the ornamental button and costume jewellery atelier (founded 1887). The subsidiary later acquired Michel (millinery, 1996) and Lemarié (feathers, 1996). The named entity Paraffection launched in 2002 with the simultaneous acquisition of embroidery house [[Maison Lesage]] (established 1868) and shoemaker Massaro (established 1947). As of 2015, Paraffection holds twelve Ateliers d'Art. Dominique Barbiery was appointed Director in 2009. The specialist workshops remain legally independent and are free to supply and collaborate with other fashion houses. [[raw/companies processed/Chanel]]'s Métiers d'Arts runway collections — staged to showcase the ateliers' craft — are the primary public-facing platform for Paraffection's portfolio. ## Market Position

Paraffection operates as a craft-preservation holding structure within Chanel, not as a commercial fashion brand. Its position is unique within the luxury industry: the ateliers it holds are primary suppliers of handcrafted embellishment, embroidery, millinery, and material craft to Parisian haute couture. [[raw/companies processed/Chanel]] is the primary client, but the ateliers remain legally independent and free to supply other houses — preserving their role as craft suppliers to the wider haute couture industry without monopolising access. The Métiers d'Arts collections act as the seasonal showcase for the ateliers' combined output.

## Products and Categories

Twelve Ateliers d'Art held by Paraffection as of 2015 (in order of acquisition):

1. **Desrues** — ornamentation and buttons (established 1887; acquired by Chanel 1985)
2. **Michel** — milliner (established 1936; acquired by Paraffection 1996)
3. **Lemarié** — feathers and camellias (established 1880; acquired 1996)
4. **[[Maison Lesage]]** — embroidery (established 1868; acquired 2002; most publicly prominent Paraffection atelier; own wiki page)
5. **Massaro** — shoemaker (established 1947; acquired 2002)
6. **Goossens** — gold and silversmith (established 1950s; acquired 2005)
7. **Guillet** — maker of fabric flowers (established 1869; acquired 2006)
8. **Montex** — embroidery (established 1939; acquired 2011)
9. **Causse** — glovemaker (established 1892; acquired 2012)
10. **Barrie** — Scottish knitwear manufacturer (established 1870s; acquired 2012)
11. **Les Ateliers Lognon** — pleater (established 1945; acquired 2013)
12. **Lanel** — embroidery (established 1949; acquired by Lesage 2013)

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Paraffection is directly and centrally relevant to surface pattern design at the haute couture level — the majority of its ateliers produce textile surface embellishment and craft that constitutes the applied surface design layer of couture garments:

- **Embroidery (Lesage, Montex, Lanel)**: Three of the twelve ateliers are dedicated embroidery houses. [[Maison Lesage]] is the most historically significant — holding one of the world's largest archives of embroidery samples and producing embroidery for Chanel, YSL, Balenciaga, Givenchy, Dior, and others. Montex (est. 1939) and Lanel (est. 1949, now held by Lesage) extend the embroidery capacity. Embroidery design — the positioning, repeat, motif, colour, and material of stitched pattern on textile — is a direct surface pattern design practice
- **Pleating (Les Ateliers Lognon)**: Pleating is a textile surface structure technique — sunray, accordion, knife, and box pleating patterns transform flat textile surfaces into dimensional surface geometry; Lognon (est. 1945) is a primary pleating atelier for Parisian haute couture
- **Feathers and camellias (Lemarié)**: Feather embellishment and camellia florals applied to garments and accessories — the arrangement, scale, and placement of feather and fabric flower on a surface is a three-dimensional surface embellishment practice. The camellia is an iconic Chanel surface motif executed by Lemarié
- **Fabric flowers (Guillet)**: Guillet (est. 1869) produces fabric flowers used as garment and accessory surface ornamentation; one of the oldest French fabric flower ateliers
- **Ornamentation and buttons (Desrues)**: Custom button and decorative trim design — button surface, material, and arrangement on a garment is a surface design element; Desrues produces the ornamental buttons central to Chanel's surface language
- **Knitwear (Barrie)**: The Barrie atelier (est. 1870s, Scotland) holds expertise in luxury knitted textiles; knitwear stitch patterns and surface constructions — fairisle, intarsia, jacquard knit, cable — are surface pattern design categories applied through knitted structure
- **Gloves (Causse)**: Glovemaking involves surface decisions at the level of material, stitch, and applied decoration (est. 1892)
- **Millinery (Michel)**: Hat surface and form design; surface treatment of hat materials
- **Goldsmithing (Goossens)**: Jewellery and decorative metalwork applied to couture garments and accessories as surface ornamentation
- **Shoemaking (Massaro)**: Luxury footwear surface — material, colour, and finish of the shoe upper

## Design Direction

Paraffection has no independent commercial design direction — it is a preservation and craft-capacity structure. Each atelier executes briefs from client houses, with [[raw/companies processed/Chanel]] as primary client. The Métiers d'Arts runway collections are the primary creative platform for the ateliers' output; each collection showcases embroidery, feather, flower, pleating, knitwear, millinery, and ornamentation craft applied to Chanel's seasonal design vision. The ateliers' independence means they also respond to the creative briefs of other haute couture houses.
