# Go Sport

French sporting goods retail chain, founded 1979 in Grenoble; acquired by Intersport in April 2023 following judicial administration; substantially rebranded as Intersport stores from 2024.

## Overview

Go Sport is a French sporting goods retailer founded in 1979 by brothers Léon and Lucien Odier in Sassenage, near Grenoble. The name "Go" stands for "Grenoble Olympique." At its peak, the Go Sport group operated over 181 stores in France and approximately 40 international franchise stores. In 2016, Go Sport held an 8% share of the French sporting goods market, ranking third behind Decathlon (30%) and Intersport (18%). The company entered judicial administration in January 2023 and was acquired by Intersport in partnership with Qatari group Al-Mana in April 2023 for €35 million. From 2024, approximately 50 of the remaining 72 French stores were rebranded as Intersport; the brand is effectively in terminal decline as a distinct retail identity. As of January 2026, only two stores operate under the Go Sport name in France, alongside a pilot Go Sport Outdoor store in Chambéry.

## Company Profile

Go Sport was created as a stand-alone brand in 1979. The Rallye Group (Casino Group parent) acquired Genty-Cathiard (which then owned Go Sport) in 1990, making Go Sport a Rallye subsidiary. In 2000, Rallye created the Go Sport Group to consolidate its sports retail brands. Sub-brands included Courir (sold to Equistone Partners for €283M in 2018), Go Sport Montagne (mountain and outdoor, rebranded from Twinner in 2016), Bike+ (cycling retail, added 2016), and Endurance Shop (running specialist). In 2021, Go Sport was sold to Hermione People & Brands (HPB), a subsidiary of Financière Immobilière Bordelaise (FIB), for a symbolic one euro. The company had significant debt and was under investigation for misuse of company assets from November 2022. The brand briefly and controversially acquired Gap France at HPB's direction in December 2022 — Gap France subsequently filed for bankruptcy (February 2023) and was acquired by Spodis/JD Sports.

## Market Position

At peak, Go Sport was France's third-largest sporting goods retailer. The company operated in a competitive three-way French market dominated by Decathlon (vertically integrated, own-brand) and Intersport (cooperative buying group). Go Sport's position — selling third-party branded goods without Decathlon's cost advantage or Intersport's cooperative model — was structurally difficult. International franchise operations covered Kuwait, Qatar, UAE, Oman, Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia, Belgium, Romania, and India. Polish operations (Go Sport Polska) were sold to Sportmaster (Russia) in 2019 and subsequently declared bankrupt in May 2022.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Sporting goods retail is a commercially significant channel for surface pattern design in sportswear, athletic apparel, and outdoor clothing. Sports apparel is among the most pattern-intensive categories in fashion: team graphics, performance print colourways, technical surface treatments (reflective, DWR-coated, mesh structures), and brand identity graphics applied to clothing and equipment are core product features. Go Sport, as a multi-brand sports retailer, stocked and distributed apparel from major sporting brands whose design language is built on surface pattern, print, and graphic application. The outdoor and mountain sub-brand (Go Sport Montagne/Twinner) stocked technical apparel where camouflage patterns, tonal colour-blocking, and functional surface treatments are standard. As a retail channel, Go Sport provided French market distribution for pattern-led sportswear brands.

## Design Direction

Go Sport operated as a retailer of third-party branded products rather than a design-originating entity. Own-brand product design direction is not documented in the available source. The brand's retail identity (store design, visual merchandising) was distinct from Decathlon's own-brand-first model.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Go Sport operated through owned stores in France and international franchise partnerships (Middle East, North Africa, India, Belgium, Romania). The franchise model allowed international reach without capital investment. Polish stores were operated as a direct subsidiary (Go Sport Polska), later sold and subsequently bankrupt. Following the Intersport acquisition (April 2023), international Go Sport stores outside France were acquired by Qatari group Al-Mana under an agreement with the Intersport group. The remaining French stores are transitioning to the Intersport banner or a Go Sport Outdoor specialty format.
