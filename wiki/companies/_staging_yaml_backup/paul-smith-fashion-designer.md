# Paul Smith (fashion designer)

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/companies/paul-smith-fashion-designer/profile/company-logo.jpg)

British luxury fashion brand founded in Nottingham in 1970, internationally recognised for its multicoloured stripe and "classic with a twist" design philosophy applied across clothing, accessories, and licensed product categories.

## Overview

Paul Smith is the eponymous brand of Sir Paul Brierley Smith (born 1946, Beeston, Nottinghamshire), knighted in 2000 and appointed to the Order of the Companions of Honour in 2020, both for services to fashion. The brand operates 130 stores in more than 60 countries, including major presence in Japan, the US, and across Europe. Japanese trading company Itochu acquired a 40% stake in 2006; Smith retains majority control. The brand is defined by the multicoloured stripe — applied across tailoring, shirts, accessories, footwear, vehicles, sporting equipment, and technology products — making it one of the most pattern-explicit luxury brands in British fashion.

## Company Profile

Smith's first shop opened in Nottingham in 1970 at 6 Byard Lane (twelve by twelve feet, open Fridays and Saturdays only). His first Paris menswear collection was shown in 1976. The Covent Garden flagship on Floral Street, London opened in 1979. International expansion followed through the 1980s: Tokyo (1984), New York Fifth Avenue (1987). Womenswear (Paul Smith Women) launched in 1993 after women had been buying smaller menswear sizes. Paul Smith Junior (childrenswear) began in 1990, becoming a seasonal line from 2010. The brand acquired workwear label R. Newbold (founded 1885) in 1993. E-commerce launched in 2004. From January 2016, Paul Smith has combined men's and women's collections at Paris Fashion Week.

## Market Position

Paul Smith occupies accessible luxury, positioned between mid-market British brands and international designer names. The brand's strength in Japan — where it opened its first international store in 1984 — is notable; Japan has been a consistently significant market. The brand competes with contemporaries such as Kenzo and Marni in accessible designer, and with British labels in the men's tailoring sector. Its distribution spans own-store retail, department store concessions, and e-commerce. Stores operate in New Delhi, Seoul, San Francisco, Paris, Amsterdam, Milan, Copenhagen, eight London locations, and three Heathrow Airport airside units.

## Products and Categories

Core menswear, womenswear, childrenswear, accessories (shoes, bags, eyewear), and a range of licensed products. The brand has applied its stripe identity to an extensive range of licensed and collaborative product categories:

- **Automotive**: Mini (1997 limited edition of 1,800; 2021 Mini Electric); Land Rover Defender (bespoke); Triumph Bonneville T100 (2005); 1965 Porsche 911 covered in multicoloured stripes (2018, Le Mans Classic and Goodwood Festival of Speed)
- **Sports equipment**: Burton Snowboards (2010, boards and apparel); Kask cycling helmets (2015, 2018); New Balance 576 sneakers and football boots (2018); Tour de France jersey (2007); Giro d'Italia Maglia Rosa jersey (2013); Manchester United suits (2009)
- **Interiors and furniture**: Cappellini homeware (2002); Anglepoise Type 75 lamp (2014–2018 editions); Gufram cactus coat stand (2016); Broadway Cinema seating, Nottingham
- **Stationery and accessories**: Caran d'Ache 849 pen (two editions, 2015–2016); Globe-Trotter 20" trolley case (2018); Cutler & Gross eyewear (2018); Leica X2 camera limited edition (2012); Lasonic boombox redesign (2005)
- **Textiles**: Maharam upholstery and surface materials (ongoing from 2003); Rapha cycle clothing (from 2007)
- **Food and publishing**: Berry Bros & Rudd wine collection (2016); Evian limited edition bottles (2009); Penguin Classics *Lady Chatterley's Lover* cover (2005); Isle of Man Olympic stamps (2012)

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

The multicoloured stripe is the brand's primary visual identifier and one of the most consistently applied signature patterns in British fashion. Its SPD relevance is direct:

- The stripe has been applied across all product categories at every price point since the 1970s — making Paul Smith a case study in a single pattern motif sustaining a global brand identity.
- Textile collaboration with Maharam (from 2003, celebrated their 15-year anniversary) demonstrates formal licensing of the Paul Smith pattern identity into interior textile and surface material markets.
- The Rapha cycle clothing partnership applied the stripe to performance apparel in a technically demanding product category.
- The Burton Snowboards collaboration (2010) brought the signature stripe into hard goods and extreme sports equipment.
- Across automotive and industrial design collaborations — Mini, Porsche, Land Rover, Triumph — the stripe functions as a surface pattern applied to three-dimensional objects, demonstrating the flexibility of a repeat linear motif across scales and substrates.
- Menswear collections feature tactile textile work — merino wool, pinstripes, windowpane checks — alongside the signature stripe, with Savile Row tailoring tradition as a foundation.

## Design Direction

Smith's stated philosophy is "classic with a twist" — established garment forms (tailored suits, shirts, jackets) combined with unexpected colour, print, or fabrication. His background includes tailoring training and work as a buyer for Browns London; his wife Pauline trained as a fashion designer at the Royal College of Art and contributed to early collections. Cycling, graphic art, and popular culture are documented personal influences. The stripe is the recurring motif across all scales and product categories; pinstripes and windowpane checks appear regularly in tailoring.

## Retail and Licensing Context

The brand operates its own retail stores globally, department store concessions, and an e-commerce platform (since 2004). The 40% Itochu stake (acquired 2006) underpins significant Japanese distribution infrastructure. Paul Smith actively licenses its stripe pattern and brand identity to adjacent product categories — textiles (Maharam), cycling apparel (Rapha), snowboards, automotive, stationery, cameras — creating an extended brand presence across consumer product and surface design markets that is unusually broad for a fashion house of its scale.
