# Kering

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/kering/profile/company-logo.svg)

French multinational luxury goods holding company; headquartered in Paris; parent of Gucci, Yves Saint Laurent, Balenciaga, Bottega Veneta, Alexander McQueen, Brioni, and others; €14.67 billion revenue (2025); CAC 40 constituent.

## Overview

Kering S.A. is a French multinational luxury holding company headquartered in Paris, in the former Hôpital Laennec in the 7th arrondissement. Founded as a timber-trading company (Établissements Pinault) by François Pinault in Brittany in 1962, the group transformed through retail acquisitions into Pinault-Printemps-Redoute (PPR) by 1994, then pivoted to luxury from 1999 onward before rebranding as Kering in March 2013. The group is a constituent of the CAC 40 (since 1995) and is listed on Euronext Paris. Chairman is François-Henri Pinault (since 2005, son of founder); CEO is Luca de Meo (since September 2025), previously CEO of Renault. In 2025, Kering generated €14.67 billion in revenue, employed 43,731 people, and managed 1,719 stores. The parent holding company is Groupe Artémis (Pinault family).

## Company Profile

Kering's transformation into a luxury group began in 1999 when Pinault-Printemps-Redoute acquired a controlling 42% stake in the Gucci group for $3 billion and 100% of Yves Saint Laurent. Through the Gucci group, PPR acquired Bottega Veneta and Balenciaga (2001), signed partnerships with Alexander McQueen and Stella McCartney (2001), and added Boucheron (2000). Further luxury acquisitions followed: Brioni (Italian tailor, 2011); Pomellato and Dodo jewellery, Qeelin (2012); Ginori 1735 (formerly Richard Ginori porcelain, 2013); Valentino 30% stake (2023). The group also built Kering Eyewear (in-house eyewear production arm, 2014; €1.5B revenue 2023; brands include Lindberg acquired 2021 and Maui Jim acquired 2022) and launched Kering Beauté in 2023 to manage fragrance and cosmetics in-house — before selling the beauty division (including Creed, acquired €3.5B in 2023) to L'Oréal for ~$4 billion in October 2025. Former holdings include Puma (acquired 2007, spun off 2018), Volcom (2011, sold), Cobra Golf (2010, sold), Stella McCartney (divested 2018), Christopher Kane (divested 2019), Girard-Perregaux and Ulysse Nardin watches (sold 2022). A minority stake in Chinese-French fashion group ICCF Group (including Icicle and Carven) was taken in 2026.

## Market Position

Kering is one of the three largest luxury conglomerates globally, alongside LVMH and Richemont, though significantly smaller than LVMH. The group's revenue has declined from a peak of €19.57 billion (2023) to €14.67 billion (2025), primarily due to the slowdown of Gucci — which at peak generated €10 billion in annual sales (2022) and has since declined. New CEO Luca de Meo's strategic priority is reducing the group's financial dependency on Gucci by strengthening other brands. The group operates across all core luxury categories: fashion, leather goods, jewellery, eyewear, and (formerly) fragrance, watches, and beauty.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Kering's brand portfolio contains some of the most influential pattern identities in global luxury.

**Gucci** is the most commercially significant pattern-relevant brand in the portfolio. The GG monogram (interlocking double-G logo repeat) is one of the most widely recognised brand surface patterns globally, applied to leather goods, clothing, accessories, and textiles. Gucci's floral patterns — from the Tom Ford era through to Alessandro Michele's maximalist florals and his successors' directions — are among the most influential print directions in luxury fashion. Gucci's Flora print (1966, originally designed for Grace Kelly) is a canonical luxury floral pattern.

**Balenciaga** operates with a graphic identity-led surface language — large logotype applications, deconstructed sportswear graphics, and print-heavy runway statements that filter into the broader fashion surface design market.

**Alexander McQueen** produces among the most pattern-intensive ready-to-wear in luxury: skull prints, floral skull combinations, tartan/heritage textile references, and intricate repeat patterns applied to silk, chiffon, and woven fabrics are central to the brand's aesthetic identity.

**Bottega Veneta**'s intrecciato weave — a hand-woven leather basket-weave surface structure — represents a surface pattern expressed through material construction rather than print: one of luxury's most distinctive textile surface identities.

**Ginori 1735** (porcelain) applies decorative surface pattern to functional objects: botanical, classical, and hand-painted patterns on ceramics are a historical craft within the brand and directly relevant to the decorative arts dimension of surface pattern design.

**Kering Eyewear** applies brand graphics and surface treatments to optical and sunglasses frames across the group's portfolio — a product surface design activity at significant scale.

## Design Direction

Each Kering brand operates with its own creative director; there is no group-level creative director. François-Henri Pinault has been the strategic architect of Kering's luxury direction since 2005. CEO Luca de Meo (from September 2025) brings an automotive industry management background and a mandate to diversify beyond Gucci. The group historically allowed creative directors significant autonomy: Tom Ford at Gucci (until 2004), Demna Gvasalia at Balenciaga, Sarah Burton's extended tenure at Alexander McQueen, Daniel Lee (Bottega Veneta, later YSL), and various creative cycles at YSL. No individual brand creative directors are named in the source for the current period.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Kering manages 1,719 stores globally as of 2025, operated primarily through its brand subsidiaries. The group's model is predominantly direct retail (owned flagship stores and selective wholesale) rather than the department store wholesale model used by some competitors. Kering Eyewear operates as a production and distribution arm for group eyewear brands. The fragrance and beauty operations (Kering Beauté, launched 2023, including Creed) have been sold to L'Oréal (~$4B, October 2025). Kering's *Women in Motion* programme, running in partnership with the Cannes Film Festival since 2015 and extended to the Rencontres d'Arles photography festival in 2019, represents a brand-building cultural initiative relevant to the intersection of fashion and visual arts.
