British premium menswear brand; founded 1983 by Jeremy Hackett and Ashley Lloyd-Jennings; 160 stores globally (2019); flagship 14 Savile Row, London; [[Pepe Jeans]] Group subsidiary; known for classic British tailoring, heritage accessories, and sports partnership livery.

## Overview

Hackett London was founded in 1983 by Jeremy Hackett and Ashley Lloyd-Jennings, who began by sourcing and selling vintage British menswear from a Portobello Road stall. The first Hackett shop opened at the "wrong end" of the King's Road, Chelsea, initially trading in vintage pieces before transitioning to original design. The brand developed around classic British menswear codes: tailoring, polo shirts, blazers, and accessories rooted in the English country and sporting tradition. International expansion followed — Spain (1989), Paris (1994), a children's line (1995). Michael Sondag, formerly of Tommy Hilfiger, joined as creative director in 2005 following Richemont's sale of the brand to Torreal S.C.R. In February 2015, [[Pepe Jeans]] Group — owned by M1 Group (Lebanon) and L Capital Asia (an LVMH subsidiary) — acquired Hackett. By 2019, the brand operated approximately 160 stores globally. ## Company Profile

Flagship store: 14 Savile Row, London — the former Hardy Amies premises (Hardy Amies occupied the site from 1946 until March 2019; Hackett took over June 2019). Sub-brands: Hackett London (mainline), Hackett Heritage (classic tailoring), Hackett Sport. Retail mix: multi-channel, including department store concessions. Website: hackett.com. Instagram: @hackettlondon. Facebook: HackettLondon. Pinterest: hackettlondon. From 2015, official clothing supplier to the Williams Martini Racing Formula 1 team.

## Market Position

Premium British menswear. Positioned between accessible luxury and mainstream premium — mid-to-upper price point, tailoring-led, with a Savile Row heritage address. Competes in the British heritage menswear segment alongside brands such as Dunhill and Barbour. [[Pepe Jeans]] Group ownership gives the brand distribution infrastructure across Europe and Asia. International footprint weighted toward Spain and other European markets.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Hackett's product vocabulary is grounded in classic British textile surface design:

- **Checks**: Classic British check patterns — houndstooth, glen check, windowpane, Prince of Wales check — are standard across tailoring and casualwear lines.
- **Stripes**: Club stripe, banker stripe, and regimental stripe in shirts, ties, scarves, and accessories. Stripe structure is a defining element of the British menswear surface language.
- **Argyle and Fair Isle knit patterns**: Present in casualwear knitwear and accessories.
- **Micro-prints and small-scale prints**: Smaller floral and geometric surface prints deployed in shirting and accessories.
- **Racing livery**: The Williams Martini Racing partnership (2015+) added a colour-blocked stripe and motorsport graphic language to the brand's SPD vocabulary — a distinct departure from heritage textile patterns toward engineered performance graphic design.
- **Tartan**: Used in accessories and seasonal collections as part of British heritage surface vocabulary.

## Design Direction

Rooted in English heritage and Savile Row tradition, with controlled seasonal adjustment. Creative director Michael Sondag's Tommy Hilfiger background introduced preparatory-school Americana alongside British codes. The brand's visual surface language is conservative and craft-oriented — focused on fabric quality, weave structure, and established textile motifs rather than trend-driven print direction.
