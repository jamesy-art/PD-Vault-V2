# awwg

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](awwg.svg)

Spanish fashion group operating as the brand owner and licensor for Pepe Jeans London, Hackett London, and Façonnable; Pepe Jeans is the group's largest brand, with 50 years of denim-led fashion rooted in London's Portobello Road; Hackett London is a British menswear brand founded 1983 by Jeremy Hackett with a Savile Row bespoke service; Façonnable is a French Riviera men's prêt-à-porter brand founded 1950 in Nice.

## Overview

AWWG (operating site: awwg.com) is the group entity managing a portfolio of three fashion brands: Pepe Jeans London, Hackett London, and Façonnable. The source material — the AWWG brand portfolio page — describes the identity and positioning of each brand within the group but provides no corporate background on AWWG itself: no founding date, ownership structure, revenue, or employee count is documented. The three brands span denim-led international fashion (Pepe Jeans), British heritage menswear with Savile Row credentials (Hackett), and French Riviera smart-casual menswear (Façonnable).

## Company Profile

AWWG's portfolio comprises three distinct menswear and mixed fashion positioning brands:

**Pepe Jeans London** has operated for approximately 50 years in international denim-led fashion, with brand roots in London's Portobello Road market — a location central to the emergence of 1970s London street fashion. The brand has pursued a high-profile celebrity marketing strategy, collaborating with Kate Moss, Sienna Miller, Alexa Chung, Cara Delevingne, Georgia Jagger, and Dua Lipa on multimedia campaigns. The brand describes its strategy as "remaining sincere to the brand's DNA and in particular the roots in London's Portobello Road."

**Hackett London** was founded in 1983 by Jeremy Hackett. The brand has become a global menswear label blending British heritage with contemporary design across multiple sub-lines: the core Hackett London collection; Hackett Sport (premium activewear); Hackett Heritage (classic British sports heritage: rowing, rugby, sailing, motorsport); and the Savile Row collection with a bespoke tailoring service offered onsite at a Savile Row townhouse. Hackett describes its bespoke garments as using "the finest fabrics from the world's most prestigious mills."

**Façonnable** was established in 1950 on the Côte d'Azur and continues to be produced in Nice. The brand's identity is built on bright, colourful, smart-casual men's garments inspired by the lifestyle of the Mediterranean region between Nice and the French hinterland. The design aesthetic emphasises "quality materials and immaculate details" with a tailored, nautical, and sporty sensibility.

## Market Position

The three brands collectively address the British and European heritage menswear market from different positions: Pepe Jeans in accessible international denim fashion; Hackett in premium British tailoring and sportswear with genuine Savile Row credentials; and Façonnable in elevated French Mediterranean casualwear. The celebrity campaign strategy at Pepe Jeans (Kate Moss, Dua Lipa) positions that brand in the aspirational contemporary fashion segment, while Hackett's bespoke and Savile Row associations place it in the premium tailored menswear category.

## Products and Categories

Pepe Jeans: denim jeans and denim-led casualwear, men's and women's apparel and accessories. Hackett: men's suits, shirts, casualwear, sportswear, bespoke tailored garments; the brand spans office to social occasion dressing. Façonnable: men's smart-casual clothing, nautical-inspired casualwear, quality leisure and travel apparel.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Each brand in the AWWG portfolio has surface design relevance anchored in distinct traditions:

- **Pepe Jeans London — denim and Portobello Road origins**: Pepe Jeans' 50-year heritage begins in Portobello Road, a market historically associated with vintage textile trading, print fabric merchants, and the emergence of London street fashion as a design culture in the 1970s. Denim as a surface design category involves wash treatments (stone-wash, acid-wash, enzyme wash), laser printing, embroidery, distressing, embellishment, and whiskering — all applied-surface techniques. A brand built on denim-led fashion over 50 years has made thousands of seasonal surface design decisions across these techniques. Celebrity endorsement campaigns (photographed by major fashion photographers) also represent a high-visibility use of fashion surface design in advertising image-making.
- **Hackett London — heritage textile and Savile Row fabric selection**: Hackett's stated use of "the finest fabrics from the world's most prestigious mills" for its Savile Row and bespoke collection directly references the woven textile surface design tradition of British tailoring — the heritage of Dormeuil, Holland & Sherry, Scabal, and other Savile Row-grade mills. The Hackett Heritage sub-line draws on classic British sports categories — rowing, rugby, sailing, and motorsport — each of which has a distinctive surface design tradition (rugby stripes, rowing club colours, sailing navy palette, racing heritage graphics). The bespoke service also involves detailed fabric selection from mill sample books — a direct engagement with textile surface design at a highly curated level.
- **Façonnable — Mediterranean colour and print tradition**: Façonnable's identity in "bright, colourful garments" in a nautical, Mediterranean, and sporty register places it within a specific European print tradition: the vivid colours and relaxed patterns of French Riviera leisure fashion from the mid-twentieth century. The tailored, nautical aesthetic implies stripe use, anchor and sailing motifs, and the clean colourways associated with Mediterranean leisure clothing. The brand's continued production in Nice suggests a maintained connection to local artisanal textile traditions.

Façonnable's creative direction is rooted in the lifestyle of the Côte d'Azur with a consistent nautical and Mediterranean identity.
