# Chopard

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/chopard/profile/company-logo.svg)

Swiss luxury watchmaker and jeweller; founded 1860 in Sonvilier by Louis-Ulysse Chopard; owned by the Scheufele family of Germany since 1963; headquartered Geneva with movement manufacturing in Fleurier, Canton of Neuchâtel; ~75,000 timepieces and 75,000 jewellery pieces per year; CHF 800m sales (2014); ~2,000 employees (900 in Switzerland); ~100 stores worldwide (2010); Fairmined ethical gold sourcing since 2013; Cannes Film Festival official partner since 1998; Mille Miglia partner since 1988.

## Overview

Chopard (formally Le Petit-Fils de L.-U. Chopard & Cie S.A.) is a Swiss manufacturer and retailer of watches, jewellery, and accessories founded in 1860 by watchmaker Louis-Ulysse Chopard in Sonvilier, in the Swiss Jura. The Chopard family operated the company through three generations before Paul-André Chopard sold it in 1963 to Karl Scheufele III, a German goldsmith and watchmaker from Pforzheim — the Scheufele family has owned it since. Chopard relocated to Geneva in 1937, enabling certification of its movements with the Geneva Seal. By 2014 the company employed approximately 2,000 people (900 in Switzerland), operated ~100 stores globally, and recorded CHF 800m in annual sales. The company produces around 75,000 timepieces and 75,000 jewellery pieces each year and is a member of the Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry FH. The Fleurier manufacturing facility, established 1996, produces high-end in-house movements.

## Company Profile

Louis-Ulysse Chopard founded his L.U.C. company in 1860 in Sonvilier having observed that marketing a finished watch was more profitable than making movements alone — establishing from the outset a commercially oriented approach to high-end watchmaking. After relocating to Geneva in 1937 and qualifying for the Geneva Seal, the company specialised in pocket watches and ladies' wristwatches, moving into men's sports watches and diamond jewellery in the 1980s. The 1976 launch of watches featuring free-floating diamonds behind sapphire crystal — the Happy Diamond concept — became the brand's signature decorative invention: a moving ornamental motif within the watch face itself. A full in-house movement manufacturing facility was established in Fleurier in 1996. Chopard co-presidents Caroline and Karl-Friedrich Scheufele (children of the 1963 buyer) have led the modern brand. European Patent Office records list 20+ Chopard patents since 2002.

## Market Position

Chopard operates at the upper tier of Swiss luxury horology and fine jewellery. The brand's estimated sales of €550m in 2010 (of which €250m from watches) indicate a near-even split between watchmaking and jewellery — unusual in the Swiss luxury watch sector. Approximately 100 retail stores globally, a Mille Miglia motorsport partnership since 1988, and the Cannes Film Festival Trophée Chopard prize (since 1998) position the brand across both masculine (motor racing, horology) and feminine (cinema, jewellery) luxury segments. The Geneva Seal designation — applied only to movements made in Canton Geneva — is an important quality marker in the haute horlogerie tier.

## Products and Categories

Watches (mechanical and complications; Happy Diamond collection; sports watches; Mille Miglia editions), jewellery (fine and high jewellery; Fairmined gold collections including Green Carpet Collection and Ice Cube Capsule), accessories, fragrance, eyewear, bags, and kidswear.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Chopard's relevance to surface and decorative design is concentrated in jewellery and watch dial/case design:

- **Happy Diamond — moving ornamental surface motif**: The 1976 Happy Diamond concept — a free-floating diamond behind sapphire crystal on a watch dial — is a literal surface design innovation. The moving stone creates a dynamic decorative element on the watch face surface; the sapphire crystal becomes a display surface for a floating motif. This concept extended to bracelets and jewellery with diamonds floating in transparent chambers. The Christie's auction of a Happy Diamond wristwatch for US$1.67 million in 2015 measures its commercial value.
- **Jewellery as surface and material design**: Chopard's 75,000 jewellery pieces per year represent a substantial commercial output in a field that is fundamentally surface design at miniature scale. Fine jewellery involves the arrangement of gemstones, metalwork, enamel, and engraving on a surface — the same organising principles as surface pattern design, applied to three-dimensional luxury objects. The Fairmined collections (Green Carpet Collection, Ice Cube Capsule by Marion Cotillard) involve deliberate surface material selection with ethical sourcing credentials.
- **Geneva Seal and movement surface finishing**: The Geneva Seal certification requires that movement components meet strict standards for surface finishing — chamfering, anglage, Côtes de Genève (Geneva stripes) on movement plates, and perlage (circular graining) on mechanical surfaces. These are specialist micro-decorative surface techniques that define Haute Horlogerie as a surface craft discipline as much as an engineering one.
- **Palme d'Or trophy design**: Chopard creates the Cannes Film Festival Palme d'Or trophy, including a special diamond-set version for the 70th anniversary. Trophy design is a luxury surface and material design commission at high public visibility.
- **Cannes and Mille Miglia aesthetic associations**: Chopard's long-term partnerships with Cannes (since 1998) and Mille Miglia (since 1988) define two distinct design aesthetic territories — cinema glamour and motorsport heritage — both of which inform the decorative surface language of specific watch and jewellery collections.

## Design Direction

No named external creative director is documented in source material; design direction is led by the Scheufele family co-presidents. The brand's dual identity — watch manufacture rooted in Geneva and Fleurier precision, jewellery rooted in ethical fine stone and goldsmithing — produces two distinct surface design vocabularies. Watch design engages with the Haute Horlogerie tradition (movement finishing, case design, dial decoration). Jewellery design engages with the stone-setting and goldsmithing traditions of the Geneva and Pforzheim workshop heritage.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Approximately 100 stores worldwide as of 2010, including a Madison Avenue flagship in New York City. Retail distribution is through own boutiques and authorised dealers. Fairmined gold sourcing since 2013; WWF 2018 environmental assessment rated Chopard "Lower Midfield" for environmental performance. No licensing of the Chopard name to third-party manufacturers is documented in source material.
