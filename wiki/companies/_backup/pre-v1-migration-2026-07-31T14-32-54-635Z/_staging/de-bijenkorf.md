## Overview

De Bijenkorf (Dutch: "The Beehive") is a Dutch premium department store chain founded in 1870 by Simon Philip Goudsmit (1845–1889) in Amsterdam. Its flagship store is located at the corner of Dam Square and Damrak in central Amsterdam. Until 2010, the chain was owned by the Maxeda group (formerly VendexKBB). In 2010, it was acquired by the Weston family, who also owned Selfridges (UK), Holt Renfrew (Canada), and Brown Thomas (Ireland) — stores that together form the [[Selfridges Group]]. De Bijenkorf was a member of the Association Internationale des Grands Magasins from 1929 to 2012.

## Company Profile

De Bijenkorf's product offer — described in the source as comparable to Le Bon Marché in France — covers clothing, leather goods, jewellery, home décor and linen, and luggage. Retail is structured around brand boutique stands that offer curator-selected products within the store floor. The company operates e-commerce for Belgium (launched 2015, serving Flanders and Wallonia), Germany (2019), and France (November 2020).

From 2013 to 2016, De Bijenkorf undertook a restructuring and repositioning toward fewer, higher-quality flagship stores. Six branches were closed (Arnhem 1975–2013; 's-Hertogenbosch 2001–2016; Breda 2001–2016; Enschede 2002–2013; Groningen 2001–2016; Venlo 2005–2008). €200 million was invested in the remaining flagships and digital channels.

## Market Position

De Bijenkorf is the Netherlands' leading premium department store, comparable in positioning to Selfridges (UK), La Rinascente (Italy), and KaDeWe (Germany) — all stores within the [[Selfridges Group]] / [[Central Group]] European premium portfolio. Its curated brand-stand model supports a broad range of luxury and premium fashion, accessories, and home brands across seven Dutch cities.
