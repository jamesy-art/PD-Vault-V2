# Inter Parfums

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](301600976_196351079488778_6948529145702720139_n.jpg)

French fragrance licensing and manufacturing company; founded 1982 by Philippe Bénacin and Jean Madar; listed on Euronext Paris; HQ 10 rue de Solférino, Paris 7th (since January 2021); operates licensed fragrance programmes for fashion and luxury brands including Lacoste, Moncler, Kate Spade, Montblanc, Jimmy Choo, Rochas, and Maison Goutal; revenue target €880M by end of 2024.

## Overview

Interparfums SA was founded in 1982 by Philippe Bénacin and Jean Madar. It began in the mass perfume market producing and distributing affordable eau de toilettes before shifting in the early 1990s to prestige and niche perfumery through brand licensing. The first major licensing agreement — with Burberry (1993) — established the model: partner with an international fashion or luxury brand, develop the fragrance under license, and distribute globally. By 2013, Burberry represented roughly half of Interparfums' revenue before transferring its licence to BPI (Beauté Prestige International, a Shiseido subsidiary) in April 2013. The company rebuilt its portfolio with Coach (2015), Kate Spade (2019, 11-year exclusive), Moncler (2020, to December 2026 with possible extension), and Lacoste (December 2022, 15-year agreement effective January 2024). In March 2015, Interparfums acquired Rochas — a Procter & Gamble subsidiary with both perfume and fashion history — for $108 million. In 2025, the company acquired the IP of Maison Goutal (from Amore Pacific) and launched Solférino Paris, its first proprietary high-end fragrance brand. In January 2021, the group relocated its headquarters to 10 rue de Solférino in Paris's 7th arrondissement — the former historic headquarters of the French Socialist Party. ## Company Profile

Headquarters: 10 rue de Solférino, Paris 7th (since January 2021; previously 4 Rond-point des Champs-Élysées-Marcel-Dassault, Paris 8th). Listed on Euronext Paris. Website: interparfumsinc.com. Co-founded by Philippe Bénacin and Jean Madar (1982). Key licensing portfolio (current and former):

- **Active**: Lacoste (2024+, 15-year), Moncler (2020–December 2026), Kate Spade New York (2019+, 11-year exclusive), Montblanc, Jimmy Choo, Rochas (owned outright since 2015), Coach (from 2015), Karl Lagerfeld, Van Cleef & Arpels, Boucheron, ST Dupont, Repetto, Maison Goutal (acquired IP 2025)
- **Former**: Burberry (1993–April 2013; transferred to Shiseido/BPI), Balmain (terminated 2016 after Mayhoola acquisition), Lanvin
- **Own brands**: Rochas (fragrance and fashion, acquired 2015); Solférino Paris (launched 2025, first high-end proprietary fragrance brand)

In July 2020, Interparfums acquired 25% of origines-parfums.fr (subsequently rebranded MyOrigines) to accelerate international digital distribution. 2021 net profit was 40% above 2019 (last pre-pandemic year), at €71.1 million.

## Market Position

One of Europe's largest independent fragrance licensing operators — specialising in the development and distribution of prestige perfumes under luxury and fashion brand licences. Revenue trajectory: from mass-market early 1980s through prestige repositioning in 1990s through Burberry to diversified multi-brand portfolio post-2013. The company operates across prestige and accessible luxury — not ultra-luxury. Brand portfolio spans fashion (Kate Spade, Coach, Lacoste, Moncler), accessories (Montblanc, ST Dupont), fine jewellery (Van Cleef & Arpels, Boucheron), and shoes (Jimmy Choo). Revenue expected to reach €880M by end of 2024.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Fragrance is a design-intensive category where surface, packaging, and bottle design are primary brand differentiators:

- **Fragrance bottle design as product surface design**: The visual identity of a fragrance depends entirely on bottle form, surface treatment, label graphics, and packaging — all surface design disciplines. Interparfums' portfolio includes some of fashion's most iconic fragrance bottle forms (Jimmy Choo, Moncler, Kate Spade).
- **Brand graphic application**: Each licensed fragrance requires brand-consistent graphic design across cap, label, outer packaging, POS, and campaign — a significant surface design commission within each licensing relationship.
- **Rochas fashion heritage**: The Rochas acquisition (2015) brought a French couture house with a fashion and textile design legacy into the group — Rochas was associated with postwar couture surface elegance.
- **Lacoste licensing** (from 2024): Lacoste's brand identity is built on the distinctive polo shirt surface and the crocodile logo — a globally recognised pattern/motif system that extends into fragrance identity.
- **Moncler licensing**: Moncler's quilted surface and colour-block outerwear vocabulary translates into branded fragrance packaging and marketing materials.

## Design Direction

No in-house fashion design function. Fragrance bottle and packaging design is developed per licensing agreement, often in collaboration with the licensed brand's design team and specialist perfume bottle design studios. The Solférino Paris brand (2025) is Interparfums' first opportunity to build a proprietary visual identity from the ground up.
