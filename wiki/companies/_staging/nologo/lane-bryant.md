# Lane Bryant

Largest plus-size women's clothing retailer in the United States; founded 1904 in New York City by Lithuanian immigrant Lena Himmelstein Bryant Malsin (name misspelled "Lane" by a bank clerk); creator of the first commercially made maternity dress and the US plus-size clothing category; 448 stores across 46 states; current parent [[KnitWell Group]].

## Overview

Lane Bryant was founded in 1904 by Lena Himmelstein Bryant Malsin, a Lithuanian Jewish immigrant and widow who began selling handmade garments from a room in New York City. A bank officer recording her account misspelled her first name "Lena" as "Lane," inadvertently creating the brand name. The company created the first commercially made maternity dress — using an elasticised waistband and accordion-pleated skirt — and pioneered plus-size and maternity mail-order cataloguing. By 1911, a newspaper advertisement in the New York Herald generated a sell-out response, establishing the brand's mass-market reach. Sales reached $5 million by 1923. Lane Bryant is the largest plus-size women's apparel specialty retailer in the United States, operating 448 stores across 46 states (2022). The brand is currently owned by [[KnitWell Group]], following a 2020 Chapter 11 bankruptcy and Sycamore Partners acquisition.

## Market Position

Lane Bryant occupies a dominant position in the US plus-size women's apparel market — the category it created in the early twentieth century and in which it remains the largest specialty retailer by store count. Price positioning is mid-market, with product spanning everyday casualwear, workwear, occasion dressing, lingerie, swimwear, and activewear for sizes 14 and above. The brand's cultural visibility has been driven by inclusive advertising campaigns — #ImNoAngel (2015, countering Victoria's Secret), #PlusIsEqual, #ThisBody — generating media attention and two ABC/Fox censorship controversies around Cacique lingerie advertising (2010 featuring Ashley Graham; 2016). The ownership history spans six decades of corporate transactions, ending in Sycamore Partners/KnitWell Group after a COVID-19-triggered bankruptcy.
