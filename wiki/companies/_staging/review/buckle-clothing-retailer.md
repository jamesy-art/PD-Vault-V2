# Buckle (clothing retailer)

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/buckle-clothing-retailer/profile/company-logo.svg)

American fashion retailer; founded 1948 in Kearney, Nebraska as "Mills Clothing" by David Hirschfeld; 451 stores in 42 US states; evolved from men's discount clothier to denim-focused specialty retailer by the early 1970s; launched private label BKE brand (formerly 'bkle') in 1991, which now accounts for 35% of sales; NYSE listed (BKE); milestone of 5 million pairs of jeans sold in a single year reached in 2011.

## Overview

The Buckle, Inc. is an American fashion retailer selling clothing, footwear, and accessories for men, women, and children across 451 stores in 42 US states, operating under the names Buckle and The Buckle. The business was founded in 1948 in Kearney, Nebraska by David Hirschfeld as a men's clothing store named Mills Clothing. His son Dan Hirschfeld took over in 1965; a second store acquired in 1967 traded as Brass Buckle. By the early 1970s, Brass Buckle had pivoted to denim as its core product category. Women's clothing was added in 1977, and the first mall-based location opened the same year. In 1991, the company renamed itself The Buckle, Inc. and launched its private label clothing line 'bkle' — now branded BKE — which accounts for approximately 35% of total sales. The company went public on NASDAQ in 1992 (ticker: BKLE) and moved to the NYSE in 1997 (ticker: BKE). E-commerce launched in 1999. The company reached 400 stores in 41 states in 2009, completed a 240,000 square foot distribution centre in 2010, and achieved the milestone of 5 million pairs of jeans sold in one year in 2011.

## Company Profile

The Buckle's trajectory from a 1948 menswear discount store to a denim-heritage specialty retailer reflects the broader commercial significance of denim as a fashion category in American retail from the 1970s onwards. The Hirschfeld family's pivot to denim in the early 1970s positioned the business to capture the growth of casual, denim-led American fashion. The launch of the BKE private label in 1991 — which now generates 35% of total sales — demonstrates a deliberate in-house design strategy that reduces reliance on third-party brand partners and builds proprietary product capability. With 451 stores in 42 states (primarily mall-based), Buckle operates as a mainstream American fashion retailer with a specific denim and casual fashion identity, selling both branded and own-label merchandise.

## Market Position

Buckle occupies a mid-market position in American specialty fashion retail, competing in the segment for casual and denim-focused fashion across men's, women's, and children's categories. The mall-based store network (the first mall location opened 1977) positions the brand alongside other specialty retailers in the American shopping mall ecosystem. The 35% own-label BKE share indicates significant market differentiation from purely branded resellers. The 5 million pairs of jeans in 2011 reflects the scale of Buckle's commercial activity in denim specifically.

## Products and Categories

Denim jeans (core product category), casual bottoms, fashion tops and shirts, dresses and rompers, activewear and athleisure, outerwear, footwear, swimwear, fragrances, sunglasses, bags and purses, wallets, and accessories. Both brand name third-party merchandise and private label BKE product; BKE private label represents approximately 35% of sales. Children's categories are included in retail operations.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Buckle's surface pattern design relevance is grounded primarily in its denim heritage and its BKE private label design function:

- **Denim as surface design category**: Buckle grew from a general menswear store to a denim specialty retailer by the early 1970s, and denim has remained the core commercial identity. Denim surface design encompasses a significant range of treatment techniques: wash processes (stone-wash, acid-wash, enzyme wash, bleach wash), distressing (sandblasting, hand-sanding, whiskering), laser printing, embroidery, embellishment (rhinestones, studs, patch application), and paint or spray applications. Buckle's sustained focus on denim as its primary product category — demonstrated by the 5 million pairs per year milestone in 2011 — means that seasonal denim surface design decisions are central to its commercial operations.
- **BKE private label — in-house design function**: The BKE private label, generating 35% of total Buckle sales, represents a substantial in-house apparel design capability. Private label design at a fashion retailer involves seasonal decisions about fabric, print, wash treatment, embellishment, colour, and graphic design across the full product range (denim, tops, shirts, dresses, activewear). BKE's 35% sales share is commercially significant in a specialty retailer context, meaning the brand's in-house design team produces a meaningful proportion of all product sold through Buckle's 451 stores.
- **Fashion tops and graphic shirts**: The "fashion tops and shirts" merchandise category in a casual specialty retail context includes a significant volume of printed and graphic product — screen-printed graphic tees, woven print shirts, and embellished fashion tops are standard assortments in this retail segment. These categories involve print design, graphic design, and embellishment as primary surface design inputs.
- **Activewear/athleisure**: The activewear and athleisure category has an established surface design tradition in colour-blocking, sublimated print, and performance textile graphic design — all relevant to Buckle's active category offering.
- **Swimwear**: Swimwear is a category defined almost entirely by surface design choices (print, colour, graphic) in the product development process.
