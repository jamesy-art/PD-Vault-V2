# Luxottica

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/luxottica/profile/281157593_387443400062849_6267473333824958787_n.jpg)

Italian eyewear multinational founded in 1961 in Agordo, Veneto by Leonardo Del Vecchio; world's largest eyewear company before merger; merged with Essilor in October 2018 to form [[EssilorLuxottica]] (~€46.3B market cap); house brands include Ray-Ban, Persol, Oliver Peoples, Oakley; licensed brands include Chanel, Prada, Giorgio Armani, Burberry, Versace, Dolce & Gabbana; ~9,100 retail locations worldwide.

## Overview

Luxottica Group S.p.A. was founded in 1961 by Leonardo Del Vecchio in Agordo, province of Belluno, Veneto — the historic centre of the Italian eyewear industry. Del Vecchio began selling complete eyeglass frames under the Luxottica name in 1967, and by 1971 had ended contract manufacturing entirely. Convinced of the need for vertical integration, he acquired distribution company Scarrone in 1974 and established international subsidiaries from 1981. The first designer licensing agreement — with Giorgio Armani — was struck in 1988. Luxottica listed on the NYSE in 1990 and the Milan Stock Exchange (FTSE MIB) in December 2000. Key acquisitions: Vogue Eyewear (1990), Persol and LensCrafters (1995), Ray-Ban from Bausch & Lomb (1999), Sunglass Hut (2001), OPSM Australia (2003), Pearle Vision and Cole National (2004), Oakley (November 2007, US$2.1B — a hostile takeover following Oakley's attempt to dispute Luxottica's pricing, which had caused Luxottica to drop Oakley from its store network). Luxottica and Essilor announced their merger in January 2017; the European Commission approved it in March 2018; [[EssilorLuxottica]] was created on October 1, 2018 (~€46.3B combined market cap). Post-merger EssilorLuxottica commands approximately 30% of global eyewear market share. ## Company Profile

Luxottica Group S.p.A. (now subsidiary of [[EssilorLuxottica]]). Founded 1961, Agordo, Veneto, Italy. HQ (pre-merger): Milan, Italy. Retail division HQ: Mason, Ohio, United States. NYSE: LUX (listed 1990; delisted 2017). Milan: FTSE MIB (joined September 2003; delisted at merger). Founder: Leonardo Del Vecchio. Website: luxottica.com (now redirects to essilorluxottica.com). Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook documented in YAML (all EssilorLuxottica channels). EyeMed Vision Care: second-largest vision benefits company in the US (as of 2014). Google Glass partnership: announced March 2014.

## Market Position

Luxottica was the world's largest eyewear company prior to the Essilor merger. Post-merger EssilorLuxottica commands approximately 30% global eyewear market share. Euromonitor estimated Luxottica's pre-merger share at ~14% worldwide, ~20% in the United States. The company's prescription lens market share was 41% in 2017. The Oakley acquisition (2007, $2.1B) followed a documented competitive suppression strategy: Luxottica dropped Oakley from its stores, crashing Oakley's stock, then completed a hostile takeover. LensCrafters founder E. Dean Butler acknowledged in 2019 that Luxottica's market position enabled price markups of up to 1,000% on premium eyewear frames.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Luxottica's SPD relevance operates through two primary channels — fashion brand surface codes translated to eyewear frames, and the visual design of owned house brand frames:

- **Fashion brand surface identity on eyewear**: Luxottica manufactured eyewear under license for Chanel, Prada, Giorgio Armani, Burberry, Versace, Dolce & Gabbana, Michael Kors, and others — each requiring the translation of that brand's surface design language (monograms, logo hardware, brand colourways, print references) into frame design and temple decoration
- **Chanel eyewear surface**: Chanel eyewear (licensed to Luxottica) carries the Chanel tweed reference, quilted pattern allusion, and interlaced-C logo onto frame surfaces and temple decoration
- **Prada and Miu Miu surface design**: Prada and Miu Miu eyewear translates both houses' geometric and abstract surface motifs (triangular Prada logo, Miu Miu Art Deco references) into frame materialisation and surface treatment
- **Versace and Dolce & Gabbana baroque surface**: Both brands' licensing relationships involve heavy surface decoration — baroque print references, Medusa medallion hardware, mosaic-inspired frame treatments
- **Swarovski crystal embellishment**: The 10-year Swarovski eyewear license (December 2022) introduces crystal surface embellishment as a frame decoration element — directly relevant to jewellery-adjacent surface design on fashion accessories
- **Ray-Ban as iconic surface object**: The Ray-Ban Aviator and Wayfarer silhouettes are among the most widely replicated product surface designs in the world; their frame shapes and colourways have defined sunglasses surface design norms globally
- **Google Glass partnership (2014)**: The announced collaboration with Google to integrate Google Glass into Luxottica's eyewear design represents early wearable technology surface design integration

## Design Direction

House brands (Ray-Ban, Persol, Oliver Peoples, Oakley) maintain independent creative direction. Licensed brands' eyewear creative direction is coordinated between Luxottica's design teams and each fashion house — ensuring the licensed brand's surface identity is faithfully translated to eyewear surfaces (frame shape, colour, material, and decoration). The 2018 EssilorLuxottica merger consolidated global design and brand management under the combined entity.
