# Safilo

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/companies/safilo/profile/101120183_257474145328853_8039621196867698688_n.jpg)

Italian eyewear manufacturer and distributor; formally Safilo Group S.p.A. (Società Azionaria Fabbrica Italiana Lavorazione Occhiali); founded 1934 by Guglielmo Tabacchi in Calalzo di Cadore, Venetian Alps; HQ Padua, Italy; third largest eyewear company globally; designs, produces, and distributes prescription frames, sunglasses, sports eyewear, ski goggles, helmets, and cycling helmets under 5 house brands and 32 licensed brands; subsidiaries in 40 countries; 50+ distribution partners in 70 countries.

## Overview

Safilo Group S.p.A. is an Italian eyewear company and the oldest active player in the Italian eyewear industry. Its manufacturing roots trace to a lens and frame production site opened in 1878 in Calalzo di Cadore in the Venetian Alps; Guglielmo Tabacchi acquired this site in 1934 and founded Safilo (Società Azionaria Fabbrica Italiana Lavorazione Occhiali). After Guglielmo Tabacchi's death in 1974, his sons took over and developed the company through the 1970s–80s, patenting the Elasta hinge, building designer licensing relationships, and acquiring Smith Sports Optics and Carrera Optyl. Safilo was introduced on the Milan Stock Exchange in 1987 and relisted in December 2005. The company began producing Gucci eyewear in 1989, which opened a designer licensing strategy that at its peak encompassed Kering brands (YSL, Bottega Veneta, Alexander McQueen) and numerous others — before Kering and LVMH moved eyewear production in-house, causing Safilo to fall from second to third place in global eyewear by market position in 2022. As of the 2023 Annual Report, Safilo has wholly owned subsidiaries in 40 countries and 50+ distribution partners in 70 countries. The company's house brands are Carrera, Polaroid Eyewear, Smith Optics, Blenders Eyewear, Privé Revaux, and Seventh Street; it also manages 32 licensed eyewear brands. HAL Holdings N.V. holds 49.8% of Safilo (from January 2019). A 10-year eyewear agreement with Victoria Beckham (debut 2026) was announced in July 2025.

## Market Position

Safilo occupies the third position in global eyewear by market size, behind EssilorLuxottica (first) and Kering Eyewear (second as of 2022). The company's market position declined from second to third when both LVMH (through Thélios) and Kering (through Kering Eyewear) moved eyewear design and manufacturing for their fashion brands in-house — ending Safilo's licensing relationships with Gucci, Yves Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, and Alexander McQueen, which had been foundational to its licensed brand portfolio. Safilo's response has included: building its own digital-native brand portfolio (Blenders Eyewear, Privé Revaux, both acquired 2020), renewing and adding licences (Levi's renewed to 2029; Victoria Beckham from 2026), consolidating production in Italy and China, and exiting the US retail business (Solstice Sunglasses sold 2019) to focus on wholesale. The company maintains manufacturing in its own Italian facilities and additional plants in China and the United States.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Safilo Group's entire commercial activity is centred on the design, manufacture, and distribution of eyewear — a product category in which surface pattern, material texture, and applied graphic design are primary design tools at both the frame and lens level.

**Frame surface design** — the external surface of an eyewear frame is a designed object: acetate frame colours, patterns, and textures (tortoiseshell, marble, solid, gradient, multi-layer acetate inlay), metal frame finish (brushed, polished, matte, gold, silver, two-tone), and lens tint/mirror coating are the central surface design decisions in eyewear. Safilo's production of 2,500 new designs annually (2005 figure) and its operation across 5 house brands and 32 licensed brands at global scale makes it one of the world's highest-volume producers of applied surface design in the eyewear product category.

**Licensed brand surface translation** — the fundamental activity of a licensed eyewear manufacturer is translating a fashion brand's visual identity into eyewear surfaces. When Safilo produced eyewear for Gucci (from 1989), Yves Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, and Alexander McQueen, the design work involved: interpreting each brand's surface design vocabulary (GG monogram, YSL logo, Bottega's intrecciato weave, McQueen's skull and floral motifs) into frame shapes, acetate patterns, hardware, and lens treatments. This brand-surface translation is a specialised applied design discipline, and Safilo operated it at scale across multiple luxury brand licences simultaneously.

**Carrera's motorsport surface identity** — Carrera's heritage as an Austrian motorsport-associated sunglasses brand carries a specific visual vocabulary: bold acetate colourways, wrap shapes, oversized frames, and high-visibility lens treatments associated with 1970s–1980s motorsport and ski culture. This identity has been consistently applied as surface design direction across seasonal Carrera collections.

**Smith Optics sports engineering** — Smith's ski goggle and sports eyewear surface design involves lens chromatic engineering (ChromaPop lens technology, colour-reactive and polarised finishes) and frame graphic application (coloured rubber, print-on-strap, colour-blocked frame construction). Sports eyewear surface design bridges functional optics engineering with visual identity work.

**The Ocean Cleanup partnership (2020)** — Safilo's partnership with The Ocean Cleanup to produce sunglasses from ocean waste plastic represents a material surface decision as design statement: the visual surface of each frame is part-documented recycled plastic, with its colour variation and texture characteristics determined by the source material. This is an emerging form of material surface narrative in product design.

**Elasta hinge patent (1970s)** — the Elasta hinge is a functional innovation (spring hinge allowing flex in the frame temple) with direct implications for frame surface engineering — the hinge mechanism integrates into the frame surface construction. Patenting a functional surface element distinguishes Safilo in the intellectual property dimension of eyewear surface design.

## Design Direction

No group-level creative director is documented in the source. Design teams operate brand-by-brand across the house brands and in close coordination with licensing brand partners. The founding Tabacchi family owned the company through four generations: Guglielmo Tabacchi (founder, died 1974), his sons (1974–2001), and Vittorio Tabacchi (grandson, who took full ownership in 2001). Roberto Vedovotto served as CEO 2002–2006 and returned in November 2009. Current majority shareholder HAL Holdings N.V. (49.8%, from January 2019) reflects a shift from family-majority to institutional-majority ownership structure. The 2025 Victoria Beckham 10-year licensing agreement signals continued strategy of adding aspirational lifestyle brand licences to anchor the portfolio after the loss of major Kering licences.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Safilo operates exclusively on a wholesale distribution model since selling the Solstice Sunglasses US retail chain in 2019. The group distributes through wholly owned subsidiaries in 40 countries and 50+ distribution partners in 70 countries globally. Its licensed brand programme — 32 brands at the 2023 count — is the centrepiece of its commercial model: major fashion and lifestyle brands contract Safilo to design and produce their eyewear ranges, with Safilo supplying through its manufacturing network and distributing through its global wholesale infrastructure. The loss of Gucci, YSL, Bottega Veneta, and Alexander McQueen licences (as Kering's in-house Kering Eyewear became the second-largest eyewear company in 2022) is the most significant recent structural shift in Safilo's licensing portfolio. Milan Stock Exchange listed (returned December 2005). The Levi's renewal (to 2029) and Victoria Beckham (from 2026) confirm the replacement strategy.
