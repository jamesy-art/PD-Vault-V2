# Marzotto Group

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](../../assets/companies/marzotto-group/profile/company-logo.png)

Italian textile manufacturer; founded 1836 as Lanificio Luigi Marzotto & Figli in Valdagno, Veneto; produces woollen and cotton yarns for clothing; holds equity positions in knitwear yarn, linen yarn, and silk production; acquired Ratti (Como silk) in 2009.

## Overview

Marzotto Group is an Italian textile manufacturer headquartered in Valdagno, Veneto. Founded in 1836 as Lanificio Luigi Marzotto & Figli by Luigi Marzotto (1773–1859), the company began as a wool mill and has grown into one of Italy's most significant integrated textile producers. The group manufactures woollen and cotton yarns for clothing and, through equity investments, also produces woollen yarns for knitwear, linen yarns, and silk. In 2005, Marzotto Group's textile business was formally separated from the Valentino Fashion Group, which had been assembled under Pietro Marzotto's ownership in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The group is privately held and headquartered in Valdagno.

## Company Profile

Marzotto is one of Italy's oldest industrial enterprises. Key milestones:

- 1836: Luigi Marzotto (1773–1859) founds Lanificio Luigi Marzotto & Figli in Valdagno with 2,000 Venetian Lira capital
- 1842: Luigi hands business to son Gaetano
- Post-1861 (Italian unification): Gaetano buys a spinning factory near Maglione; payroll exceeds 1,200 employees
- 1910 (after Gaetano's death): Company splits — grandson takes Maglione factory; son Vittorio Emanuele takes Valdagno operations and drives export business
- 1920: Gaetano Jr. inherits Valdagno mill; modernises during 1920s; survives the Great Depression without nationalisation; reacquires Maglione mill
- 1935: Agricultural complex established between Fossalta di Portogruaro and the Venetian Lagoon
- WWII: Company placed under government control; Gaetano Jr. recovers it post-war and capitalises on the Italian economic miracle
- 1950s: Diversifies into private-label menswear ("Principe by Marzotto")
- 1961: Listed on Borsa Italiana (Milan)
- 1960s–70s: Begins exporting to the United States; up to 40% of clothing production transferred to the US
- 1987: Acquires Lanerossi → Marzotto becomes Europe's first fully integrated wool producer
- 1991: Acquires Hugo Boss
- 1994: Acquires Nová Mosilana (Czechoslovakia) wool mill
- 1997: Pietro Marzotto announces merger with HPI (Holding Partecipazioni Industriali) to form Gruppo Industriale Marzotto, which would have been the world's largest designer clothing manufacturer; Pietro abruptly stops negotiations; resigns as CEO; Jean de Jaeger appointed (first non-Marzotto CEO); Pietro retains majority shareholding
- 2000: Pietro buys Liteksas (Lithuanian wool garments manufacturer) to shift part of production to lower-cost market
- 2002: Pietro acquires Valentino S.p.A. (which had been struggling under HPI)
- 2005: Marzotto Group's textile business formally separated from Valentino Fashion Group
- 2009: Acquires NTB Nuova Tessilbrenta (cotton casual and sportswear); collaboration with Schneider Group for wool/noble fibre processing in Egypt (Marzotto 30% stake); acquires 66.7% of Ratti (Como silk company founded 1945 by Antonio Ratti), together with Faber Five (Antonio Favrin)
- July 2018: Davide Favrin appointed CEO, replacing Sergio Tamborini (who led the group since 2006)

## Market Position

Marzotto operates primarily as a B2B textile producer — a supplier of yarns and woven fabrics to the clothing and fashion industries rather than a consumer-facing brand. Its historical consumer-facing activity was concentrated in the "Principe by Marzotto" private-label menswear line (from the 1950s). The group's acquisition of Hugo Boss (1991) and Valentino (2002), and their subsequent separation (Hugo Boss sold; Valentino separated into its own group in 2005), reflect a period of fashion brand consolidation that Marzotto attempted and then unwound. The current group focus is the textile manufacturing core: wool, cotton, linen, silk, and knitwear yarn production. The Como silk acquisition (Ratti, 2009) and the Egyptian wool processing joint venture (Schneider Group, 2009) indicate continued vertical integration and geographic expansion of production.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Marzotto Group operates at the upstream end of the surface pattern design supply chain — as a textile producer whose fabrics are the substrate upon which surface pattern, colour, and texture are realised.

**Woven woollen cloth** is the foundational category. Marzotto's heritage in Valdagno wool milling since 1836 positions the group as a producer of the woven wool fabrics — flannels, tweeds, gabardines, worsteds, and suiting weights — used by menswear and womenswear designers globally. The surface appearance of woven woollen cloth (weave structure, yarn twist, colour blending, finish) is itself a form of surface design that Marzotto has produced continuously for nearly two centuries.

**Ratti** (acquired 2009) is commercially significant to surface pattern design in a direct way. Founded in 1945 in Como, Ratti is one of the leading names in Como silk manufacturing — a sector historically associated with woven and printed silk production for luxury fashion. Como silk manufacturers have historically produced both woven jacquard silk (with structural pattern embedded in the weave) and printed silk (with surface pattern applied by screen or digital printing). Ratti's position as one of Italy's most important Como companies in this sector gives Marzotto Group a direct stake in luxury silk surface design production — fabrics destined for scarves, dresses, blouses, linings, and accessories for international fashion houses.

**Cotton casual fabrics** (NTB Nuova Tessilbrenta, 2009) extend the group's coverage into the casual and sportswear fabric segment — a category where surface print, colour application, and finish are primary design activities.

**Menswear suiting** ("Principe by Marzotto" and US export activity) — Marzotto's wool cloth and private-label menswear history is rooted in the suiting fabric category, where woven check, stripe, and plain surface constructions are the primary pattern types. As Europe's first fully integrated wool producer (from 1987 following the Lanerossi acquisition), Marzotto was a critical upstream supplier for menswear suiting design.

## Design Direction

Marzotto Group is a manufacturing company rather than a creative design house. Its design output is at the fabric and yarn development level: fibre blending, weave structure, surface finish, and colour development for B2B clients. Davide Favrin became CEO in July 2018 (Management Engineering graduate; son of Antonio Favrin, historical manager and shareholder, CEO from 2002, president from 2004). No named head of fabric design or creative direction is documented in the source.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Marzotto Group does not operate consumer retail in the available source. Its routes to market are through wholesale supply to clothing brands, manufacturers, and fashion houses. The Valentino acquisition (2002) and separation (2005) ended Marzotto's direct participation in the luxury fashion brand market. Hugo Boss, acquired in 1991, was later sold. The group's licensing and supply activity is primarily at the fabric and yarn level — producing for fashion brands rather than licensing its own brand name for consumer products. Ratti's Como silk operations supply to luxury fashion clients (implied by Ratti's market position; no specific clients documented in source). The Marzotto Prize (established 1951) — awarding achievement in medicine, modern sciences, poetry, journalism, theatre, and painting — reflects the founding family's civic engagement with Valdagno and cultural patronage.
