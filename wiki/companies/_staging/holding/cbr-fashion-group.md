# CBR FASHION GROUP - Successful Fashion Brand with CECIL, Street One, and Street One MEN

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](cbr_fashion_logo.jpeg)

German privately held fashion company founded 1980 in Celle; operates Street One (1983), CECIL (1989), Street One MEN (2021), and Street One Studio (2024); present in 20 European countries; "one of the most important suppliers of fashion in Germany"; current owner Alteri Investors (British, from February 2018); CEO Jim Nowak; Street One delivers 8 collections per year; Street One MEN 10 per year; Street One Studio 12 per year.

## Overview

CBR Fashion Group is a German privately held fashion company founded in 1980 in Celle by skilled retailers with the aim of supplying the trade with high-quality commercial collections. The company's first brand, Street One, launched in 1983 as the first collection to offer the market a fast-moving young fashion range eight times per year. CECIL followed in 1989, also targeting the young fashion segment. International expansion began in 1990 (Austria, Netherlands, Switzerland) and today spans 20 European countries. Street One MEN launched in 2021 (10 collections per year); Street One Studio launched in 2024 (12 collections per year). The group is currently owned by British specialist retail investor Alteri Investors (from February 2018) and led by CEO Jim Nowak, who previously held management positions at Esprit and Mexx.

## Company Profile

CBR's founding in 1980 by skilled retailers — rather than designers — established a commercial-first orientation that has shaped the group's product strategy across four decades. The Street One brand (1983) was the first to position itself explicitly on collection frequency, offering eight collections per year at a time when two seasonal collections per year was the norm — a structural commitment to trend responsiveness. CEIL (1989) was positioned in the same young fashion segment as a complementary brand. Leadership transferred from founders to professional management in 2002; private equity investors entered in 2004 (Cinven and Apax) and 2007 (EQT V Ltd), supporting the group's scale and operational development. E-commerce launched in 2011. Alteri Investors (founded 2014, specialising in European retail) acquired the group in February 2018 and describes themselves as an "excellent financial partner" in European retail. The CFO, Christian Rudlof, has been with CBR since 2013, previously serving at Netrada Holding and Otto Group.

## Market Position

CBR positions as "one of the most important suppliers of fashion in Germany" and one of the best-known fashion companies in Europe. The company operates through independent local retail partners — physical retail trade accounts rather than primarily company-owned stores — alongside e-commerce (launched 2011). The group's market positioning is accessible European fashion at a contemporary-commercial level, targeting customers who shop in towns and cities across 20 European countries from small towns to major metropolitan centres. The high collection frequency (8–12 per year depending on brand) places CBR well within the fast-fashion structural model.

## Products and Categories

Women's fashion apparel and accessories under Street One and CECIL; men's fashion apparel under Street One MEN; contemporary lifestyle fashion under Street One Studio. The brand philosophy emphasises quality and durability ("fashion is not just made for a season, but should be worn for years to come") — a durability-focused positioning that distinguishes CBR's stated values from pure disposable fast fashion, even at high collection frequency.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

CBR Fashion Group's surface pattern design relevance is driven primarily by the extraordinary volume of seasonal collection design the group produces:

- **Collection frequency as SPD scale**: Street One's eight collections per year — established from the brand's 1983 founding as an industry innovation — means the Street One design team makes seasonal surface design decisions (print selection, colour palette, pattern choice, graphic development) eight times per year rather than the industry standard two. Street One MEN at 10 collections per year and Street One Studio at 12 collections per year extend this further. Across the group's four brands, CBR's design teams are producing print, colour, and surface decisions approximately 38 times per year in aggregate — one of the highest collection frequencies in the European fashion market. Each collection involves a new set of print and pattern decisions across tops, dresses, bottoms, outerwear, and accessories.
- **"Fast-moving young fashion" positioning**: Street One's founding brief — "fast-moving young fashion collection eight times a year" — is a direct commitment to trend-responsive design. In young women's fashion, print and pattern are among the most powerful signals of trend currency: the right floral print or graphic treatment can define a collection's commercial performance. CBR's design teams explicitly work from trend intelligence to translate "current fashion trends into relevant items" — a standard commercial fashion design workflow where SPD decisions are central.
- **CECIL and Street One Studio as additional design channels**: CEIL adds a second design team working in the young fashion segment. Street One Studio (launched 2024, 12 collections/year) introduces the highest-frequency brand in the portfolio — 12 collections annually represents monthly collection turnover and requires a design process with very fast print and surface design cycles.
- **20-country European distribution**: The breadth of CBR's European distribution (20 countries, from small towns to major cities, through independent local retail partners) means that print and surface design choices made by CBR's teams reach consumers across a broad geographic range. The group's network of independent local retail partners also makes CBR a significant commercial supplier to European fashion retail in aggregate.
- **Durability-as-sustainability positioning**: CBR's stated commitment to quality and longevity — "We uncompromisingly focus on durability and strong quality in each of our products" — positions the group against purely disposable fashion design. If genuine, this implies a surface design approach in which print choices are selected for longer commercial life rather than single-season trend currency.

## Design Direction

CEO Jim Nowak has product design expertise developed at Esprit (a brand with a historically print-forward, colourful identity) and Mexx (a Dutch pan-European fashion brand). The stated design process: "our design teams translate current fashion trends into relevant items for our customers" — trend-responsive commercial design guided by proximity to market and consumer knowledge. No named head of design or creative director is documented in source material for any of the four brands.

## Retail and Licensing Context

CBR operates primarily through a wholesale model supplying independent local retail partners across 20 European countries, alongside own e-commerce (launched 2011). The group does not appear to operate company-owned retail stores as the primary channel — trade supply to independent retailers is the described distribution model. Ownership history: founded 1980 (owner-managed) → Cinven/Apax majority 2004 → EQT V Ltd + management 2007 → Alteri Investors (UK) from February 2018. Financial investor involvement since 2004 reflects a private equity management model common in European mid-market fashion.
