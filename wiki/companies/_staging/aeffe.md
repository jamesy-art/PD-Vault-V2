# Aeffe

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](wiki/assets/companies/aeffe/profile/company-logo.svg)

Italian luxury fashion group founded 1988; owns Alberta Ferretti, Philosophy di Lorenzo Serafini, Moschino, and Pollini; has licensed and produced collections for Jean-Paul Gaultier, Narciso Rodriguez, Jeremy Scott, Basso & Brooke, and Emanuel Ungaro; listed on the Milan Stock Exchange (Borsa Italiana: AEF); revenue €351.4 million (2019).

## Overview

Aeffe S.p.A. is an Italian joint-stock company that operates internationally in the luxury fashion sector, producing ready-to-wear, footwear, leather goods, lingerie, and beachwear. Founded in 1988 — with roots in Alberta Ferretti's sole-proprietorship business begun in 1972 — Aeffe has been listed on the Milan Stock Exchange since 2007 (Borsa Italiana: AEF), where it is included in the FTSE Italia STAR and FTSE Italia Small Cap indices. The group develops collections for its proprietary brands (Alberta Ferretti, Philosophy di Lorenzo Serafini, Moschino, Pollini) and has historically operated as exclusive licensee for major international designers. In September 2024, Aeffe sold the Moschino perfume licence to Euroitalia.

## Company Profile

Aeffe's first licensed collaboration dates to 1983, when it began producing Franco Moschino's Moschino Couture line under licence. Expansion through designer licensing partnerships followed across four decades: Jean-Paul Gaultier (1994–2012, ended Spring/Summer 2013), Narciso Rodriguez (1997–2007), Cacharel (2009–2012), Emanuel Ungaro (2012–2015), Cédric Charlier (2012–2020), and Jeremy Scott (from 2015). Basso & Brooke production was taken on from 2005. The group took control of Pollini (footwear house) in late 2000 and introduced an apparel line in 2003; creative directors of the Pollini apparel line included Rifat Ozbek (2004–2007), Jonathan Saunders (2008), and Nicholas Kirkwood (from 2010); the apparel line was discontinued in 2010 while footwear continued. Aeffe acquired the remaining 28% of Pollini in 2011 for €1.2 million. Full ownership of Moschino was achieved in 2021 when Aeffe acquired the remaining 30% stake it had not previously owned. Sanpaolo IMI held a 20% stake in Aeffe from 2000 to 2006 (purchased for $44.5 million); Aeffe bought this stake back in November 2006. The group also licenses to partners for production and distribution of fragrances, children's and junior lines, watches, and eyewear.

## Market Position

Aeffe occupies an unusual position in the Italian fashion industry as both a brand owner and a long-established production and licensing partner for significant international designer labels. The group's capacity to develop and produce collections across multiple creative directors and aesthetic directions — simultaneously running Alberta Ferretti's romantic femininity, Moschino's graphic irreverence, and Jeremy Scott's pop-cultural maximalism — demonstrates operational versatility at scale. Revenue of €351.4 million in 2019 with €12.0 million net profit reflects a mid-tier luxury group with broad creative range. The Milan Stock Exchange listing (since 2007) places Aeffe in the same publicly accountable market as larger Italian fashion conglomerates.

## Products and Categories

Ready-to-wear collections across all four proprietary brands (Alberta Ferretti, Philosophy di Lorenzo Serafini, Moschino, Pollini); footwear (Pollini is primarily a footwear brand); leather goods; lingerie; beachwear. The group also produces fragrances, children's and junior clothing lines, watches, and eyewear under licence to external partners for its proprietary brands.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Aeffe's portfolio and licensing history encompasses some of the most distinctive surface pattern and graphic design identities in luxury fashion:

- **Moschino**: The Moschino brand — fully owned by Aeffe since 2021 and produced under licence from 1983 — is one of fashion's most explicitly graphic and surface-design-driven labels. Franco Moschino's original vision drew on pop art, logo repetition, and subversive graphic motifs. Under Jeremy Scott's creative direction (licensed through Aeffe from 2015), Moschino became internationally recognised for cartoon-referencing graphic prints, fast food imagery applied to garment surfaces, and bold pop-cultural visual statements. This is among the most overt examples of surface pattern and motif design as the central creative statement in a luxury fashion context.
- **Jeremy Scott**: As an independently licensed brand within Aeffe (from 2015), Jeremy Scott's output shares the same graphic maximalism — bold prints, cartoon motifs, commercial iconography translated to fashion surfaces.
- **Basso & Brooke (from 2005)**: Basso & Brooke is one of the most directly significant brands in the history of digital fashion print. Co-founded by Brazilian designer Bruno Basso and British designer Christopher Brooke, the label established its entire aesthetic identity on large-scale, complex digital print — intricate, photographic, and layered prints that were critically important in establishing digital printing as a luxury fashion medium. Aeffe's production of Basso & Brooke from 2005 placed one of surface pattern design's most innovative practices within the group's manufacturing infrastructure.
- **Jonathan Saunders as Pollini Creative Director (2008)**: Jonathan Saunders, the Scottish designer widely recognised for his geometric prints and complex colour-blocking, served as creative director of the Pollini apparel line in 2008 — bringing his characteristic print-forward design approach to Aeffe's production capabilities.
- **Alberta Ferretti**: Known for romantic, feminine aesthetic employing delicate fabric surfaces, organza, lace, and embellishment — a different surface design register (textile quality, embellishment, drape) than Moschino but equally fabric-forward in design identity.
- **Jean-Paul Gaultier (1994–2012)**: Gaultier's aesthetic drew heavily on graphic body references, sailor stripes, Breton motifs, and pattern as body commentary — produced through Aeffe's infrastructure across almost two decades.

## Design Direction

Each proprietary brand maintains a distinct creative direction: Alberta Ferretti's romantic minimalism in refined fabrics; Philosophy di Lorenzo Serafini's younger, fashion-forward positioning; Moschino's graphic, pop-cultural maximalism (currently under the Jeremy Scott creative direction which Aeffe licenses); and Pollini's footwear specialism. The group's ability to simultaneously support these diverse aesthetics reflects the breadth of its production capabilities.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Aeffe operates both directly owned retail and wholesale distribution for its brands. The licensing model operates in both directions: Aeffe has historically been a licensee (producing under licence for Gaultier, Ungaro, Scott, and others) and a licensor (granting licences to partners for fragrances, eyewear, children's lines). The Moschino fragrance licence was sold to Euroitalia in September 2024. Jeremy Scott operates as a licensed brand within Aeffe from 2015. The ending of the Cédric Charlier relationship in 2020, and the Moschino perfume sale in 2024, indicate ongoing portfolio rationalisation.
