# Hudson's Bay

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](495488671_18503334826041764_6026207105386302366_n.jpg)

Canadian department store chain and flagship brand of Hudson's Bay Company. Oldest company in North America. Defined by the Hudson's Bay Point Blanket stripe — one of the most recognisable trademarked textile patterns in Canadian history. Filed for creditor protection March 2025; all 80 stores closed by June 2025.

## Overview

Hudson's Bay was the flagship department store chain of Hudson's Bay Company (HBC), the oldest company in North America (founded 1670). The first department store under the brand opened in Winnipeg in 1881. By 2025, the chain operated 80 stores across Canada. Hudson's Bay filed for creditor protection in March 2025 and closed all stores by 1 June 2025, resulting in 8,000+ job losses. Canadian Tire Corporation acquired HBC's brand intellectual property — including the Hudson's Bay Point Blanket stripe trademark — for $30 million in May 2025, and began marketing Point Blankets under the acquired IP by October 2025.

## Company Profile

HBC reorganised its retail operations in 1910 and adopted a Harrods-guided full-line department store model from 1913. Major acquisitions over the twentieth century: Morgan's (1960), Freimans (1972), Simpsons (1991), Woodward's (1993). The brand name was informally known as "the Bay" for several decades before a formal rebrand to Hudson's Bay in October 2012, with the new identity launching publicly on 6 March 2013 (design by Lipman; heraldic coat of arms redrawn by illustrator Mark Summers). NRDC Equity Partners, owner of Saks Fifth Avenue, acquired HBC in July 2008.

## Market Position

Historically the dominant full-service department store in English Canada, competing with Sears Canada (closed 2017) and Nordstrom (Canadian operation closed 2023). The chain offered fashion, home, beauty, and accessories across a broad price range. The Room, a luxury womenswear department established in the 1930s as the St. Regis Room, represented the premium end of the offer — stocking Balmain, Armani, and Moschino. The Room flagship was renovated in 2009 by design firm Yabu Pushelberg ($5.3M, 2,000 sq m) and extended to Vancouver (2011) and Montreal (2013). Ground-floor boutique partnerships with Burberry, Coach, and See by Chloé were also documented.

## Products and Categories

Full-line Canadian department store: fashion, accessories, beauty, home, gifting, and electronics. The HBC Signature Shop was the brand's heritage-oriented retail line, centred on the Hudson's Bay Point Blanket stripe pattern applied to blankets, coats, bags, and T-shirts. Limited-edition Signature collaborations included partnerships with Virginia Johnson, Pink Tartan, and Klaxon Howl. Heritage gifting partnerships included Steiff (heritage teddy bear, 2,500-unit run) and Best Made Axe Co.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

The Hudson's Bay Point Blanket stripe is the brand's most significant and documented surface pattern asset.

**Origin and history**: Hudson's Bay Company commissioned woollen blankets from English mills beginning in 1779 for use as trade goods with First Nations peoples across the Canadian fur trade. The multi-stripe design — combining broad stripes of green, red, yellow, and indigo on a cream or white ground — became known as the "Queen Anne colours" pattern. The four-stripe variant became the most recognisable version and was trademarked worldwide.

**Commercial extension**: From its origin as a functional wool blanket, the Point Blanket stripe was extended across apparel and lifestyle products. Coats and outerwear carrying the stripe pattern became a Canadian wardrobe staple sold through the HBC Signature Shop, Lord & Taylor (US), and Colette (Paris). The stripe's status as a national symbol was reinforced by its prominent use in Hudson's Bay's official 2010 Winter Olympics clothing collection, which generated significant commercial and cultural visibility for the pattern.

**IP transfer**: Canadian Tire Corporation acquired the Hudson's Bay brand, Point Blanket stripe trademark, coat of arms, and related IP (including the Gluckstein, Distinctly Home, and Hudson North sub-brands) for $30 million in May 2025. Canadian Tire began commercially exploiting the stripe IP — including marketing Point Blankets — by October 2025, continuing the pattern's commercial life under new ownership.

## Design Direction

The brand's design identity was built primarily on the Point Blanket stripe heritage rather than a contemporary design programme. The 2013 rebrand reinterpreted the heraldic identity (coat of arms, beaver, shield) through updated illustration. The HBC Signature Shop operated as a heritage retail concept in which the stripe pattern was the primary design asset. No in-house fashion design operation is documented.

## Retail and Licensing Context

Hudson's Bay operated as a multi-brand retailer stocking external brands, with the Signature Shop as its primary own-brand retail proposition. The stripe pattern was distributed internationally through wholesale arrangements with Lord & Taylor and Colette. The IP acquisition by Canadian Tire in 2025 transfers the stripe trademark into a new retail context — Canadian Tire will control the licensing and merchandising of the stripe across future product categories.
