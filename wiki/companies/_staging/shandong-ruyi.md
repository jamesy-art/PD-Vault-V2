# Shandong Ruyi

## Profile Image

![pd-profile|160](ruyigroup_logo.jpeg)

Chinese textiles and clothing conglomerate; full name Shandong Ruyi Technology Group Co., Ltd; founded 1972 in Jining, China; as of 2017 the largest textile manufacturer in China; acquired [[SMCP Group]] (Sandro, Maje, Claudie Pierlot) in 2016 for £1.15B; held Aquascutum, Trinity Ltd (HK menswear), and temporarily Bally (Swiss luxury) and The Lycra Company (acquired 2019, lost to creditors 2022); expressed ambition to be the "Chinese equivalent of LVMH"; struggled with debt and COVID-19 from 2020.

## Overview

Shandong Ruyi Technology Group Co., Ltd is a Chinese textiles and clothing company founded in 1972 in Jining, Shandong Province, China. It is a subsidiary of Jining Ruyi Investment Co., Ltd. Originally known as Shandong Jining Woolen Mill, the company was formally restructured as Shandong Ruyi in 2001. Qiu Yafu became chairman in 1997. As of 2017, Ruyi Group was the largest textile manufacturer in China. The company operates across the full clothing industrial chain: yarn, fabric, raw materials, dyeing, weaving, sewing, and production. From 2010 onward, Ruyi Group pursued an aggressive international luxury acquisition strategy, acquiring minority stakes and full ownership of European and Japanese fashion brands. ## Market Position

As of 2017, Ruyi Group was the largest textile manufacturer in China and in the top 20 global luxury fashion groups by revenue (as of 2018). The company's stated ambition was to become the "Chinese equivalent of LVMH." Its acquisition strategy targeted European luxury brands (Aquascutum, SMCP, Bally), Japanese apparel (Renown Inc., Trinity Ltd), and global performance textile businesses (Invista's Lycra business). The SMCP Group acquisition (£1.15B in 2016) was the most significant fashion deal — gaining ownership of Sandro Paris, Maje, and Claudie Pierlot, which were subsequently taken public in 2017. Significant debt accumulation and COVID-19 impacts from 2020 constrained further growth; The Lycra Company was lost to creditors in 2022 after loan defaults.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Shandong Ruyi's SPD relevance operates at two levels — as a textile manufacturer and as a brand holding company:

**As textile manufacturer:**
- **Yarn, fabric, and textile production**: Shandong Ruyi's core manufacturing operations produce the woven and knitted textile substrates on which surface patterns are applied in the downstream fashion supply chain. As China's largest textile manufacturer (2017), the company is a major production node in the global fashion textile supply chain
- **Wool textile heritage**: The company's origins as Shandong Jining Woolen Mill place it within the wool textile tradition — woven woollen and worsted fabrics carry surface pattern (check, stripe, tweed, jacquard) as a primary design element
- **Performance fibre (The Lycra Company, 2019–2022)**: Lycra/spandex and Coolmax performance fibres are critical substrates for activewear and swimwear surface design — stretch properties determine how surface patterns behave on the body. Shandong Ruyi briefly held The Lycra Company (lost to creditors 2022)

**As brand holding company:**
- **[[SMCP Group]] (Sandro, Maje, Claudie Pierlot)**: Contemporary French fashion brands whose primary design output includes seasonal print and surface direction on womenswear — florals, abstract, and geometric prints on RTW garments. Shandong Ruyi's majority ownership (2016–c.2022) of SMCP gave it strategic control over three significant European contemporary fashion print brands
- **Aquascutum**: British luxury brand historically associated with the Aquascutum check — a signature heritage check pattern. Owning Aquascutum gave Shandong Ruyi stewardship of one of British fashion's most recognisable surface pattern identities
- **Bally**: Swiss luxury brand with a distinctive surface design identity in luxury leather goods

## Design Direction

Shandong Ruyi does not operate a central consumer design function — it is a manufacturer and holding company. Design direction for its portfolio brands (SMCP, Aquascutum, Trinity) is managed by each brand's own creative teams. The company's strategic design contribution is in M&A — assembling a portfolio of brands with strong print/surface identities and European luxury credentials, in pursuit of its stated LVMH-equivalent ambition. Debt and COVID-19 challenges constrained the strategic execution from 2020.
