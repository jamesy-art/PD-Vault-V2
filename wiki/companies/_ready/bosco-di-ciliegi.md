## Overview

Bosco di Ciliegi is a Russian luxury fashion retail group and the controlling shareholder of GUM — the historic State Department Store on Red Square in Moscow, one of the world's most recognisable retail buildings. The company was founded in 1991 when a group of graduates of the Moscow Art Theatre began organising an amusement park in Moscow's Gorky Park and simultaneously made contact with Italian fashion manufacturers. Its first commercial activity began in 1992 with consignment supply from the SIMA knitwear factory in Reggio Emilia, resulting in three boutiques — Nani Bon, Giunco, and Fiume — in Moscow's Petrovsky Passazh shopping centre. In 1993, a boutique gallery was developed in Petrovsky Passazh and the Bosco di Ciliegi name emerged. In 2004, the company acquired a controlling stake in GUM Trading House OJSC for approximately $100 million; the GUM building on Red Square is leased by Bosco di Ciliegi until 2059, the terms of which are classified as a state secret.

Bosco Sport's design direction for national Olympic kit is driven by national identity requirements — Russian tricolour, Soviet/Russian design heritage, and the requirements of performance sportswear engineering. No named design director for the Bosco Sport brand is documented in source material. The company's luxury multi-brand retail activity reflects curation rather than proprietary design direction. The Italian fashion origin story (SIMA knitwear, Reggio Emilia, 1992) embedded an Italian fashion reference point in the company's founding aesthetic framework.

## History

The company's founding by Moscow Art Theatre graduates establishes an arts and cultural background as the foundation of its curatorial approach to luxury retail — a context that shaped the boutique identity and editorial quality of its multi-brand retail model from the outset. The Italian fashion connections established in 1992 (with Reggio Emilia knitwear manufacture via SIMA) provided the initial supply chain. Through the 1990s and 2000s, Bosco di Ciliegi expanded from Petrovsky Passazh to GUM and across Russian regional cities (St. Petersburg, Samara, Yekaterinburg, Omsk), with international locations in London and Milan. The company operates its own multi-brand retail fascias alongside authorised monobrand boutiques: BoscoDonna (women's), BoscoUomo (men's), Bosco π (lifestyle), BoscoBambino (children's), BoscoScarpa (footwear), Bosco (Privé) personal service salon, Sublime Bosco (jewellery and watches), and Rimanenze Dolci (outlet stores for past collections). Adjacent services include Articoli perfume and cosmetics stores, Articoli Salon & Spa, Dior Institut beauty salons, restaurants (Bosco Café, L'Altro Bosco Café, Bosco π Bar, Bosco Cafeteria, Emporio Armani Café), Bosco Pharmacy, and Bosco Clinic.

## Products

Third-party brands distributed across Bosco di Ciliegi monobrand stores: Alberta Ferretti, Kenzo, Max Mara Group (multiple brands), La Perla, Etro, Ermanno Scervino, Paul Smith, Gucci, Zegna, Barbara Bui, and others. Own multi-brand fascias carry mixed selections across women's, men's, children's, and footwear. Bosco Sport: own-label sportswear brand used for Russian, Ukrainian, and Spanish Olympic team kit supply. Jewellery and watches via Sublime Bosco. Perfume, cosmetics, and beauty via Articoli. Outlet product via Rimanenze Dolci.

## Services

Bosco di Ciliegi operates 100+ monobrand stores across Moscow (including GUM and Petrovsky Passazh), St. Petersburg, Samara, Yekaterinburg, Omsk, London, and Milan. It distributes international luxury brands via authorised monobrand boutique agreements (not a department store wholesale model). GUM — the historic building on Red Square — is the group's flagship retail asset, leased until 2059. The Bosco Sport brand operates as Bosco di Ciliegi's own-label commercial division, with national Olympic team supply contracts generating both revenue and brand visibility. Rimanenze Dolci outlets sell product from past collections across the group.

## Markets

Bosco di Ciliegi occupies the top tier of Russian luxury fashion retail, with the controlling position in GUM — the symbolic commercial heart of Moscow — providing unmatched retail prestige. Ownership of the GUM lease to 2059 gives the company long-term control of what is effectively the most prominent luxury retail location in Russia. The Bosco Sport Olympic partnerships (Russia from 2001, Ukraine from 2009, Spain from 2011) have given the company national-scale visibility as a sportswear provider at the highest level of athletic competition. The combination of luxury multi-brand retail, own-brand multi-brand fascias, and Bosco Sport uniform supply positions the company across both high-fashion and performance apparel market segments.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Bosco di Ciliegi's surface pattern design relevance operates across two distinct channels — luxury fashion curation and Olympic sportswear design:

- **Curating print-intensive luxury fashion houses**: Several of the brands distributed through Bosco di Ciliegi monobrand stores have surface pattern design as a defining element of their identity:
  - **Etro**: the Italian luxury house is internationally known for its distinctive interpretation of Paisley motifs across its full product range — clothing, accessories, and home goods. Etro's paisley is among the most commercially recognised fabric print motifs in contemporary luxury fashion.
  - **Kenzo**: the Japanese-French fashion house founded by Kenzo Takada built its identity on bold floral and botanical print, vibrant colour, and graphic surface energy. Kenzo's print identity has remained central to the brand across ownership by LVMH and beyond.
  - **Paul Smith**: the British designer's signature multi-stripe motif — running irregular or ordered bands of colour across accessories and clothing — is one of the most recognised branded surface design motifs in contemporary British fashion.
  - **Max Mara**: the Italian group's brands address the refined womenswear segment; fabric and textile surface quality are central to the product proposition.
  - **La Perla**: Italian luxury lingerie brand; surface design in embellishment (lace, embroidery, delicate fabric treatment) is the primary product differentiator.
  - **Alberta Ferretti**: Italian fashion house; garment textile craftsmanship and surface quality are core to the brand's design identity.
- **Bosco Sport Olympic uniform design**: The Bosco Sport brand was the official supplier of the Russian national Olympic team across multiple Games — Salt Lake City (2002), Athens (2004), Turin (2006), Beijing (2008), Vancouver (2010), London (2012) — as well as the Ukrainian team (from 2009) and the Spanish team (from 2011, eight-year contract). National Olympic team uniform design is a high-visibility, high-stakes surface design commission: the uniforms must represent national identity through colour, motif, and graphic design, reaching global audiences through live broadcasting and photography. Bosco Sport's repeated role as official Russian Olympic kit supplier represents a significant national sportswear surface design function over more than a decade.
- **Sochi 2014 general partner**: Bosco Sport became general partner of the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympic and Paralympic Games in October 2009 — the highest level of Olympic affiliation available to a commercial sponsor, likely involving comprehensive kit design for Russian athletes at the home Games.
- **Founding by arts graduates**: The company's origins among Moscow Art Theatre graduates — a prestigious Russian theatre institution — grounds its curatorial aesthetic sensibility in a fine art and performance tradition, distinct from the more commercially driven origins of most luxury retail chains.
