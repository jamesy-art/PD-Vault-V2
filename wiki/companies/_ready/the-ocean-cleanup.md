# The Ocean Cleanup

## Overview

The Ocean Cleanup is a Dutch nonprofit environmental engineering organisation founded in 2013 by then-19-year-old Boyan Slat in the Netherlands. Headquartered in Rotterdam, the organisation develops large-scale passive and active systems to remove plastic pollution from oceans and rivers. Its ocean cleanup systems include System 001 (2018), System 002 "Jenifer" (2021), and System 03 (2023, a 2,250m barrier deployed November 2023); its river Interceptor systems have been deployed across Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Dominican Republic, Jamaica, Thailand, Guatemala, and Cambodia. As of January 2026, The Ocean Cleanup has removed more than 50 million kilograms of plastic from oceans and rivers. The organisation entered the consumer product space in October 2020 with a limited-edition sunglasses collection made from certified Great Pacific Garbage Patch (GPGP) ocean plastic — designed by Yves Béhar (fuseproject), manufactured by Safilo Group, 21,000 pairs at €200 each, sold out by early 2022. It is this product collaboration that connects The Ocean Cleanup to the eyewear and fashion supply chain. ## Markets

The Ocean Cleanup is not a commercial brand; it operates as a nonprofit engineering organisation funded by donations and corporate partnerships. Key funders include Marc Benioff ($35M, 2018), Joe Gebbia ($25M, 2023), Kia (7-year global partnership, 2022), Deloitte, and the Dutch government. The 2026 Audacious Project grant ($121M) funds the 30 Cities Program for river cleanup. The organisation's market relevance to fashion and product design is limited to its 2020 sunglasses — a design-led consumer object that demonstrated a viable supply chain for ocean plastic certification, manufacture, and retail. The DNV-GL certification system for recovered ocean plastic (launched June 2020) enables broader material supply chains for brands seeking certified ocean plastic.
