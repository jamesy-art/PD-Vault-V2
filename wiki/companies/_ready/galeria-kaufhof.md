# Galeria Kaufhof

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/galeria-kaufhof/profile/galeria-kaufhof-logo.svg)

## Overview

Galeria Kaufhof GmbH was one of Germany's two major department store chains, headquartered in Cologne. A member of the International Association of Department Stores from 1930 to 2010, the business passed through several ownership structures: Metro AG subsidiary until September 2015, then acquired by Hudson's Bay Company, then Signa Holding. In March 2019, Galeria Kaufhof merged with its largest competitor Karstadt to form Galeria Karstadt Kaufhof, based in Essen and operating under galeria.de.

No in-house design direction is documented in the source material. Galeria Kaufhof operated as a multi-brand retailer rather than a design-led own-label business.

## History

97 Galeria Kaufhof branches in Germany at the point of merger. Subsidiary operations included DINEA Gastronomie GmbH (58 restaurants) and Galeria Inno (16 stores in Belgium). The company was a subsidiary of Metro AG until 30 September 2015, when Hudson's Bay Company announced its acquisition. In June 2019 Signa Holding acquired all shares as part of the Karstadt merger process. Galeria Karstadt Kaufhof launched 25 March 2019 with a new brand identity and website.

## Products

## Services

Operated as a traditional brick-and-mortar department store retailer with multi-brand stock. Post-merger, Galeria Karstadt Kaufhof continued operating under the galeria.de platform. The Hudson's Bay Company ownership period (2015–2019) connected the business to the HBC group's broader North American and European portfolio.

## Markets

One of two dominant department store operators in Germany (alongside Karstadt, later merged). At 97 branches, Galeria Kaufhof had significant national retail footprint across German cities. The Galeria Inno subsidiary extended the group's presence into Belgium with 16 stores.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

German department stores of Galeria Kaufhof's scale are significant distribution channels for fashion brands whose output includes print and pattern — from branded fashion and accessories to home textiles and gifting. The department store format concentrates multiple brand adjacencies and seasonal fashion buying under one roof, making chains like this a high-volume route to market for pattern-driven product categories.

The Galeria Inno Belgian network extends this reach across a further Western European market.
