# LVMH

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/lvmh/profile/lvmh-logo.jpg)

## Overview

LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE is a French multinational luxury holding company headquartered in the 8th arrondissement of Paris. Formed in 1987 by the merger of fashion house Louis Vuitton (founded 1854) and Moët Hennessy (a 1971 combination of Moët & Chandon champagne and Hennessy cognac), LVMH controls approximately 60 subsidiaries managing 75 luxury brands. Bernard Arnault has been chairman and CEO since January 1989. In April 2023, LVMH became the first European company to surpass a $500 billion valuation. 2024 revenue: €84.68 billion (1% organic growth); 2025 revenue: €80.8 billion (−4% H1, with Fashion and Leather Goods sales down 12%). Approximately 215,000 employees (2025). Listed on Euronext Paris; component of the CAC 40.

Each LVMH subsidiary operates independently with its own creative direction. LVMH does not impose a group design identity. Key creative appointments at the fashion houses are managed at the house level. Notable: the appointment of Rihanna to head Fenty (2019, first woman of colour to lead an LVMH house) and the Armani succession stake (2025) represent strategic brand additions rather than design direction changes. The Fondation Louis Vuitton (Frank Gehry building, Bois de Boulogne, Paris, opened 2014) functions as LVMH's cultural institution for art collection and major art exhibitions.

## History

Bernard Arnault seized control of LVMH in the late 1980s following an invitation to invest by Henry Racamier (then head of Louis Vuitton). By end of 2017, the Arnault Family Group held 46.84% of LVMH stock and 63.13% of voting rights, following the €13.1 billion buyout of all remaining Christian Dior SE shares. Louis Vuitton CEO Pietro Beccari was appointed head of LVMH's fashion division in December 2025. Stéphane Bianchi was appointed Group Managing Director in 2024, overseeing strategic and operational management.

Key acquisitions: Bulgari ($5.2B, 2011); Tiffany & Co. ($16B, completed January 2021); Barton Perreira eyewear ($80M, November 2023); L'Epée 1839 Swiss clocks (June 2024). Additional investments: Aimé Leon Dore minority stake (January 2022, via LVMH Luxury Ventures); Pedemonte Group jewellery (November 2022); Château Minuty rosé (2023); La Joux-Perret watchmaker minority stake (November 2025). September 2025: Giorgio Armani's will named LVMH as preferred buyer of up to 54.9% of Armani over three to five years (initial 15% stake within 18 months of September 2025).

Fenty (Rihanna): launched by LVMH in Paris, May 2019 — the first new LVMH fashion house in 32 years and the first headed by a woman of colour. Stella McCartney partnership announced July 2019.

## Products

Six operating divisions:

1. **Fashion and Leather Goods**: Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior Couture, Fendi, Celine, Kenzo, Loewe, Givenchy, Marc Jacobs, Loro Piana, Emilio Pucci, Berluti, JW Anderson, Moynat, Patou, Rimowa, Barton Perreira, Kapital, Vuarnet; planned Armani stake. Selective retailing: Le Bon Marché, La Samaritaine, DFS, 24S.
2. **Wines and Spirits**: Moët & Chandon, Dom Pérignon, Hennessy, Veuve Clicquot, Krug, Ruinart, Château d'Yquem, and others.
3. **Perfumes and Cosmetics**: Sephora, Parfums Christian Dior, Guerlain, Givenchy Parfums, Fenty Beauty, Make Up For Ever, Benefit Cosmetics, Kenzo Parfums, Maison Francis Kurkdjian, Officine Universelle Buly, and others.
4. **Watches and Jewelry**: TAG Heuer, Bulgari, Tiffany & Co., Chaumet, Hublot, Zenith, Repossi, Fred.
5. **Selective Retailing**: DFS, Le Bon Marché, La Grande Épicerie, La Samaritaine, 24S.
6. **Other Activities**: Belmond (luxury hospitality), Bulgari Hotels and Resorts, Maisons Cheval Blanc, Les Echos-Le Parisien Group (media: *Les Echos*, *Le Parisien*, *Paris Match*, Radio Classique).

## Services

LVMH's fashion and leather goods brands operate primarily through own-retail: company boutiques, department store concessions, and proprietary e-commerce. Selective retail includes Le Bon Marché, La Samaritaine, and DFS duty-free. 24S is the group's multi-brand luxury e-commerce platform. Lyst received LVMH investment in May 2018 ($60M round) to support luxury e-commerce search for younger audiences. Alibaba partnership expanded May 2024 for Chinese market presence. Journées Particulières (biennial open house across LVMH ateliers) attracted 145,000 visitors in 2017 across France, Italy, Switzerland, UK, Netherlands, Spain, and Germany.

## Markets

World's largest luxury goods company by revenue. Ranked 47th in Forbes Global 2000 (2023). Chief markets: Asia ex-Japan (27.5% of 2024 sales), United States (25.4%), Europe ex-France (17.2%), Japan (8.8%), France (8.3%). First surpassed Hermès as most valuable European luxury group by valuation (April 2023; Hermès later reclaimed this position). The brand portfolio spans accessible luxury (Sephora, Loro Piana accessible ranges) through ultra-high-end (Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior Couture, Bulgari) across fashion, leather goods, watches, jewellery, perfume, and retail.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

LVMH is the holding company for several of the most commercially significant surface pattern identities in global luxury fashion and accessories:

**Louis Vuitton monogram**: The LV interlocking monogram on toile canvas is one of the most globally reproduced and legally protected graphic surface patterns in luxury goods. Applied across handbags, luggage, accessories, and apparel. The pattern's licensing and enforcement is central to Louis Vuitton's commercial identity.

**Emilio Pucci**: LVMH's most directly print-identified fashion house. Pucci is internationally known for kaleidoscopic, geometric, and abstract multicolour prints applied to women's RTW, swimwear, and accessories — a surface pattern identity as defining as any brand in the luxury sector.

**Kenzo**: Kenzo Takada's founding identity was built on bold botanical prints, floral all-overs, and graphic nature-inspired patterns in women's and men's RTW. Under LVMH ownership, Kenzo has continued as a print-forward house. Kenzo's collaboration with H&M (November 2016) brought the brand's bold graphic print vocabulary to mass retail.

**Fendi**: The Fendi double-F "Zucca" pattern on canvas is a documented luxury surface pattern identity, applied to handbags and leather accessories at significant commercial scale. Fendi also operates one of the luxury sector's most distinctive fur surface design programmes.

**Christian Dior Couture**: Dior's couture and RTW tradition includes significant floral and abstract print directions across silk, cotton, and jacquard textiles. The Dior bar jacket silhouette with its structured fabric surface and seasonal print iterations is a recurring design signal.

**Louis Vuitton artist collaborations**: Louis Vuitton has an extensive documented history of collaborating with artists on bag surface design (Takashi Murakami's monogram multicolore; Yayoi Kusama polka dot patterns; others). These are not documented in the immediate source material but are contextually established in the Louis Vuitton subsidiary brand.

**LVMH Young Fashion Designer Prize (from 2013)**: €300,000 grant with a year of mentoring. Documented winners include Thomas Tait (2014), Marques'Almeida (2015), and Thebe Magugu (2019, first African winner). The prize supports emerging fashion designers — including those working in print and surface design. LVMH also supports ANDAM (France), the Hyères International Festival of Fashion and Photography, and a scholarship programme at Central Saint Martins.
