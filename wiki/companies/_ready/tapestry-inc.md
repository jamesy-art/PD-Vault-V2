## Overview

Tapestry, Inc. is an American holding company based in New York City and the parent of Coach New York and Kate Spade New York. The business originated as Coach, founded in 1941 by Miles and Lillian Cahn as a small Manhattan workshop producing leather goods. As Coach grew into a company with over 17,000 employees, it sought to broaden its portfolio and acquired Kate Spade New York in 2017 for $2.4 billion and Stuart Weitzman in 2015 for $574M. To reflect this expanded multi-brand identity, Coach, Inc. officially changed its name and NYSE ticker from COH to Tapestry/TPR on October 31, 2017. In late July 2020, CFO Joanne Crevoiserat was appointed CEO following the resignation of Jide Zeitlin (amid personal misconduct allegations); Crevoiserat became the 36th female to lead a Fortune 500 company. In August 2023 Tapestry announced a planned $8.5B acquisition of Capri Holdings (Versace, Michael Kors, Jimmy Choo); the FTC sued to block the deal in April 2024, a federal judge upheld the block on October 24, 2024, and Tapestry called off the merger in November 2024. Stuart Weitzman was sold to Calares Inc. on August 4, 2025, leaving Tapestry with two brands: Coach and Kate Spade. ## Markets

Tapestry operates at the affordable luxury end of the US fashion market — a segment between mass-market and European luxury. Coach is the flagship brand, generating the majority of Tapestry's revenue; Coach handbags, in particular, are positioned as entry-level luxury leather goods for the North American and Asian consumer. Kate Spade serves a younger, more lifestyle-oriented female customer. Tapestry's attempted acquisition of Capri Holdings in 2023–2024 — blocked by the FTC on competition grounds in the handbag market — confirmed that Tapestry is considered a dominant player in the "accessible handbag" segment where Coach and Michael Kors compete directly.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Tapestry's portfolio brands are significant surface pattern design consumers and commissioners:

- **Coach New York**: Coach's leather goods and apparel lines have historically featured strong signature patterns — the Coach monogram (interlocking C pattern), reissued vintage Coach floral and turnlock prints, seasonal colour-block and printed canvas patterns. The Coach monogram is one of the most widely recognised branded surface pattern identities in US fashion
- **Kate Spade New York**: Kate Spade is known for bold, graphic, and playful surface design — polka dots, stripes, florals, novelty prints, and illustrated patterns applied across handbags, accessories, stationery, homeware, and apparel. Surface pattern design is a core brand expression for Kate Spade across multiple product categories
- **Leather goods surface design**: Both Coach and Kate Spade produce coated canvas and printed leather goods — the fabric surface (coated canvas, printed satin, embossed leather) carries the brand's pattern identity on its highest-volume product categories
- **Stationery and home (Kate Spade)**: Kate Spade's extension into stationery and home products represents a direct entry into pattern-heavy consumer categories where printed surface is the primary design value
- **Brand identity and logo pattern**: The Tapestry corporate rebrand (from Coach) implicitly references the weaving and surface complexity of tapestry fabric — a metaphor for multi-brand integration that also resonates with textile and pattern design
