## Overview

New Look is a British fashion retailer selling womenswear, menswear, and clothing for teens through a chain of high street shops. Founded in 1969 by Tom Singh in Taunton, Somerset, the company expanded from a single UK store into a chain that at its peak operated over 900 shops internationally. International presence at various points included Ireland (from 2003), Belgium (2006), France, Netherlands, Romania, Malaysia, South Korea, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, UAE, China, Germany, Russia, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Azerbaijan, and Poland; the company employed over 18,000 staff and recorded a turnover of £1,147 million with profits of £180 million in 2008. In 2015, New Look was acquired by South African investment company Brait SE for £780 million; at the time it operated 852 stores globally, including 94 in China. A restructuring in 2018 closed 60 UK shops and withdrew from most international markets. In 2020, COVID-19 prompted a Chapter 15 bankruptcy filing in the US. By February 2025, the company exited the Irish market after the High Court of Ireland appointed provisional liquidators to its Irish operations.

## History

Tom Singh founded New Look in 1969. In 2004, the company was taken private by founder Tom Singh, CEO Phil Wrigley, and private equity investors Permira, Apax Partners, and Quillian Investments. It continued public company status until this delisting; Brait SE (South Africa) subsequently acquired the business for £780 million in May 2015.

In June 2017, New Look launched the "My Life My Style My Look" campaign and announced British model Georgie Aldous — the first male model in full make-up — as a campaign face, described as a history-making fashion campaign choice. Prior to this, the company opened its largest store to date in Liverpool One Shopping Centre (2008); its three-hundredth store at Glasgow Fort Shopping Park (July 2010, 18,000 sq ft); and the largest New Look store in the world at Jervis Shopping Centre, Dublin (November 4, 2010). A 2016 Reuters report noted New Look was seeking a "fashion edge in China with local designs" — a documented localisation strategy in the Chinese market. The company's China business peaked at 94 stores (2015); international market withdrawal began in 2018.

## Services

Own-retail model: own-brand clothing sold exclusively through New Look stores and website. No outbound licensing programme documented. The company's international expansion (900+ stores) was a wholly owned or co-operated retail model. Brait SE acquisition (2015) brought South African investment into a British-owned retail chain. The 2018 withdrawal from international markets and 2020 US bankruptcy filing represent the company's financial contraction. As of 2025, Ireland exit is confirmed; UK operations continue.

## Markets

Value-to-mid-market British fashion retailer. At peak (2015): 852 stores globally, 94 in China, presence across 20+ countries. Post-2018 restructuring: primarily UK-focused. Turnover £1,147M and profit £180M in 2008 reflect significant scale at expansion peak. The company has historically competed in the accessible womenswear market with Primark, H&M, and Zara. The 2017 "My Life My Style My Look" campaign signalled a push toward fashion identity positioning (self-expression as core value) rather than pure price-led messaging.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

New Look operates in the womenswear segment of fast fashion, which is the primary mass-market distribution channel for surface pattern design in clothing:

**Womenswear as pattern design field**: Womenswear at the value-to-mid-market level is the category where printed fabric, floral motifs, graphic prints, and seasonal colourways have the highest volume and widest reach. As a chain with 900+ stores at peak, New Look is a significant distribution channel for printed textiles applied to clothing.

**Local design strategy in China (2016)**: Reuters reported in October 2016 that New Look was "seeking fashion edge in China with local designs" — a documented intent to adapt clothing design direction to local market aesthetic preferences. This confirms that design direction (including print and pattern choices) was a strategic variable in market positioning.

**"My Life My Style My Look" (2017)**: The campaign's explicit theme of personal style and self-expression aligns with fashion as individual identity — which is the consumer-facing language of surface pattern design's role in clothing (personal aesthetic choice through print, colour, and motif selection).

**Teen and young women's fashion**: The documented clothing-for-teens category is one of the most graphic and print-intensive product categories in mass-market fashion — logo graphics, novelty prints, bold colourways, and trend-driven motifs are foundational to this segment.
