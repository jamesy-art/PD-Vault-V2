# Deckers Brands

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/deckers-brands/profile/deckers-brands-logo.jpeg)

## Overview

Deckers Brands (formally Deckers Outdoor Corporation) is an American footwear designer and distributor founded in 1973 and based in Goleta, California. The company was co-founded by Karl F. Lopker, who began making and selling flip-flops at West Coast craft fairs in 1973 under the name "Styled Steer," and Doug Otto, who encountered the word "deckas" (Hawaiian slang for their sandals' striped layered sole construction) while on business in Hawaii in 1975. The name Deckers was adopted based on this visual description of the product's layered surface structure. The company went public on NASDAQ in 1993. Its current brand portfolio consists of UGG (acquired 1995), Hoka (acquired 2013), and Teva (licensing agreement 1985; full patent and trademark acquisition 2002). Koolaburra, acquired 2015, operates under the UGG brand.

No named head of design or creative director documented in source material across the Deckers portfolio. Product direction is brand-specific: UGG centres on material selection (sheepskin sourcing, tanning, colour palette), Hoka on biomechanical construction and colourway, and Teva on strap hardware and webbing design. Portfolio acquisition strategy (Teva, Hoka, Sanuk, Koolaburra) indicates brand-building through acquisition rather than internal creative development.

## History

Deckers' growth from craft-fair sandal sales to a significant publicly traded footwear company is driven primarily by the UGG brand, acquired in 1995 from UGG Holdings, Inc. — the company founded by surfer Brian Smith in 1979 to bring Australian sheepskin boots to the US market. UGG broke into mainstream consumer culture when featured on The Oprah Winfrey Show's Favorite Things segment in 2003. Deckers is described as a niche market supplier due to heavy revenue concentration in UGG. Teva, invented by geophysicist Mark Thatcher in 1982 — who attached a nylon ankle strap to a thong sandal to create the first sports sandal for river activities — was first licensed to Deckers in 1985 and fully acquired in 2002, including US patent #4,793,075. Hoka One One, founded in 2009 in Annecy, France and known for its maximalist (oversized midsole) running shoes, was acquired in 2013. Sanuk (acquired 2011 for $120 million) was divested to Canadian company Lolë in 2024. MOZO Shoes (culinary footwear, acquired 2010) was sold in July 2015.

## Products

**UGG**: sheepskin upper boots with wool inner lining and tanned outer surface; footwear for men and women in fashion and lifestyle contexts. Koolaburra positioned as entry-level complement under the UGG brand umbrella.

**Hoka**: running shoes with oversized midsoles ("maximalist" construction); athletic and lifestyle footwear; sponsored professional runners and running competitions.

**Teva**: outdoor sports sandals; Universal Strapping System with heel strap, side strap, and triangular connector rings; dual-layer sole with arch support and Microban Zinc antibacterial treatment.

## Services

Deckers began its Teva relationship through a licensing agreement in 1985 before acquiring full patent and trademark rights in 2002 — an acquire-via-licensing model. ## Markets

Deckers operates across three distinct market positions: UGG in fashion and lifestyle casual (sheepskin boots as aspirational lifestyle product), Hoka in performance running and athletic footwear, and Teva in outdoor and adventure sandals. Revenue is heavily weighted toward UGG. UGG's mainstream cultural breakthrough in 2003 and sustained commercial relevance establish it as one of the most commercially successful footwear brand acquisitions in recent US fashion history. Hoka's maximalist running shoe positioning, initially focused on trail and ultra-running, has broadened into mainstream athletic and lifestyle footwear.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Deckers' surface pattern design relevance is concentrated in material surface identity and footwear surface construction:

- **Brand name derived from a surface description**: The name "Deckers" originates from Hawaiian slang for the sandals' "striped layered construction" — the visual appearance of the layered sole creating a stacked, striped cross-section resembling a deck of stacked wood. The company was named after a surface pattern observation. This is a direct surface design origin story: a product's visual material structure — layered stripes — became the brand identity.
- **UGG — material surface as design identity**: UGG boots' design language is almost entirely a material surface proposition. The sheepskin upper — with its tanned outer surface and wool fleece lining visible at the cuff — is the product's defining design element. No graphic print pattern is employed; instead, the surface texture of the sheepskin (tanned, brushed, or sueded) in various colourways defines the aesthetic identity. Material surface treatment (tanning, dyeing, sueding, shearling versus smooth finish) is the primary surface design variable in UGG's product development.
- **Teva strap pattern system**: Teva sandals feature a strap construction with a recognisable visual surface geometry. The nylon webbing straps in their characteristic colours and patterns — connected by triangular rings in horizontal and diagonal arrangements — create a graphic visual structure across the foot. The woven and dyed nylon webbing is a direct application of surface colour and pattern to the sandal upper.
- **Hoka — graphic colour-blocking as product identity**: Hoka's maximalist running shoe design combines oversized midsoles with bold colour-blocking on upper and midsole surfaces. In athletic footwear, colour-blocking and graphic material combinations (mesh, synthetic, foam in contrasting hues) constitute the surface design language. Hoka's high-contrast colourways are central to its visual identity in the running shoe market.
- **Surf and outdoor culture aesthetic origins**: UGG's origin in surf culture (Brian Smith selling sheepskin boots to Californian surfers from 1979) and Teva's origin in river-guiding (Mark Thatcher in the Grand Canyon, 1982) draw from outdoor and coastal aesthetic traditions — engaging with colour palettes (ocean, sand, earth, trail) and material surface vocabularies (natural materials, technical synthetics, woven straps) specific to those cultures.
