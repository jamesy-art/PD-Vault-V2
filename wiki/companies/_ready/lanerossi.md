Italian textile brand established 1817 in Schio, Veneto by Francesco Rossi; historically one of Italy's largest wool producers; acquired by [[Marzotto Group]] in 1987; currently produces accessories and decorative fabrics under a Marzotto joint venture via licensee Filivivi.

## Overview

Lanerossi traces its origin to 1817 when Francesco Rossi established a wool lanificio (wool mill) in Schio, in the Veneto region of northern Italy. Under Alessandro Rossi (who inherited the business in 1849), the lanificio grew into one of Italy's major industrial enterprises. In 1873 it was incorporated as Lanificio Rossi s.p.a. and listed on the Milan Stock Exchange. By 1959, under president Giuseppe Eugenio Luraghi (appointed 1956 following a management crisis), the company generated approximately 23 billion lire in annual turnover with around 10,000 employees. The Italian state acquired a controlling interest through ENI during the 1960s. Marzotto Group acquired Lanerossi in 1987, resulting in delisting from the Milan Stock Exchange. The brand currently produces accessories and decorative fabrics through Filivivi, a joint venture with Marzotto Group.

Lanerossi's historical market position was as a major Italian industrial wool producer — among the largest wool employers in the Veneto at its 1959 peak (~10,000 employees, ~23 billion lire turnover). Post-Marzotto acquisition (1987) and under the Filivivi joint venture, the brand operates at a significantly reduced scale as a licensed accessories and decorative fabrics line, positioned within the Marzotto Group textile portfolio and the broader Italian heritage textiles sector.
