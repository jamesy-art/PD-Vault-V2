# Chanel

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/chanel/profile/chanel-logo.png)

## Overview

Chanel is a French luxury fashion house founded in 1910 by Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel, initially as a millinery shop in Paris. It is privately owned by French brothers Alain and Gérard Wertheimer through the holding company Chanel Limited, established in 2018 and headquartered in London. The house specialises in women's ready-to-wear, luxury goods, and accessories, and licences its brand name to Luxottica for eyewear. Karl Lagerfeld served as chief designer from 1983 until his death in February 2019; Virginie Viard succeeded him and served until June 2024; Matthieu Blazy (formerly creative director of Bottega Veneta) was appointed artistic director in December 2024. Leena Nair has served as Global CEO since December 2021. Worldwide, Chanel S.A. operates approximately 310 boutiques. Parfums Chanel — the fragrance and cosmetics division — is historically its most profitable business unit.

Coco Chanel's foundational design philosophy — simplicity, functionality, and the repurposing of masculine fabrics and forms for women — established an aesthetic vocabulary that Karl Lagerfeld maintained and evolved for 35 years (1983–2019). Lagerfeld retained tweed, gold chains, quilted leather, and the double-C monogram as signature design constants while reinventing seasonal references. Virginie Viard (2019–2024) continued in this register. Matthieu Blazy's appointment in December 2024 — his debut space-themed runway show in Paris (October 2025) was credited as a new direction — signals a potential shift away from Lagerfeld-era vocabulary. Design is anchored by the Paraffection ateliers, which supply embroidery, feathers, and ornamentation directly into couture collections.

## History

- **Founded:** 1910, Paris (Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel; first shop at 31 Rue Cambon)
- **Owner:** Alain and Gérard Wertheimer (Chanel Limited, est. 2018, HQ London)
- **Global CEO:** Leena Nair (from December 2021)
- **Artistic Director:** Matthieu Blazy (appointed December 2024; ex-Bottega Veneta)
- **Previous creative directors:** Karl Lagerfeld (1983 – February 2019); Virginie Viard (2019 – June 2024)
- **Boutiques:** ~310 worldwide (94 Asia, 70 Europe, 128 North America, 10 Middle East, others)
- **Key properties:** 400 North Rodeo Drive, Beverly Hills ($152M, 2015); Bond Street flagship, London (£310M, October 2020)
- **Subsidiaries/holdings:** Paraffection (artisanal ateliers, 1997/2002); Eres swimwear (1996); Orlebar Brown (2018); Holland & Holland (gun-makers, 1996); Bell & Ross watchmaker (2001); 43% stake in Cariaggi Lanificio cashmere (with Brunello Cucinelli, 2022)
- **Watch investments (as of 2024):** 25% MB&F; 20% F.P. Journe; undisclosed stakes in Romain Gauthier and Bell & Ross
- **Eyewear licence:** Luxottica (renewed 2019)
- **Fur and exotic skins ban:** announced December 2018

## Products

- **Ready-to-wear** — couture and prêt-à-porter; the Chanel Suit (tweed, braiding, chain hem weight) is the signature product
- **Handbags** — Chanel 2.55 (February 1955; quilted leather, shoulder strap); Classic Flap; Boy bag; quilted construction is the defining material identity
- **Fragrance** — No. 5 (1921); No. 19; Chance; Coco Mademoiselle; Coco Noir; Allure; extensive men's line; Olivier Polge as in-house perfumer (2015–present)
- **Cosmetics and skincare** — distributed through department stores worldwide; product lines include Sublimage, Le Lift, Hydra Beauty, N°1 de Chanel (97% naturally derived, 2022)
- **Fine jewellery** — High Jewellery since 1932; collections include Camellia, Comète, Coco Crush, Baroque, 1932
- **Watches** — Chanel J12 (ceramic, 2000); Première (1987); Matelassé (1995)
- **Eyewear** — licensed to Luxottica
- **Swimwear** — Eres (acquired 1996); Orlebar Brown (acquired 2018; men's tailored swim shorts)

## Services

Chanel operates approximately 310 boutiques globally through company-owned stores, with shops inside major department stores including Harrods, Selfridges, Bergdorf Goodman, Neiman Marcus, and Saks Fifth Avenue. Eyewear is licensed to Luxottica (renewed 2019). Fragrance and cosmetics are distributed through Chanel's own parfumerie and department store counters. Chanel maintains strict brand control — no broader fashion licensing programme is documented in the source. A 2018 lawsuit against The RealReal addressed counterfeit product distribution. The first US flagship dedicated to watches and fine jewellery opened on Fifth Avenue, New York City, in February 2024.

## Markets

Chanel occupies the top tier of global luxury fashion, competing with Hermès, Louis Vuitton, and Dior. Its most profitable division is Parfums Chanel. The house achieved net profit of €67M on €570M in ready-to-wear sales in 1994, making it the most profitable French fashion house that year; by the end of the 1980s the Wertheimer family fortune stood at US$5 billion. Chanel's pricing strategy is deliberately escalatory — the Classic Flap handbag rose from $220 at inception to over $10,000 in recent years — to reinforce exclusivity. Counterfeit goods are an active brand management challenge: Chanel operates a consumer-facing anti-counterfeit website and sued The RealReal in 2018 for hosting counterfeit products.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Chanel's history is deeply embedded in textile and surface design:

**Quilted fabric** — quilted construction is the defining material identity of Chanel leather goods (2.55 handbag, 1955) and some garments. The diamond-quilted pattern (matelassé) has become one of the most recognised surface patterns in luxury fashion, referenced and copied across the industry.

**Tweed** — the Chanel suit is defined by bouclé and tweed fabrication, with Karl Lagerfeld retaining and innovating the material through his 35-year tenure. Chanel is now sourcing eco-responsible tweeds as part of its Mission 1.5 Climate Action Plan — a supply chain intervention with direct surface pattern implications.

**Jersey** — Coco Chanel pioneered the use of jersey cloth in women's fashion during World War I, introducing a fabric previously used for men's underwear into couture. This represents a foundational textile-to-fashion transfer.

**Double-C interlocking monogram** — the interlocked opposed CC logotype, trademarked in the US in November 1924/February 1925, is used as a repeating surface pattern across accessories, hardware, and products. It is one of the most widely recognised monogram patterns in luxury.

**Camellia motif** — a recurring Chanel decorative motif across jewellery, accessories, and garments.

**Paraffection ateliers** — Chanel's subsidiary company (est. 1997, formalized 2002) consolidates artisanal workshops including: Lesage (embroidery — direct surface pattern work), Lemarié (feathers and flowers — applied surface decoration), Desrues (ornamentation and buttons), Massaro (shoemaking), Michel (millinery). These ateliers represent active surface pattern craft production under Chanel ownership.

**Camellia embroidery and beading** — used across haute couture collections; the beaded dress traditions of the 1920s (Flapper era) and current couture continue to place embroidery and bead work as surface pattern design elements.
