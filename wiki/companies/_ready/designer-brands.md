# Designer Brands

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/designer-brands/profile/designer-brands-logo.jpg)

## Overview

Designer Brands Inc. is an American company selling designer and name-brand shoes and fashion accessories, primarily through its Designer Shoe Warehouse (DSW) chain of 500+ US stores and an e-commerce website. The company was founded in 1969 as Shonac Corporation, operating as the shoe licensee for Value City department stores. The first DSW store opened in Dublin, Ohio in July 1991. In 1998, the company was acquired by Value City; in December 2004, by Retail Ventures (an affiliate). The company went public on the NYSE in June 2005. In March 2019, DSW Inc. was rebranded as Designer Brands Inc. (NYSE: DBI from April 2, 2019), reflecting the company's evolution from a single-banner shoe warehouse chain into a multi-brand footwear business with own private-label brands. The company also owns private-label footwear brands Audrey Brooke, Kelly & Katie, Lulu Townsend, and Poppie Jones.

No named head of design or creative director documented in source material. Private-label design function is implied by the ownership of Audrey Brooke, Kelly & Katie, Lulu Townsend, and Poppie Jones. The Jennifer Lopez collaboration (2020) indicates an external celebrity design partner model for headline product launches.

## History

Shonac Corporation's origins as a shoe licensee for Value City — a value-oriented department store group — established a commercial orientation toward broad-market footwear distribution at accessible price points. By 1999, the company operated 48 DSW stores; rapid expansion followed the 2005 IPO. In March 2005, a significant data security breach resulted in theft of customer data from 1.4 million credit card transactions, preceding the IPO filing by three months. The 2011 acquisition of Retail Ventures (its largest shareholder) simplified ownership. Expansion into Canada through Town Shoes (44% acquired 2014, full acquisition May 2018) proved unsuccessful; the chain was subsequently shuttered. A 2016 franchising agreement with Apparel Brands opened DSW stores in the Middle East, with the first opening in Oman in June 2017. A March 2017 deal with Under Armour brought athletic footwear to the store network. In February 2019, a nail salon concept tested within Columbus, Ohio stores was announced for wider rollout to 5 stores. The corporate rebrand to Designer Brands in March 2019 coincided with a structural orientation toward private-label brand ownership.

## Products

Designer and name-brand footwear for women, men, and children (children's shoes added July 2016). Fashion accessories. Private-label brands: Audrey Brooke, Kelly & Katie, Lulu Townsend, and Poppie Jones — all footwear brands developed and owned by Designer Brands.

## Services

NYSE listed (DBI from April 2, 2019; formerly DSW). 500+ US stores plus e-commerce (launched April 2008). Middle East franchise agreement with Apparel Brands (August 2016); Oman store opened June 2017. Canada: Town Shoes 44% stake acquired April 2014 (with DSW licensing agreement), full acquisition May 2018, subsequently shuttered. Ebuys acquired for $62.5 million in February 2016. Children's shoes added to inventory July 2016. Under Armour deal for distribution through DSW stores March 2017.

## Markets

Designer Brands occupies the mid-market US footwear retail segment — above pure discount (TJ Maxx, Rack) but below specialist luxury (Nordstrom, Saks). The DSW format — a wide-format warehouse store carrying multiple name brands plus own private-label lines — targets value-conscious consumers seeking recognised brand footwear at competitive prices. The company holds 500+ US stores with an e-commerce complement (launched April 2008). Under the Designer Brands corporate identity, the business has signalled an intent to move beyond pure retail distribution toward a brand-building platform capable of developing and owning footwear products under its own private-label names.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Designer Brands' surface pattern design relevance is concentrated in its private-label footwear design function and its role as a major commercial aggregator of footwear aesthetics:

- **Private-label footwear brands as in-house design output**: The four private-label brands — Audrey Brooke, Kelly & Katie, Lulu Townsend, and Poppie Jones — require seasonal product design including all the surface design decisions inherent in footwear: upper material selection (leather, synthetic, fabric), print and texture application, embellishment, colour palette, hardware details, and sole design. Owning and developing these brands means Designer Brands operates a footwear design function, not merely a retail function. Footwear upper design is a surface design discipline: material choices, texture, print, embossing, stitching, and colour-blocking on the upper surface define the product's aesthetic identity.
- **Jennifer Lopez collaboration — celebrity-directed footwear design**: The March 2020 Jennifer Lopez line represents a documented celebrity design collaboration. The line was redesigned during the COVID-19 pandemic to incorporate sneakers and comfort shoes as fashion trends shifted toward remote-work footwear — a direct example of commercial footwear design decision-making in response to rapid lifestyle and trend shifts.
- **Multi-brand retail curation as surface design aggregation**: DSW's core business model — selecting and curating footwear from multiple brands for a single retail environment — constitutes a macro-level surface design curation function. Buying decisions at DSW determine which footwear surface design aesthetics reach the mid-market consumer at scale. With 500+ stores and a broad national footprint, DSW is a significant commercial gateway for footwear surface design trends reaching mainstream American consumers.
- **Under Armour distribution deal (2017)**: The 2017 deal to carry Under Armour shoes extended the company's athletic footwear curation, a category where colour-blocking and technical surface graphics are primary design variables.
- **Nail salon concept (2019)**: The in-store nail salon testing — a service that applies surface colour and pattern to fingernails and toenails — is a literal surface design service embedded within footwear retail, targeting millennial women as described in contemporary coverage.
