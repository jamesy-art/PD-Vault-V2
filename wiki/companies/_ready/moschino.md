## Overview

Moschino was founded in Milan in 1983 by Franco Moschino (1950–1994) under the holding company Moonshadow. Moschino studied at Accademia di Belle Arti in Milan (1968–1971), freelanced as a design sketcher for Versace (1971–1977), and designed for Italian house Cadette until 1982. He launched "Moschino Couture!" in 1983, building a reputation for colourful, witty, and provocative designs. Franco died of HIV/AIDS-related causes in September 1994. Rossella Jardini served as creative director 1994–2013. Moschino joined [[raw/companies processed/Aeffe]] S.p.A. in 1999; [[raw/companies processed/Aeffe]] gained full control in July 2021 by acquiring the remaining 30% stake for €66.6M ($78.51M). Jeremy Scott was creative director October 2013–March 2023. Davide Renne was appointed in October 2023 and died in November 2023 at age 46. ## Market Position

Moschino operates in Italian luxury fashion as a campy, Pop art-inflected house with strong cultural visibility through celebrity dressing. By the late 1980s US sales accounted for 15–20% of business. Jeremy Scott's tenure (2013–2023) significantly elevated global visibility through culturally provocative collections (McDonald's Fall 2014, Sims 4 collaboration 2019, Barbie at Met Gala 2019) and mass-market partnerships (H&M 2018). ## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Moschino's entire creative identity is built on graphic, Pop art-derived, and provocative surface design applied to garments and accessories:

- **Pop art print and graphic surface**: Roy Lichtenstein-inspired suit incorporating Pop art imagery; "Channel No. 5" TV t-shirt (Chanel lawsuit) — both are early examples of graphic image application as garment surface design, using appropriated commercial imagery as pattern
- **McDonald's-inspired collection (Fall 2014, Jeremy Scott)**: Brand packaging, arches, and McDonald's graphic language applied directly to clothing surfaces — one of the most widely circulated examples of commercial brand identity repurposed as fashion surface pattern
- **Sims 4/EA Games collaboration (2019)**: Pixelated illusion-effect clothing inspired by the computer game's pixelated aesthetic — digital image surface technique applied to garment surfaces; a documented intersection of game graphics and fashion print design
- **Chandelier gown for Katy Perry (Met Gala 2019, Camp theme)**: Sculptural surface construction — the garment's surface is the design concept
- **Barbie concept for Kacey Musgraves (Met Gala 2019)**: Life-size Barbie reproduction including packaging visual language translated to fashion surface
- **Swarovski crystal embellishment (Madonna Rebel Heart Tour 2015)**: Crystal surface decoration on a performance garment
- **Graffiti lawsuit (2015–2016)**: New York artist Rime claimed the Moschino dress worn by Katy Perry at the 2015 Met Gala copied his mural work — direct case of alleged surface pattern appropriation from street art; settled 2016
- **Love Moschino diffusion line**: Carries the brand's graphic/pop surface language into the contemporary segment

## Design Direction

Franco Moschino established a campy, socially aware aesthetic that incorporated Pop art, social criticism of the fashion industry, and witty graphic surface provocation. Rossella Jardini maintained the brand's eccentricity. Jeremy Scott (2013–2023) amplified the Pop art direction into cultural spectacle — McDonald's, The Sims, Barbie — making graphic surface appropriation the brand's most visible output. Adrian Appiolaza (from January 2024) brings a Loewe-trained, CSM-educated background; his direction is documented but not yet detailed in this source.
