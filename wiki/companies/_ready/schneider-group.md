## Overview

The Schneider Group was founded in 1922 by Giovanni Schneider, a young wool buyer, in Sydney, Australia. Over the following decades — particularly after World War II — the company grew into an international business focused on wool top production, shifting production in-house to create a vertically integrated supply chain. The Group is headquartered in Italy, with processing mills in Italy and Egypt and sourcing offices and farm networks in Australia, New Zealand, South America, and South Africa. The Schneider Group describes itself as "a world leader in the sourcing, processing and supply of high-quality wool" for the global fashion and outdoor industries. ## Products

**Wool tops**: combed wool ready for spinning into yarn; the primary product. Range: 10.5 to 30+ microns; capacity 10,000 tonnes per year.
**Open tops**: wool processed to an open structure suitable for specialist spinning.
**Speciality fibres**: includes cashmere, vicuna (with a dehairing plant), and other animal fibres; capacity 250+ tonnes per year.

**Certifications**: GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard), RWS (Responsible Wool Standard), OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100.

**Authentico®**: Schneider Group's proprietary wool traceability brand — a verification system tracing wool from farms to delivered tops. Ensures provenance and responsible sourcing claims for fashion clients.

**Raw material sourcing regions**: Australia, New Zealand, South America (Argentina, Uruguay), South Africa.
**Processing locations**: Italy (directly owned mill), Egypt (directly owned mill).

## Markets

The Schneider Group operates as a B2B raw material supplier to the global fashion (luxury, premium, contemporary) and outdoor industries. It occupies the upstream supply chain position as a primary processor of animal fibres — between the farm and the yarn/fabric mill. Its claimed position is "most trusted source for responsibly and beautifully made animal fibre tops." Clients are yarn spinners, fabric weavers, and apparel manufacturers who require premium, certified wool and speciality fibre inputs. The Group's supply range covers 10.5 to 30+ microns across wool and speciality fibres — spanning super-fine merino to coarser wool grades. As of 2017, the Group had been operating for 95+ years; as of 2025, over a century. The 2025 acquisition by G. Modiano Ltd (a major wool and raw material trading group) consolidates the Schneider Group within an integrated raw wool trading structure.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

The Schneider Group operates at the raw material stage of the textile supply chain — upstream of fabric manufacturing and far upstream of surface pattern design. Its direct SPD relevance is through the premium fibre inputs it supplies to the luxury and premium fashion textile supply chain:

- **Wool tops as the feedstock for luxury woven and knitted textile SPD**: Luxury woollen and worsted fabrics used by high-fashion brands for suiting, knitwear, and womenswear carry repeating woven surface patterns (houndstooth, herringbone, Prince of Wales check, tweed, jacquard) — these surface structures begin with the quality of the combed wool tops supplied by processors like the Schneider Group. The fibre micron count and processing quality directly influence the drape, texture, and surface legibility of finished woven patterns
- **Cashmere and speciality fibre for luxury knitwear**: Cashmere knitwear surface — colour, stitch, and fairisle/intarsia pattern in luxury knitted garments — depends on premium cashmere tops input. The Schneider Group supplies cashmere as part of its speciality fibre range
- **Certified fibres for sustainable luxury**: GOTS and RWS certifications allow fashion brands with sustainability commitments to make verified claims about the fibre origins of their woven and knitted surface patterns
- **Vicuna processing**: Vicuna is among the world's rarest and most expensive animal fibres, used in ultra-luxury outerwear and accessories; the Schneider Group's vicuna dehairing capability positions it in the most exclusive segment of the luxury fibre supply chain
