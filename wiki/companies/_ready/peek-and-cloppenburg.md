## Overview

Peek & Cloppenburg is an international chain of fashion and clothing stores originating in Germany. It is operated by two separate and legally independent companies: Peek & Cloppenburg KG Düsseldorf (P&C West) and Peek & Cloppenburg KG Hamburg (P&C North). Both entities sell designer and own-brand fashion for men, women, teens, and children across multiple European markets. P&C Düsseldorf operates through a Vienna-based subsidiary extending its footprint into twelve Central and Eastern European countries. In 2021, P&C Düsseldorf acquired Danish department store chain Magasin du Nord from British retailer Debenhams for £120 million.

Own-brand design direction for McNeal and Review is not publicly documented. The flagship store architectural programme — Richard Meier (Düsseldorf), Gottfried Böhm (Berlin), Christoph Ingenhoven (Lübeck) — reflects investment in retail environment design as a brand expression. No published aesthetic direction or design manifesto is available from source material.

## History

The business was founded in 1869 in Rotterdam by Johann Theodor Peek (1845–1907) and Heinrich Anton Adolph Cloppenburg (1844–1922), both from the Cloppenburg area of Lower Saxony. The Düsseldorf entity was founded on 20 April 1900 and opened its first German store in 1901 on Schadowstrasse, Düsseldorf. James Cloppenburg, son of founder Heinrich, opened the second store in Berlin the same year. The family split into two companies when Anton Cloppenburg — who had married Johann Theodor Peek's daughter — founded a separate private partnership in Hamburg in 1911; this Hamburg entity trades independently under the same name to this day.

Peek & Cloppenburg introduced the uniform size system in menswear at its earliest stores — an early retail standardisation innovation. The Düsseldorf flagship was rebuilt for the company's 100th anniversary with a new building designed by New York architect Richard Meier (white façade, 14,200 sq m sales area). Later flagship developments include stores by architects Gottfried Böhm (Berlin, Tauentzienstrasse) and Christoph Ingenhoven (Lübeck).

## Products

Men's, women's, teen, and children's fashion across designer and own-brand labels. Own brands include:

- **McNeal** — men's fashion; associated with P&C Düsseldorf; sold in P&C stores, competitors' stores, and standalone shops in Austria, Croatia, and Germany.
- **Review** — young fashion for men and women; associated with P&C Düsseldorf; distributed through the same channels as McNeal.

The 2021 acquisition of Magasin du Nord (Denmark's oldest department store chain, dating to 1868) added a multi-category retail operation in the Danish market.

## Services

Both P&C entities operate as private companies (no listed equity). P&C Düsseldorf runs 67 stores in Germany, 5 Netherlands, 2 Belgium, 2 Switzerland, and through its subsidiary Peek & Cloppenburg KG Wien, 43 stores across Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Serbia, and Slovenia. P&C Hamburg operates independently in northern Germany. Own brands McNeal and Review are distributed through P&C stores and also sold through external retail channels. The Magasin du Nord acquisition (2021) extends P&C Düsseldorf into Denmark.

## Markets

Peek & Cloppenburg operates at the mid-to-upper end of European fashion retail, positioned between mass-market chains and premium department stores. It is one of the largest fashion retail groups in German-speaking Europe, with 67 stores in Germany (P&C Düsseldorf alone), plus presence in Austria, Switzerland, Belgium, Netherlands, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Serbia, and Slovenia. The Hamburg company operates separately across northern Germany. Both companies carry designer brands alongside own-label fashion.

The own-brand labels McNeal and Review are internally designed for the mid-market and follow standard European fashion retail cycles that refresh seasonal print and pattern selections biannually.
