# G-III Apparel Group

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/g-iii-apparel-group/profile/g-iii-apparel-group-logo.svg)

## Overview

G-III Apparel Group is an American clothing company that designs, manufactures, markets, and sells women's and men's apparel across a global portfolio of licensed, owned, and private label brands. Headquartered in New York, the company was founded in the Garment District in 1956 by Aron Goldfarb as a leather outerwear business. His son Morris Goldfarb joined in 1972 and expanded sourcing, making G-III one of the first US companies to import outerwear from South Korea. Morris Goldfarb remains CEO. The company went public in 1989. Owned brands include Karl Lagerfeld, Donna Karan, DKNY, Vilebrequin, Sonia Rykiel, Andrew Marc, and Wilsons Leather. Licensed brands include Calvin Klein, Tommy Hilfiger, Nautica, Halston, Champion, Converse, Levi's, and Dockers, as well as the NFL, NBA, MLB, and NHL and 150+ US colleges.

G-III's model is production under licensor-directed creative briefs — Calvin Klein, Tommy Hilfiger, and Karl Lagerfeld each provide their own design direction, which G-III executes in manufacturing. For owned brands, internal design teams operate within established brand aesthetics. No named group-level creative director is documented. The Karl Lagerfeld brand's aesthetic codes have continued following the founder's death in 2019.

## History

G-III operates through wholesale (design, manufacture, and distribution of branded apparel to retailers) and retail (direct-to-consumer stores and digital channels) segments. The company sources and manufactures through global partners in China, Vietnam, Indonesia, India, Bangladesh, and Central and South America. International offices are maintained in Canada, China, Italy, France, the Netherlands, and Switzerland. Key owned brand acquisitions: Vilebrequin (luxury swimwear, 2012); G.H. Bass (footwear, 2013); Donna Karan International / DKNY (from LVMH, 2016, $650M); Karl Lagerfeld (joint venture 2015, full ownership 2022, $210M); Sonia Rykiel (2021). Licensed partnerships with Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger date to the 2005 acquisitions of Marvin Richards and Winlit. The company maintains over 30 brand relationships and licensing agreements with all four major US professional sports leagues.

## Products

- **Women's and men's outerwear** — G-III's original category; produced under Calvin Klein, Tommy Hilfiger, Kenneth Cole, Cole Haan, and others
- **Women's dresses and sportswear** — expanded post-2005; Eliza J and Jessica Howard brands (acquired 2007)
- **Swimwear** — Vilebrequin (owned luxury); Nautica (licensed)
- **Team outerwear** — NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, 150+ US colleges; under G-III Sports
- **Footwear** — G.H. Bass (owned)
- **Accessories** — under DKNY, Karl Lagerfeld, and other brands
- **Fragrance (sub-licensed)** — Inter Parfums holds fragrance licences for Donna Karan and DKNY

## Services

G-III licences major fashion and sports brands for manufacturing and distribution rights, typically under multi-year, product-category-specific, territory-defined agreements. Retail operations include Wilsons Leather outlet stores and Vilebrequin freestanding boutiques globally. The company also sub-licences its owned brands: Donna Karan and DKNY fragrances are licensed to Inter Parfums. The Citizen acquisition of Frédérique Constant and G-III's model are comparable in structure — both are examples of manufacturing-led companies managing licensed brand portfolios. G-III's deal to acquire Gap France is not related to Gap Inc. (USA); it relates to Go Sport's parent company Hermione People & Brands acquiring Gap France stores in 2022.

## Markets

G-III occupies a specialist position as a multi-brand manufacturing licensor in the US apparel wholesale market. Its core competency is design, production, and distribution under major licensed nameplates — a capital-efficient model that avoids the cost of originating brand equity. Owned brands (Karl Lagerfeld, DKNY, Vilebrequin) represent a strategic shift towards higher-margin brand ownership. Sports licensing, active since 1988 (NFL), provides a consistent revenue stream in team-branded outerwear and apparel. G-III distributes to department stores, specialty retailers, and e-commerce channels.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

G-III's portfolio is directly relevant to surface pattern and print across several major categories. Vilebrequin's commercial identity is built on distinctive printed swimwear fabrics — its patterns are among the most recognisable in luxury swimwear. DKNY and Karl Lagerfeld women's collections require seasonal print and surface design direction. Team outerwear (NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL) is a pattern-intensive product category: every garment carries applied team graphics, wordmarks, and visual identities across fabrications. Karl Lagerfeld as an owned brand has a documented history of graphic motifs applied across apparel and accessories. Donna Karan New York carries historical associations with abstract and minimalist surface treatments in New York fashion.
