# Capri Holdings

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/capri-holdings/profile/capri-holdings-logo.png)

## Overview

Capri Holdings Limited (formerly Michael Kors Holdings Limited) is a multinational fashion holding company incorporated in the British Virgin Islands, with executive offices in London and operational offices in New York. The company was founded in 1981 when Michael Kors launched his women's label at Bergdorf Goodman after the store's fashion director Dawn Mello noticed his work while he was serving as both designer and visual display head at a nearby boutique. Capri currently owns the Michael Kors brand and Jimmy Choo (acquired 2017, £897 million). The company previously owned Versace, which was sold to Prada on April 10, 2025 for €1.25 billion. Tapestry's $8.5 billion acquisition offer for Capri was blocked by a federal judge in October 2024 and formally terminated in November 2024 following an FTC antitrust challenge.

**Michael Kors brand**: Michael Kors' own design direction for his eponymous brand is characterised by American sportswear ease, accessible glamour, and jet-set references — a luxury sensibility at accessible price points. The monogram and MK logo accessories are the brand's most commercially distinctive surface design outputs.

**Jimmy Choo**: No named current creative director documented in source material. The brand is known for luxury footwear embellishment.

**Versace (formerly)**: Donatella Versace served as creative director through the Capri ownership period and into the Prada transition. Versace's creative direction is among the most print-forward in luxury fashion.

## History

Michael Kors enrolled at the Fashion Institute of Technology in 1977 but dropped out after nine months. Working at Lothar's boutique opposite Bergdorf Goodman on 57th Street, he became both designer and visual display head — a dual role that combined product design with retail presentation. Bergdorf's fashion director Dawn Mello spotted his work and connected him with Bergdorf's buyers, leading to the 1981 launch. In 1990, the company launched KORS Michael Kors as a licensee. A Chapter 11 filing in 1993 followed the closure of the KORS licensing partner; Kors designed for other labels before relaunching in 1997 alongside being named the first women's ready-to-wear designer at French house Celine — a position he held until October 2003. The MICHAEL Michael Kors line launched in 2004 (handbags, shoes, and women's RTW), following a $100 million majority investment from investors Lawrence Stroll and Silas Chou. John D. Idol was appointed CEO. The company went public on the NYSE in 2011. By 2014, annual revenue was $3.2 billion with net income of $670 million; by April 2017, 827 full-price or outlet stores and 133 licensed stores were operational. Jimmy Choo was acquired in July 2017 for £897 million. The Versace acquisition (announced September 2018, $2.1 billion) triggered the January 2019 renaming to Capri Holdings. The 2025 Versace sale to Prada and fiscal 2025 losses ($1.18 billion, revenues down 21% to $4.4 billion) mark the company's current post-Versace restructuring period.

## Products

**Michael Kors**: women's and men's ready-to-wear apparel, handbags, shoes, watches, jewellery, and accessories. Flagship product: the MICHAEL Michael Kors handbag and accessories line. Both full-price and outlet store formats.

**Jimmy Choo**: luxury footwear (women's and men's), handbags, small leather goods, and accessories. Embellished, metallic, and crystal-decorated product at the luxury price tier.

**Versace** (formerly): Italian luxury fashion house with full ready-to-wear, accessories, footwear, fragrance, and home goods; sold to Prada April 2025.

## Services

By April 2017, Capri operated 827 full-price or outlet stores and 133 licensed stores. Michael Kors launched as a licensee product in 1990 (KORS Michael Kors); licensing remains part of the business model across distribution. The company is publicly traded on the NYSE. The attempted Tapestry acquisition ($8.5 billion, announced August 2023) was blocked by a federal judge on October 24, 2024, following FTC suit filed April 2024 citing reduced competition in the affordable luxury handbag market; the deal was formally terminated November 2024. Versace was sold to Prada for €1.25 billion on April 10, 2025. Fiscal 2025 revenues fell 21% to $4.4 billion with a $1.18 billion operating loss.

## Markets

Capri Holdings positions across the accessible luxury (Michael Kors) and luxury (Jimmy Choo) segments. Michael Kors' core commercial product — the MK-monogram handbag — defines the brand's position in the "accessible luxury handbag" segment, the same category that the FTC cited as potentially subject to reduced competition in its challenge to the Tapestry acquisition. Jimmy Choo operates in the luxury footwear and accessories tier. The 2017 counterfeit enforcement action in Montreal (multiple raids across the Greater Montreal Area) illustrates the commercial value of Michael Kors' logo and pattern identity — sufficiently valuable to generate a significant counterfeiting network.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Capri Holdings' surface pattern design relevance is concentrated across the distinct design identities of its portfolio brands:

- **Michael Kors — logo monogram and accessible luxury graphics**: The MK monogram — the interlocking M and K logo motif applied as an all-over repeat pattern to handbags, wallets, scarves, and accessories — is one of the most widely sold branded surface pattern motifs in American accessible luxury fashion. The MK logo repeat is a direct surface design product: a graphic identity applied to leather, coated canvas, and textile surfaces as a branded pattern. The scale of the counterfeiting operation (multiple Montreal raids in 2015) is a measure of the commercial value of this specific logo pattern. Michael Kors' fur-free policy (adopted December 2017) extended across all company products reflects the brand's position in the mid-luxury goods supply chain.
- **Michael Kors as visual display head (early career)**: Before launching his label, Kors served as both designer and visual display head at Lothar's — an early integration of surface design (clothing) and environmental/retail display design. This dual role is relevant as it frames the brand's founder as someone who understood design as both product and presentation surface.
- **Michael Kors at Celine (1997–2003)**: Kors served as the first women's RTW designer at Celine, a French house, from 1997 to October 2003. This represents a significant commercial fashion design credit: six years of seasonal collection responsibility for a Parisian luxury house. The surface design decisions made during his Celine tenure — fabric, print, textile, colour — were among the most commercially visible in luxury fashion at the time.
- **Jimmy Choo — embellished surface design**: Jimmy Choo's product identity in luxury footwear and accessories is built on surface embellishment: metallic leathers, crystal and rhinestone applications, embroidery, printed exotic-skin textures, and distinctive material treatments are the primary design differentiators in the brand's product range. Footwear surface design at Jimmy Choo's price point is one of the most technically and aesthetically demanding commercial design categories.
- **Versace (formerly owned) — print-intensive luxury identity**: During Capri's ownership (2018–2025), Versace contributed the portfolio's most print-intensive design identity. Versace's design language under Gianni and then Donatella Versace is built on: the Baroque print (architectural ornamental motifs applied at large scale to silk), the Medusa head motif, the Greek key (meander) border pattern, the iconic Baroque print repeat that became one of the most recognisable luxury print signatures globally. Versace's bold graphic print identity — described in source as a planned expansion from 200 to 300 stores — brought a direct SPD-centred brand into the Capri portfolio for seven years.
