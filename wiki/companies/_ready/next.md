## Overview

Next plc is a British multinational clothing, footwear, and home products retailer headquartered in Enderby, England. The company operates approximately 700 stores — around 500 in the UK and 200 internationally across Europe, Asia, and the Middle East — and is listed on the London Stock Exchange as a FTSE 100 constituent. As of FY January 2026, Next reported revenue of £6.9 billion and net income of £898 million. Next became the largest UK clothing retailer by sales in 2012, overtaking Marks & Spencer, a position it confirmed again in 2014.

The company traces its origins to a Leeds tailor founded in 1864 by Joseph Hepworth. The Next brand was created in 1982 by designer-retailer George Davies (recruited by Terence Conran, then Chairman of Hepworth) to replace the Kendall's womenswear chain acquired in 1981. The brand formula — coordinated clothing ranges in an edited, designed environment — was expanded into menswear in 1984 and home furnishings shortly after. The Next Directory home shopping catalogue launched in 1988.

George Davies created the Next brand aesthetic in 1982 — coordinated clothing ranges in a minimal, modern retail environment, targeting womenswear at accessible mid-market prices. Davies's formula proved transformative for British fashion retail; he was later the founder of Per Una for M&S. No single named creative director has defined Next's design identity post-Davies. The company's current direction is commercially driven by sales performance and range planning. The acquisition of Cath Kidston, Joules, and Made.com signals an active strategy of acquiring fashion and home design brands with documented aesthetic identities rather than developing them internally.

## History

Joseph Hepworth founded Joseph Hepworth & Son as a tailor in Leeds in 1864. The company became a pioneer of chain store retail, with 100 outlets by 1884. In 1963, Hepworth brought in Savile Row designer Hardy Amies to revitalise its RTW suit collection. In 1981, Hepworth acquired Kendall & Sons (£1.75 million), giving it 600+ shops; George Davies was recruited to develop these into a new womenswear chain. The first Next shops opened on 12 February 1982.

Davies was appointed CEO in 1984; Hepworth stores were converted to Next format (extending to menswear); "Next Interiors" was introduced to selected stores for home furnishings. HQ moved from Leeds to Leicester in 1986 (closer to garment manufacturers). The company was renamed Next plc. The Next Directory launched in 1988, with Yasmin Le Bon modelling in the first catalogue. Davies was sacked in 1988 following over-expansion; 433 jewellery stores were sold to Ratners Group ($232M).

Key acquisitions: Lipsy (young women's fashion brand, 2008); Made.com (furniture/home products, November 2022 — in administration); Joules Group (joint venture with Tom Joule, December 2022 — in administration, £34 million, Tom Joule retaining 26%); Cath Kidston (March 2023 — brand name, domain names, IP only; £8.5 million; four stores closed); Russell & Bromley (British footwear retailer, January 2026 — pre-pack insolvency). Joint ventures: Victoria's Secret UK (stores and e-commerce); Gap UK (September 2021 — Gap concessions in selected Next stores; Next manages Gap UK website).

## Products

Clothing: women's, men's, children's (own-brand and third-party brands through Directory). Footwear (own-brand and from the Russell & Bromley acquisition). Home products: furniture, bedding, curtains, lighting, rugs, homeware (Next Home; expanded through Made.com acquisition). Beauty and fragrance (through Next Beauty).

Sub-brands and JVs: Lipsy (younger women's fashion, wholesale and retail); Victoria's Secret UK (stores and online, managed by Next); Gap UK (concessions and online, managed by Next); Joules (nature-inspired lifestyle clothing and accessories, joint venture since 2022); Cath Kidston (pattern-identified British consumer brand; IP owned by Next since 2023); Russell & Bromley (British footwear heritage brand, acquired January 2026).

Next clothing often carries "82" or "1982" as a graphic design feature — the founding year of the Next brand, used as a garment motif across ranges.

## Services

Own-retail (550+ UK stores); Next Directory (catalogue + website, 3M+ active customers); 180+ international stores; Victoria's Secret UK (franchise-style management); Gap UK (concession management). Cath Kidston IP acquired for £8.5M — the brand can be licensed or relaunched; no current Cath Kidston stores remain (four closed at time of acquisition). Joules brand operates through the joint venture structure with Tom Joule retaining 26%. Made.com brand IP and assets acquired. Russell & Bromley acquired via pre-pack January 2026.

## Markets

Largest UK clothing retailer by sales (since 2012; confirmed 2014). Revenue £6.9 billion (FY January 2026); net income £898 million. Online sales now exceed in-store revenue (£3.9 billion online vs £3.0 billion in stores, FY2026). The Next Directory — now an online catalogue with 3 million+ active customers — is a major UK multi-brand retail channel for clothing and home products. International stores (180+) extend reach across Europe, Asia, and the Middle East.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Next's SPD relevance spans several distinct areas:

**Cath Kidston brand IP (acquired March 2023)**: This is the most directly significant SPD acquisition. Cath Kidston is a British consumer brand specifically and internationally known for its floral, botanical, polka-dot, and vintage graphic print designs applied to bags, stationery, homeware, clothing, and accessories. The brand name, domain names, and intellectual property are now owned by Next — making Next the legal owner of a recognised British surface pattern brand and its associated print archive.

**Joules joint venture (December 2022)**: Joules is a British lifestyle brand associated with nature-inspired prints — floral, wildlife, and botanical motifs on clothing, home textiles, and accessories. The joint venture structure (Tom Joule retaining 26%) means Next controls the design direction of a documented nature-print brand.

**Made.com acquisition (November 2022)**: A design-led contemporary home furnishings retailer. Its acquisition extends Next's home category into curated interior products where surface design — upholstery fabric, cushion covers, rug patterns, wallpaper — is a primary product differentiator.

**Next Home and Next Directory**: Home furnishings have been part of the Next brand since the mid-1980s ("Next Interiors" sections in stores). The Next Directory — catalogues and online — is a major retail channel for home textiles including bedding, curtains, cushions, and rugs where surface pattern is the primary purchase driver for consumers.

**"1982" as applied graphic motif**: The documented use of "82" or "1982" as a design feature on Next clothing across all ranges confirms that graphic typography applied to garment surface is a consistent part of Next's brand design vocabulary.

**Lipsy (acquired 2008)**: Younger women's womenswear sub-brand; womenswear in this age bracket is a print- and graphic-intensive category.
