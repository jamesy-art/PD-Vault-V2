# TJX

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/tjx/profile/tjx-logo.jpeg)

## Overview

The TJX Companies, Inc. is an American multinational off-price department store corporation headquartered in Framingham, Massachusetts. The TJ Maxx concept was first announced by parent company Zayre Corp. in April 1977, with the first two stores opening simultaneously on May 19, 1977 in Auburn and Worcester, Massachusetts. TJX Companies was formally constituted as a Zayre subsidiary in June 1987; in 1989, Zayre Corp. merged into the subsidiary and renamed itself The TJX Companies, Inc., becoming the independent legal entity listed on the New York Stock Exchange. The company operates 4,557+ discount stores across nine countries through five operating divisions — Marmaxx (TJ Maxx and Marshalls, US), HomeGoods (HomeGoods and Homesense, US), Sierra (US), TJX Canada (Winners, HomeSense, and Marshalls), and TJX International (TK Maxx in the UK, Ireland, Poland, Austria, and the Netherlands; Homesense in the UK and Ireland). TJX ranked No. 80 on the 2024 Fortune 500 and carried a market capitalisation of approximately $170 billion in November 2025.

TJX has no in-house design direction or creative team for product design; all merchandise is sourced from third-party vendors. The company's commercial model centres on buying rather than designing. No creative director or design leadership is documented in the source.

## History

- April 1977: Zayre Corp. announces TJ Maxx concept — "family apparel, giftwares, and domestic goods at discount prices"
- May 19, 1977: First two TJ Maxx stores open in Auburn and Worcester, Massachusetts; third opens in Charlotte, North Carolina four days later
- June 1987: Zayre establishes The TJX Companies as subsidiary and holding company of TJ Maxx and Hit or Miss
- October 1988: Zayre Corp. sells ~400 Zayre stores to Ames Department Stores for $431.4M in cash, receivable note, and Ames preferred stock
- 1989: BJ's Wholesale Club and Home Club spun off (form Waban); Zayre Corp. merges into TJX, renaming itself The TJX Companies, Inc.; company lists on NYSE
- 1990: Acquired Winners (5-store Canadian chain); first international expansion
- 1992: HomeGoods launched in the United States
- 1994: T.K. Maxx launched in the United Kingdom; subsequently expanded into Ireland
- 1995: Acquired Marshalls chain from Melville Corp.; TJ Maxx and Marshalls consolidated under Marmaxx Group; company added to S&P 500 Composite Index
- 1998: A.J. Wright launched (eastern US value apparel chain)
- 2001: HomeSense launched in Canada; TJX revenue exceeds $10 billion
- 2003: Bob's Stores acquired (New England sporting goods/apparel chain)
- 2004: TJX tests side-by-side Marmaxx/HomeGoods superstores in the US; Fortune 500 rank 141 with ~$15 billion revenue
- 2007 (January): Computer systems intrusion disclosed — hackers accessed systems November–December 2006; 45.7 million customer records compromised; ringleader Albert Gonzalez later indicted August 2009
- 2008 (April): HomeSense launched in UK
- 2008 (August): Bob's Stores sold to Versa Capital Management and Crystal Capital
- 2010 (December): A.J. Wright stores announced for closure, cutting ~4,400 jobs
- 2012 (December): Sierra Trading Post acquired (off-price internet outdoor gear/apparel retailer)
- 2015 (July): Trade Secret and Home Secret acquired from Gazal Corporation, Australia; stores subsequently converted to TK Maxx
- 2015 (October): Ernie Herrman named CEO; effective January 2016 (successor to Carol Meyrowitz)
- 2019: Sierra Trading Post rebranded Sierra; 25% stake acquired in Russian retailer Familia (November)
- 2020: Revenue drops 31% (May–July) due to COVID-19 store closures; Q2 net loss of $214 million

## Products

**Current store brands:**
- **TJ Maxx** (US, founded 1977) — flagship apparel and home off-price chain
- **Marshalls** (founded 1956, acquired 1995) — apparel and home off-price; consolidated with TJ Maxx under Marmaxx Group
- **HomeGoods** (US, founded 1992) — off-price home furnishings, décor, and textiles
- **Homesense** (Canada, founded 2001; UK, from 2008) — home furnishings/décor
- **TK Maxx** (UK, founded 1994) — European equivalent of TJ Maxx
- **Winners** (Canada, founded 1982, acquired 1990) — Canadian apparel/home off-price
- **Sierra** (US, founded 1986 as Sierra Trading Post, acquired 2012, rebranded 2019) — off-price outdoor gear and apparel; 70+ brick-and-mortar stores

**Product categories (across all banners):** womenswear, menswear, children's apparel, footwear, accessories, home décor, home textiles, furniture, gifts, outdoor and sporting goods.

**No owned product brands** — all merchandise sourced from thousands of third-party brand vendors; assortment varies by store and season.

## Services

TJX operates exclusively through its own retail store network and associated e-commerce channels; no licensing programmes are documented. The group's retail footprint is concentrated in the US (Marmaxx, HomeGoods, Sierra), Canada (TJX Canada), and Europe (TJX International). Its growth model has been primarily through banner acquisitions (Marshalls 1995, Sierra 2012) and new-market entries (TK Maxx UK 1994, Australia via Trade Secret 2015). TJX divested Bob's Stores in 2008 and closed A.J. Wright in 2011 to concentrate on core off-price banners.

## Markets

TJX operates the world's largest off-price retail network, with the Marmaxx division (TJ Maxx and Marshalls combined) as its biggest revenue contributor in the US. The off-price model — purchasing excess inventory, end-of-season merchandise, and overruns from thousands of brand vendors at significant discounts and passing savings to consumers — gives TJX structural price advantages and a broad, constantly rotating merchandise assortment across apparel, accessories, footwear, and home categories. In home furnishings and textiles, HomeGoods and Homesense are primary off-price channels. In international markets, TK Maxx (UK, Ireland, Poland, Austria, Netherlands) replicates the TJ Maxx model. TJX's closest comparable US competitor is Ross Stores, which operates a similar off-price model.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

TJX is not a designer or producer of surface pattern design, but it is a major commercial distribution channel for pattern-designed goods at scale.

**Off-price distribution of pattern-designed goods** — TJX's purchasing model involves acquiring excess and end-of-season inventory from thousands of brand vendors, many of whom produce pattern-designed apparel, accessories, and home textiles as core product. Pattern-designed goods — printed dresses, patterned knitwear, printed scarves, surface-decorated homeware — pass through TJX's off-price retail network to consumers at volume. The constant merchandise rotation means TJX stores carry a cross-section of commercially produced surface pattern design from across the apparel and home industries.

**HomeGoods and Homesense as home textile channels** — HomeGoods (US) and Homesense (Canada, UK) are specifically positioned for discounted home furnishings and textiles: printed cushions, patterned rugs, quilts, printed bedding, decorative tableware, and surface-decorated home accessories. These categories represent a significant consumer-facing distribution channel for home textile and surface design product.

**Apparel pattern design at commercial scale** — TJ Maxx, Marshalls, TK Maxx, and Winners collectively move high volumes of discounted fashion apparel — including branded and designer clothing with surface pattern design content — to price-sensitive consumers. This makes TJX a large indirect market participant in commercial fashion print, even though it does not commission or originate designs.

**Sierra's outdoor apparel** — carries off-price outdoor and active apparel including performance fabrics and technical textiles that incorporate surface design elements such as digital print, sublimation print, and engineered stripe.
