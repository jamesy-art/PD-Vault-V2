## Overview

Façonnable is a French ready-to-wear brand founded in 1950 by Jean Goldberg, a master tailor, who opened a boutique-workshop on Rue Paradis in Nice. Its early clientele included international film stars attending the Cannes Film Festival, including Cary Grant and Tony Curtis. The name "Façonnable" — a play on "façonner" (to shape, to craft) — was introduced in 1961 by Albert Goldberg (Jean's son) when he took over the business. The brand passed from family ownership to Nordstrom ($270–370M, 2000) and Lebanese group M1 (2007), before Pepe Jeans (an M1 subsidiary) acquired it in 2016, closing the Nice headquarters and transferring operations to Barcelona. ## History

Albert Goldberg partnered with Jean-Pierre Benaym (company president) in 1971. First boutiques opened in 1973 across Monaco, Cannes, Saint-Tropez, Marseille, Lyon, Paris, and Avignon. US expansion began in 1988 via Nordstrom as exclusive distributor. A New York Fifth Avenue store opened in 1993; Beverly Hills and Costa Mesa stores followed in 1997. In 2000, Nordstrom acquired the brand for $270–370M; design offices remained in Nice; 36 European stores were operational at time of sale. In 2007, M1 Group acquired the brand from Nordstrom; Moustapha El Solh became head of company. By 2013, the company reported losses of €30.5 million on €47 million revenue; restructuring eliminated 96 of 448 positions. In 2016, M1 sold Façonnable to Pepe Jeans (an M1 subsidiary), triggering closure of the Nice HQ and relocation to Barcelona. Artistic directors: Jean Goldberg (founder, tailor, 1950); Albert Goldberg (1961); Eric Wright (2008, ex-Karl Lagerfeld and Roberto Cavalli); Julian Neale (2011, ex-Michael Kors and Céline); Daniel Kearns (2014, ex-Louis Vuitton and Yves Saint Laurent; objective: "a brand more connected to the reality of the market").

## Markets

Façonnable was positioned in the premium French Riviera resort and smart casual menswear segment — its Nice origins, celebrity clientele (Cary Grant, Tony Curtis), and Riviera boutique network placed it within the sportswear chic tradition of French Riviera style. Under Nordstrom ownership it broadened into the US premium market. Chronic losses from 2013 and successive ownership changes represent a protracted repositioning from original luxury standing.
