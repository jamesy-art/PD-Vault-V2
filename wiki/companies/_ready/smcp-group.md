# SMCP Group

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/smcp-group/profile/smcp-group-logo.jpeg)

## Overview

SMCP Group is a French holding company specialising in accessible luxury ready-to-wear, headquartered in Paris. The company was formed in 2010 through the merger of three French accessible luxury brands: Sandro (founded by Évelyne Chetrite), Maje (founded by Judith Milgrom), and Claudie Pierlot (acquired by the Chetrite/Milgrom sisters in 2009 following founder Claudie Pierlot's death from cancer). SMCP has operated through a sequence of private equity ownerships — L Capital (LVMH, 51%, from September 2010), KKR (65%, from March 2013), and Shandong Ruyi (Chinese textile conglomerate, controlling interest, from April 2016) — before listing on the Paris stock exchange in October 2017 at €22/share (market cap ~€1.7B). Shandong Ruyi's Luxembourg-based subsidiary European TopSoho defaulted on €250M of bonds in October 2021; creditors sold a 29% share and board members representing the former majority owner were dismissed in January 2022. Isabelle Guichot (former CEO of Balenciaga and Maje) has served as group CEO since August 2021. In June 2019, SMCP acquired De Fursac, a French luxury menswear brand. The group operates over 1,600 points of sale in 43 countries. India market entry was announced in 2023 through a partnership with Reliance Brands Limited, with first stores planned for 2024.

Brand creative direction is maintained independently at each brand. No group-level creative director is documented in the source. Isabelle Guichot (CEO since August 2021, formerly CEO of Balenciaga and Maje) brings creative industry experience but operates in a CEO rather than creative director role. Évelyne Chetrite (Sandro founder) and Judith Milgrom (Maje founder) were the founding creative forces of two of the three core brands; their involvement in the current group structure is not specified in the source beyond the 2009 Claudie Pierlot acquisition.

## History

- 2009: Évelyne Chetrite (Sandro) and Judith Milgrom (Maje) acquire Claudie Pierlot's eponymous label following Pierlot's death from cancer
- 2010: SMCP S.A. formed through merger of Sandro, Maje, and Claudie Pierlot
- September 2010: L Capital (LVMH private equity arm) purchases 51% stake; SMCP gains access to LVMH's textile industry experience and assets
- October 12, 2011: First SMCP store in the US opens (Maje boutique, New York City)
- March 2013: KKR (American PE firm) acquires 65% from L Capital and Florac for ~€650M
- H1 2015: Revenue +32%; SMCP roughly doubles in size 2013–2016
- April 2016: Shandong Ruyi (Chinese textile conglomerate) acquires controlling interest from KKR for ~€1.3B; strategy: leverage China's growing middle class; target "global leader in accessible luxury"
- H1 2016: Revenue +19.2%; Greater China +51%
- October 2017: IPO on Paris stock exchange at €22/share; market cap ~€1.7B; second-largest Paris IPO in 2017
- June 2019: Acquires De Fursac (French luxury menswear brand)
- Spring 2020: Claudie Pierlot (last SMCP brand) bans fur; SMCP becomes fully fur-free
- H1 2020: Revenue –31%; net loss €88.5M (COVID-19)
- H1 2021: Profit of €600K; increase of +€89M vs H1 2020
- August 2021: Isabelle Guichot appointed CEO (succeeds Daniel Lalonde who resigned)
- October 2021: Shandong Ruyi's European TopSoho defaults on €250M bonds; 29% SMCP share sold by creditors; Blackrock and Carlyle Group shareholders angered
- January 2022: Blackrock, Carlyle Group, and others under GLAS trustee vote to dismiss five board members representing European TopSoho (former majority owner)
- 2023: SMCP announces entry to Indian market via Reliance Brands Limited partnership; first stores planned 2024; 47 countries presence at this time
- April 2024: Maje partners with Save Your Wardrobe startup for clothing repair after-sales service

## Products

**Sandro** — women's and men's accessible luxury RTW; known for Parisian aesthetic; tailored separates, feminine dresses, knitwear, outerwear; the group's largest brand by revenue

**Maje** — women's accessible luxury RTW; known for a more bohemian-romantic aesthetic than Sandro; fluid silhouettes, feminine embellishment, modern casual; first US-market brand (NYC boutique 2011)

**Claudie Pierlot** — women's accessible luxury RTW; delicate, romantic, Parisian feminine direction; named after founder who died in 2009; last group brand to go fur-free (spring 2020)

**De Fursac** — French luxury menswear; acquired June 2019; tailoring-centred proposition; adds dedicated menswear brand to portfolio

## Services

SMCP operates over 1,600 points of sale in 43 countries (stated as 47 countries at the 2023 India announcement), encompassing standalone boutiques, flagships, and concessions within high-end department stores. The US entry (Maje, NYC, October 2011) was the group's first international flagship. The India entry (2023/2024) is managed through Reliance Brands Limited (RBL), following a franchise/partnership model. SMCP is listed on the Paris stock exchange (Euronext Paris) since October 2017. The group has no documented licensing programme for its brand names to third-party manufacturers.

## Markets

SMCP occupies the French accessible luxury segment — positioned between high-street fashion (Zara, H&M) and established luxury houses (Chanel, Saint Laurent). Its three core brands — Sandro, Maje, and Claudie Pierlot — target educated, fashion-conscious urban women (primary demographic) and men, with positioning built on Parisian aesthetic sensibility, premium quality at aspirational pricing, and a store format suited to high-end department stores and prime fashion streets. The De Fursac acquisition (2019) added a French luxury menswear brand to the group's primarily female-skewing portfolio. China growth (+51% H1 2016 under Shandong Ruyi ownership) underlined the strong appetite for French accessible luxury in Greater China. The group's recent governance instability (Shandong Ruyi bond default; European TopSoho board dismissal; new CEO 2021) has not prevented continued geographic expansion. The India entry (2023/2024 through Reliance Brands Limited) extends the pattern of emerging market growth.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

SMCP Group's three accessible luxury women's brands — Sandro, Maje, and Claudie Pierlot — collectively represent one of the most commercially significant buyers of premium fashion surface design in the French accessible luxury segment.

**French accessible luxury RTW as print-intensive category** — the women's RTW market at the accessible luxury price point is structurally dependent on seasonal print and surface design direction. Dresses, blouses, skirts, knitwear, and lightweight outerwear in the Sandro, Maje, and Claudie Pierlot price range are primarily differentiated by fabric selection and print design. Each of the three brands develops and refreshes its seasonal print direction independently, but within a shared Parisian aesthetic register — requiring seasonal florals, abstract prints, graphic motifs, tonal prints, and texture-based surface treatments across their product ranges.

**Sandro** — the brand's Parisian tailoring-forward identity incorporates pattern in its fabric choices: subtle check and stripe in suiting, monochrome graphic print in tops and dresses, and occasional bold print in womenswear highlights. The brand's men's line requires its own seasonal surface direction across knitwear, shirting, and casualwear.

**Maje** — Maje's bohemian-romantic aesthetic makes it the most print-oriented of the three core SMCP brands. Fluid printed dresses, floral blouses, and pattern-mixed casualwear are central to Maje's seasonal commercial offering. The clothing repair partnership with Save Your Wardrobe (2024) reflects investment in extending the life of Maje's print-forward RTW.

**Claudie Pierlot** — the brand's delicate, feminine aesthetic typically involves refined floral print, soft embroidery-inspired motif, and textured surface work in its RTW offer.

**Knitwear surface texture** — all three brands include knitwear in their seasonal range. Knitwear surface design (jacquard pattern, ribbing, intarsia motif, textured stitch) is a core surface design activity distinct from woven or printed fabric.

**L Capital (LVMH) textile access** — SMCP's 2010 investment from L Capital specifically provided access to LVMH's assets in the textile sector, documenting that LVMH-connected sourcing and production relationships were leveraged in the group's early phase.
