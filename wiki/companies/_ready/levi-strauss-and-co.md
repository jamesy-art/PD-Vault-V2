# Levi Strauss & Co.

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/levi-strauss-and-co/profile/levi-strauss-and-co-logo.png)

## Overview

Levi Strauss & Co. is an American clothing company founded in 1853 in San Francisco by Levi Strauss (1829–1902), a German-Jewish immigrant from Buttenheim, Bavaria. The company is best known for the Levi's brand of denim jeans, co-invented in 1873 by Levi Strauss and Jacob Davis — a Latvian-Jewish tailor from Reno, Nevada — who received US Patent 139,121 for the use of copper rivets to reinforce points of strain on work trousers. The 501 model, the company's founding style, received its lot number in 1890. Levi Strauss & Co. is headquartered at Levi's Plaza in San Francisco and is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: LEVI) following its IPO on March 21, 2019, which valued the company at $6.6 billion. The company reported revenues of $4.6 billion in 2016 and operates in more than 110 countries.

No named chief creative officer or head of design is documented in the available source. The brand's aesthetic is defined by its product heritage — the 501 silhouette, trucker jacket, arcuate stitching — rather than by individual creative director vision. Seasonal collections introduce trend-reactive updates in colourway, wash, print, and silhouette against the stable core product templates. Chip Bergh served as president and CEO from 2011. The brand is organised into three geographic divisions: Levi Strauss Americas (San Francisco), Levi Strauss Europe (Brussels), and Levi Strauss Asia Pacific/Middle East/Africa.

## History

Levi Strauss moved to San Francisco in May 1852 to open a West Coast branch of the Strauss dry goods business. In 1873, Davis wrote to Strauss proposing a business partnership for the copper rivet reinforcement technique; together they received the patent (May 20, 1873). Levi Strauss died in 1902, leaving the company to four nephews. The Stern–Haas family retained ownership until the company first went public in 1971; the Haas family took it private again in 1985. The company remained private until the 2019 NYSE IPO. Key corporate milestones:

- 1873: Copper rivet patent (US 139,121) with Jacob Davis
- 1890: 501 lot number assigned
- 1936: Red Tab introduced (small fabric tag on right back pocket; one of fashion's first exterior brand marks)
- 1943: Arcuate stitching trademarked in the US
- 1960s–1974: Manufacturing capacity expanded from 16 to 63+ US plants, plus 23 overseas (under George P. Simpkins Sr.)
- 1980s: ~60 US plants closed due to financial difficulties and competition
- 1986: Dockers brand launched (khaki and casual trousers)
- 1991: Saipan labor scandal (Northern Mariana Islands); Tan Holdings paid $9M+ restitution to ~1,200 workers
- 2002: Walmart "Signature" line launched (until 2006)
- 2002–2003: Closed last US manufacturing plants
- 2011: Chip Bergh hired as CEO; 20+ waterless manufacturing techniques launched (Water<Less)
- 2013: Levi's Stadium naming rights ($220.3M/20 years, San Francisco 49ers)
- 2017: Google Jacquard "smart jacket" ($350)
- 2019: IPO, NYSE: LEVI, $6.6B valuation
- 2021: Beyond Yoga activewear acquired (August 5)
- 2023 AW: WellThread sustainability capsule (100% transitional cotton, plant-based natural dyes)
- 2026: "Behind Every Original" campaign at Super Bowl LX (directed by Kim Gehrig)
- February 2026: Dockers sold to Authentic Brands Group for $311 million

## Products

- **Levi's** — core brand; denim jeans (501, 505, 511, 512, 517, 550, 569, and more), trucker jackets, tops, accessories; produced in Bangladesh, India, Egypt, Lesotho, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Indonesia, Mexico; some premium styles made in the USA
- **Dockers** — khaki and casual trousers (launched 1986); sold to Authentic Brands Group for $311M (February 2026)
- **Beyond Yoga** — women's activewear; acquired August 2021; expected to contribute $100M+ net revenue annually
- **Levi Strauss Japan K.K.** — Japanese affiliate; publicly listed

Key product identifiers (all trademarked):
- Arcuate stitching (back-pocket double arc, since 1873; US trademark 1943; 100+ jurisdictions)
- Red Tab (right back pocket fabric tag, since 1936)
- "Levi's", "501", "505", "517", "550", "569", "Silvertab", "Orange Tab", "Dockers"

## Services

Levi's distributes globally through owned retail stores, wholesale (department stores and specialty retail), and e-commerce across 110+ countries. The Walmart "Signature" line (2002–2006) was a direct mass-market entry at a lower price tier. Levi's Stadium naming rights ($220.3M/20 years, San Francisco 49ers, 2013) represent a marketing sponsorship at significant scale. The Dockers sale to Authentic Brands Group (February 2026, $311M) follows the ABG model of acquiring heritage brand IP for licensing management — consistent with ABG's acquisitions of Lee Jeans (from Kontoor Brands, May 2026), Champion (from Hanesbrands), and Reebok. Beyond Yoga (acquired 2021) is a direct-to-consumer-led activewear brand. The WellThread and Water<Less programmes are commercially relevant to sustainability-positioned retail buyers. First hemp jeans produced in March 2019 (cottonized hemp denim via Outerknown collaboration).

## Markets

Levi Strauss & Co. is the world's largest denim brand by global recognition and volume. The Levi's 501 is the reference point for the global jeans category. Peak annual sales reached $7.1 billion in 1997 before declining through the 2000s; 2016 revenue was $4.6 billion. The company occupies a mass-market premium position — more widely distributed than premium denim labels and more heritage-positioned than fast-fashion competitors. The 2019 IPO brought the company back to public markets after 34 years of family ownership. Dockers' sale to ABG (February 2026) reduces the portfolio to Levi's and Beyond Yoga. Levi's has aggressively defended the arcuate stitching trademark, filing nearly 100 lawsuits in six years (2001–2007) against companies including Guess, Polo Ralph Lauren, Esprit Holdings, Zegna, and Lucky Brand Jeans.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Levi Strauss & Co. is foundational to the surface design history of denim and workwear, and contributes several of the most commercially significant surface marks in global fashion.

**The arcuate stitching** is one of fashion's most distinctive trademarked surface design elements. Applied to Levi's back pockets since 1873 and trademarked in 1943, the arcuate (double-arc stitch pattern) identifies Levi's product at a glance and is protected in 100+ jurisdictions. The company has successfully defended this mark against Guess, Polo Ralph Lauren, Esprit Holdings, Zegna, Zumiez, and Lucky Brand, among others — making it one of the most actively enforced surface design marks in fashion.

**Denim surface finish and wash techniques** are central product development activities. Stone-washing was acquired through the Great Western Garment Co. (GWG) acquisition. Levi's Water<Less process (2011) established more than 20 waterless finishing techniques, reducing the water usage in denim washing and finishing — both a sustainability initiative and a product surface treatment innovation. Laser finishing (replacing chemical and sand techniques for creating worn or ripped surface effects) was in process by 2020.

**The Red Tab** (introduced 1936) is a small fabric tag applied to the exterior of the right back pocket — one of the earliest examples of a visible exterior brand mark as a surface identity element on a mass-market garment.

**Seasonal print and graphic collections** are significant commercial activities within Levi's women's and youth lines: printed and embroidered trucker jackets, patterned tops, and graphic tees are produced across seasonal collections and represent an ongoing applied surface design activity within the brand's product range.

**The Google Jacquard smart jacket** (2017, $350) — a conductive denim jacket developed in a two-year partnership with Google — represents an engagement with e-textile and smart surface technology, where conductive threads are woven into the fabric to enable gesture-based interaction.

**WellThread** (AW2023) used plant-based natural dyes as a sustainability-aligned surface treatment — a shift from synthetic to natural colourants on denim and related fabrics.
