# Pentland Group

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/pentland-group/profile/pentland-group-logo.svg)

## Overview

Pentland Group Limited is a British privately held multinational company that owns and invests in sports, outdoor, and sports fashion businesses. Founded in 1932 when Lithuanian immigrant couple Berko and Minnie Rubin established the Liverpool Shoe Company, the business was transformed by their son Stephen Rubin — who joined as joint managing director in 1959 and became chairman on his father's death in 1969. Pentland's trajectory was defined by its 1981 acquisition of 55% of Reebok for $77,500; by 1984, Reebok accounted for 70% of Pentland's turnover, and the 1985 partial Reebok flotation valued the business at $300 million. The family took the company 100% private again in 1999. Annual sales reached $6.4 billion; the group employs more than 50,000 people. Stephen Rubin was named Britain's top taxpayer by the Sunday Times in January 2019, with a tax liability of £181.6M.

Pentland Group operates as a holding company; individual brand design teams are led at brand level. No group-level creative direction is documented in the source. The most commercially significant design identity within the owned-brand portfolio is Speedo's swim performance aesthetic and Ellesse's Italian sportswear heritage graphic. Canterbury's design is centred on licensed national team kit development.

## History

- 1932: Berko and Minnie Rubin (Lithuanian immigrants) found Liverpool Shoe Company
- 1959: Stephen Rubin joins as joint managing director; becomes chairman on Berko's death (1969)
- 1964: Listed on London Stock Exchange
- August 1981: Acquires 55% of Reebok for $77,500
- 1984: Reebok = 70% of Pentland turnover; Pentland shares rise from £0.55 to £10 (June 1985); profits £1M (1983) → £12.9M (1985)
- 1985: Floats 60% of Reebok at $300M valuation
- 1989: Increased public offering on London Stock Exchange
- 1996: Acquires Reusch (gloves brand) for £12M
- 1998: Stephen Rubin splits CEO role; appoints son Andrew Rubin as chief executive
- 1999: Buys back entire public float; returns to 100% private family ownership
- May 2005: Acquires 45% of JD Sports Fashion from founders John Wardle and David Makin; now 55% majority shareholder
- 2013: Revenue £1.74B (+14.2%); 45% drop in pre-tax profits to £62.9M
- 2014: Revenue £1.9B (+10%); pre-tax earnings £85M; Speedo record performance
- 2015: Revenue £2.2B (+14%); Andy Rubin promoted to Chairman of Pentland Brands plc from January 1, 2015; Speedo record sales for second consecutive year
- 2001: Sells Reusch to Weitzmann family consortium
- February 2016: Andy Rubin appointed non-executive director of JD Sports board
- June 2016: Revenue £2.4B (+10%); Canterbury Brands record sales (Rugby World Cup); operating profits £220M (+36%)
- March 2017: Acquires majority stake in SeaVees (California sneaker brand)
- January 2018: 50/50 joint venture footwear partnership with Lacoste Group
- March 2018: Acquires Endura (performance cycling brand); sells Ted Baker footwear business back to Ted Baker
- 2018: Revenue £5.1B (+38.9%); operating profits £411M (+9.3%) — driven by JD Sports record performance; JD buys Finish Line (US), opens 83 new stores
- December 2019: Sells 2.47% of JD Sports shares for £180M (retains 55% majority)
- January 2020: Buys Speedo USA and Speedo North America back from PVH for $170 million in cash

## Products

## Services

Pentland Group owns 55% of JD Sports (2,500+ stores worldwide) — the portfolio's dominant retail vehicle. The group's own-brand retail and distribution operates through Pentland Brands plc (the operating brand subsidiary), which manages Speedo, Berghaus, Canterbury, Ellesse, Endura, and others. Pentland is the UK footwear licensee for Kickers and operates a 50/50 footwear joint venture with the Lacoste Group (from January 2018). The 1999 return to full private ownership was described by the family as enabling "focus on longer-term investments." The Rubin family — Stephen Rubin (chairman), Andrew Rubin (former CEO, later chairman of Pentland Brands) — retain majority control across generations.

## Markets

Pentland Group occupies a unique structural position: it is simultaneously a brand owner (Speedo, Berghaus, Ellesse, Canterbury, etc.) and the majority shareholder of JD Sports, the world's largest sports-fashion retailer by store count. This dual role means Pentland has both brand equity assets and mass-market retail distribution in the same portfolio — a structural advantage in the sports, outdoor, and sports-fashion sectors. Speedo's position as the world's leading performance swimwear brand, and Canterbury's status as the global leader in licensed rugby apparel, anchor Pentland's brand portfolio with high-visibility athletic identity. The JD Sports stake provides access to 2,500+ retail doors globally without requiring direct retail operation.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Pentland Group's portfolio spans several categories with significant surface pattern design relevance.

**Speedo swimwear** — competitive and recreational swimwear is among the most print-intensive product categories in sportswear. Speedo's seasonal swimwear collections — across both performance racing and leisure — require extensive print direction: geometric, tropical, graphic, and colour-block prints across swimsuits, trunks, and accessories. Speedo's global market leadership and high unit volumes make it one of the largest buyers of surface pattern design in the swim category. The re-acquisition of Speedo North America from PVH in January 2020 ($170M) unified the global Speedo design identity under Pentland's control.

**Ellesse** — the brand's Italian origin in tennis and ski sport in the 1970s–1980s established an aesthetic rooted in bold sportswear colour, stripe combinations, and logo application. Ellesse's lifestyle repositioning has centred on surface graphic application (logo repeat, colourful stripe, sports-heritage motif) as the primary design currency in the sports-fashion segment.

**Canterbury of New Zealand** — rugby apparel involves both performance fabric surface engineering and the design of national team and club kit graphics — a specialised form of applied surface design at international competition level. Canterbury's record sales driven by the Rugby World Cup (2016) reflect the commercial weight of licensed sports kit design.

**Berghaus** — outdoor apparel involves technical textile surface decisions (waterproof coating, reflective detail, printed camouflage/colour-block) in addition to branding/logo applications at seam and panel level.

**Lacoste footwear JV** — Lacoste's distinctive crocodile logo and signature piqué fabric surface are applied across the footwear range produced through the Pentland joint venture, making this a direct licensed brand surface application activity.

**JD Sports** — as Pentland's most commercially significant asset, JD Sports functions as the primary retail distribution channel for branded sportswear graphics and prints at global scale. The store network carries Nike, Adidas, Jordan, Converse, and all major sportswear brands — and distributes the visual languages of those brands (graphic tees, patterned knits, printed performance kit) across 2,500+ retail doors.
