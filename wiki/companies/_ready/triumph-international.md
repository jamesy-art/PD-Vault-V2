# Triumph International

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/triumph-international/profile/triumph-international-logo.jpeg)

## Overview

Triumph International is one of the world's largest manufacturers and retailers of lingerie and underwear. Founded in 1886 in Heubach, Baden-Württemberg, Germany by Johann Gottfried Spiesshofer and Michael Braun — initially as a six-person corset workshop — the company grew through the 20th century into a multinational textile group. The holding has been headquartered in Bad Zurzach, Switzerland since 1977, and the German subsidiary (Triumph International GmbH) operates from Munich. Triumph is wholly privately owned by the descendants of its two founding families (Braun and Spiesshofer); shares of the German subsidiary were traded on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange until 2011, when the company fully repurchased the outstanding minority interest and de-listed. By end of 2017, Triumph operated 3,600 controlled points of sale in 120 countries (company-owned, franchised, or partner-operated) and served 40,000 retail trade partners. It is one of Germany's largest lingerie retailers by branch count. The company has held OEKO-TEX certification on all collections since 2012.

No named creative director or individual designer is documented in the source for the Triumph or sloggi brands. Multiple seasonal collections per year are produced under the Triumph umbrella, implying structured in-house design direction. The Magic Wire bra (Red Dot Design Award 2015) and Triaction Seamless Motion Sports Bra (ISPO Award 2018) document award-winning product design in the technical underwear category.

## History

- 1886: Johann Gottfried Spiesshofer and Michael Braun found a corset factory in Heubach, Baden-Württemberg; 6 employees, 6 sewing machines
- 1890: Workforce grows to 150 employees
- 1894: First exports, to England
- 1902: "Triumph" brand registered (named after the Parisian Arc de Triomphe); "International" later added
- 1920s: Corset demand declines following economic boom; company begins parallel brassiere production
- 1930s: Corselets added to product range
- 1933: First foreign branch set up in Bad Zurzach, Switzerland
- 1950s: Offices opened in Belgium, Great Britain, Sweden, Norway, and Austria
- 1960: Asia-Pacific headquarters established in Hong Kong; ~50% market share in German corsetry by late 1960s
- 1963: First Japanese branch
- c.1970: Group sales of DM 620 million; 22,600 employees
- 1969: Tights production begins; discontinued 1972 during economic crisis
- Early 1970s: Significant difficulties from global economic crisis; introduces nylon and lycra fabric production
- Late 1970s: sloggi brand introduced (high cotton content underwear; initially women's, later sloggi Men and sloggi swim)
- 1977: Holding moves to Bad Zurzach, Switzerland
- 1986: Sales reach CHF 996 million; ~19,000 employees; China sales begin
- 1988: Individual Triumph products manufactured under licence in East Germany for local market (swimwear and underwear)
- Early 1990s: Acquired HOM (French men's underwear brand, HQ Marseille) and Valisère (French high-end lingerie); campaign featuring Naomi Campbell and Helena Christensen
- 1995: Strategy announced to focus on Triumph umbrella brand
- 1998: India market entry
- 2001: New production facility opens in Dunaújváros, Hungary (subsequently sold)
- 2008–2012: Triumph Inspiration Awards (annual lingerie design competition; annually changing theme; international final with jury + public vote; London 2009 final attracted Vogue UK coverage)
- 2010: Acquired Beldona (leading Swiss lingerie distributor); subsequent distributor acquisitions in Mexico and the US
- 2011: German subsidiary (Triumph International AG, Munich) fully re-acquired by excluding minority shareholders; de-listed from Frankfurt Stock Exchange; becomes Triumph International GmbH; wholly owned by Braun and Spiesshofer families
- 2012: All Triumph International collections OEKO-TEX certified (individual products certified since 1993)
- 2012: Largest German store opened in Dresden's Centrum Gallery
- 2015: Magic Wire bra wins Red Dot Design Award; HOM sold to Austrian Huber Group
- 2017: BeeDees brand sold to Jansen Fashion Group (Wesseling, Germany); standalone UK stores closed — UK operations moved to online + concessions at Debenhams and House of Fraser
- 2018: Hamburg Jungfernstieg flagship opens; other flagship stores in Amsterdam, Berlin, Copenhagen
- 2019: Voted most popular bodywear manufacturer brand by readers of German trade magazine Textilwirtschaft

## Products

**Triumph** — main brand; brassieres, lingerie, shapewear, sleepwear, swimwear; multiple seasonal collections per year; Red Dot Design Award 2015 (Magic Wire bra); ISPO Award 2018 (Triaction Seamless Motion Sports Bra, Health & Fitness category)

**sloggi** — cotton-forward underwear brand (late 1970s); women's, sloggi Men, and sloggi swim; thongs/briefs

**Triaction by Triumph** — sports bra line (since late 1990s)

**AMO's Style by Triumph** — third active brand

**Valisère** — upper-segment luxury lingerie label; available in Germany from 2007

**Former brands:** BeeDees (young fashion underwear, patterned fabrics; sold 2017 to Jansen Fashion Group); HOM (French men's upscale underwear; sold 2015 to Huber Group)

**Distribution:** 3,600 controlled points of sale (owned, franchise, partner) in 120 countries + 40,000 retail trade partners; online stores (via Arvato fulfillment); branches in 45 countries

## Services

Triumph operates through both wholesale (retail trade partners) and direct retail (owned and franchised stores) simultaneously. The 3,600 controlled points of sale in 120 countries include Triumph-operated retail stores, franchised locations, and partner-operated points. Following the 2017 decision to close standalone UK stores, UK operations shifted to online and concessions within Debenhams and House of Fraser. The company built its distribution in Mexico and the US through distributor acquisitions alongside the Beldona (Switzerland) acquisition in 2010. No licensing programmes are documented in the source beyond historical production under licence in East Germany in 1988.

## Markets

Triumph International is one of the world's leading lingerie companies by distribution scale: 3,600 controlled points of sale in 120 countries and 40,000 retail trade partners. The Triumph brand has been a market leader in women's and sleepwear in Germany since the 1960s, with roughly 50% of the German corsetry market in the late 1960s. The group's two core global brands — Triumph (women's lingerie, shapewear, swimwear) and sloggi (cotton-forward basic underwear) — address distinct market segments. The portfolio was streamlined through the sale of BeeDees (2017) and HOM (2015). Wholly private and family-owned since de-listing in 2011, Triumph occupies a mid-premium positioning between mass-market underwear producers and luxury lingerie brands.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Triumph International's product portfolio intersects directly with surface pattern design across several commercial textile categories.

**Fabric surface design in lingerie** — lace, embroidery, and decorative printed fabric surfaces are primary differentiators in premium lingerie and constitute a significant component of seasonal collection design. Triumph's seasonal collections involve ongoing surface design decisions across fabric selection, lace pattern, embroidery motif, and colour that define each collection's commercial identity. Valisère (luxury lingerie segment) represents more elevated fabric surface design work.

**BeeDees and patterned fabrics** — the BeeDees sub-brand (sold 2017) was explicitly documented as featuring "accessories and patterned fabrics" as product characteristics, confirming patterned textile as a commercial design feature in Triumph's youth-targeted line.

**sloggi swimwear** — swimwear is a print-intensive product category. sloggi swim involves seasonal print design applied to swimsuit fabric — a direct surface pattern design activity at commercial scale.

**Triumph Inspiration Awards (2008–2012)** — annual international lingerie design competition with an annually changing theme, judged by both professional jury and public vote. The competition engaged external designers in structured lingerie design briefs, directly intersecting with surface and structural design disciplines applied to intimate apparel. The 2009 London final attracted broader attention including coverage in Vogue UK.

**Nylon and lycra fabric transition** — Triumph's introduction of nylon and lycra in the early 1970s enabled finer surface decoration, stretch lace, and lighter fabric constructions, expanding the surface design possibilities of lingerie and swimwear products.
