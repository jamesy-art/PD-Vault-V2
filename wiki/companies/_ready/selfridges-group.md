# Selfridges Group

## Profile Image

![Profile](../../assets/companies/selfridges-group/profile/selfridges-group-logo.jpeg)

## Overview

Selfridges Group is a holding company of European luxury and premium department stores, currently owned by Thailand's Central Group (60%) and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (40%). The group was formed in 2003 when Galen Weston Sr. of the Irish-Canadian Weston family — whose Wittington Investments already held Brown Thomas (acquired 1983) and Holt Renfrew (acquired 1986) — acquired the Selfridges department store chain and integrated it with other premium retailers. De Bijenkorf (Netherlands) was added in 2011 and Arnotts (Ireland) in 2015. In 2021, the Weston family sold Selfridges Group to Central Group (Thailand) and Signa Holding (Austria) jointly; the Canadian department stores Holt Renfrew and Ogilvy were retained by the Weston family. Signa Holding collapsed in late 2023 and its founder René Benko was removed; in October 2024, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund acquired Signa's 40% stake, forming the current Central Group/PIF ownership structure. Group sales exceeded £2.8 billion in the most recent fiscal year. Current nameplates are Selfridges (UK, founded 1908), Brown Thomas (Ireland, founded 1848), Arnotts (Ireland, founded 1843), and De Bijenkorf (Netherlands, founded 1870).

Selfridges Group does not have a group-level creative director. Design is led at the nameplate level. Central Group's investment in store redevelopment (Beauty Hall, Oxford Street; Brown Thomas Dublin; De Bijenkorf Amsterdam) suggests an active programme of retail environment design under Central Group's direction. The most recent fiscal year group sales of over £2.8 billion reflect the scale of the retail operations under the group's banner.

## Products

Selfridges Group nameplates are full-line luxury and premium department stores. Documented specific product categories:

**Selfridges (UK)** — luxury multi-brand department store on Oxford Street, London (flagship); also Birmingham, Manchester, and Trafford Park; beauty hall recently transformed (Central Group-directed redevelopment); carries fashion, accessories, beauty, home, food

**Brown Thomas (Ireland)** — premium department store; Dublin and Cork; recently renovated under Central Group ownership

**Arnotts (Ireland)** — Dublin; full-line department store; broader price range than Brown Thomas; acquired 2015

**De Bijenkorf (Netherlands)** — premium department store; Amsterdam (flagship), Rotterdam, and other Dutch cities; men's department in Amsterdam recently renovated; one of the Netherlands' most historically significant retailers

## Services

Selfridges Group is a retail operator; it does not licence design or manufacture branded product. Revenue is generated through retail margin on third-party branded and own-label products across its four nameplates. The current ownership structure — Central Group (Thailand, 60%) and Saudi Arabia Public Investment Fund (40%) — reflects the globalisation of luxury retail asset ownership. Central Group also owns KaDeWe Group (Germany), making it a significant operator of European luxury department store real estate. The Weston family's retention of Holt Renfrew and Ogilvy (Canada) confirms that the Canadian business was separated from the European group in the 2022 sale.

## Markets

Selfridges Group operates at the premium and luxury end of European department store retail. The four remaining nameplates cover three markets: the UK (Selfridges, Oxford Street flagship plus Birmingham, Manchester, and Trafford Park), Ireland (Brown Thomas — Dublin and Cork — and Arnotts — Dublin), and the Netherlands (De Bijenkorf — Amsterdam, Rotterdam, and other cities). Each nameplate has significant heritage: De Bijenkorf dates to 1870 (oldest in the group), Arnotts to 1843. Central Group's current ownership brings the group into a major Asian retail conglomerate's international portfolio alongside KaDeWe Group (Germany). The Saudi PIF co-investment reflects Gulf sovereign wealth fund interest in Western luxury retail real estate and brands. The group's recent redevelopment programme — the Selfridges Beauty Hall on Oxford Street, Brown Thomas Dublin renovations, De Bijenkorf Amsterdam men's department — signals continued investment despite the ownership turbulence.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Selfridges Group's SPD relevance is structural: as a multi-brand luxury and premium department store operator across four European countries, its nameplates are among the most important retail distribution points for fashion, textiles, home goods, and accessories bearing surface pattern design.

**Luxury multi-brand retail as pattern design channel** — Selfridges, Brown Thomas, De Bijenkorf, and Arnotts are premium multi-brand retailers. Their product assortment — across fashion apparel, accessories, beauty, home, and gifts — is drawn from leading international and domestic brands. The surface pattern and print decisions in those products are made by the brands themselves; the department stores' role is curation and retail distribution. As such, the Selfridges Group nameplates function as high-visibility display environments for the commercial fashion industry's seasonal print and pattern output.

**Home textiles and décor** — department stores of this calibre typically carry substantial home product departments including bedding, cushions, throws, ceramics, wallpapers, and decorative accessories — all categories involving significant surface pattern design. No specific home brands or categories are named in the source.

**Beauty and packaging** — the Selfridges Beauty Hall renovation (Oxford Street) specifically calls out beauty as a strategic category for Central Group. Premium beauty packaging is a surface design category: fragrance bottles, cosmetics packaging, and skincare packaging involve graphic and material surface design decisions that are commercially significant at luxury retail level.

**The De Bijenkorf heritage** — as a Dutch institution dating to 1870, De Bijenkorf has a long history of curatorial textile and fashion retail that has included collaborations with Dutch and international designers across print, pattern, and applied textile art categories. The men's department renovation in Amsterdam signals continued investment in in-store design.

**Selfridges Oxford Street** — as London's most commercially significant luxury department store, Selfridges has a long history of editorial fashion curation, window display design, and branded retail environment design — all areas of applied visual and surface design.
