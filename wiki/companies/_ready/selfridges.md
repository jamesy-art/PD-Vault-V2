## Overview

Selfridges Retail Limited was founded on 15 March 1909 by Harry Gordon Selfridge, an American entrepreneur, at 400 Oxford Street, London. The building was designed by Daniel Burnham (architect of the Marshall Field's main store in Chicago), making its construction one of the earliest uses of steel cage frame construction for a UK department store. Selfridge's model — placing merchandise on open display, front-and-centre perfume counters, and transforming shopping into a social and cultural event — established the modern British department store format. The chain was acquired by the Liverpool-based Lewis's in 1951, by the Sears Group (Charles Clore) in 1965, and demerged from Sears in 1998. In 2003, Canadian businessman Galen Weston acquired Selfridges for £598 million; the store became the flagship of Selfridges Group, which also includes Brown Thomas, Arnotts, Holt Renfrew, and De Bijenkorf. In December 2021, the Weston family sold the majority of Selfridges Group to a joint venture of Central Group (Thailand) and Signa Holding (Austria) for ~£4 billion; acquisition completed August 2022. Following Signa's bankruptcy, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) acquired Signa's shares in 2024 — leaving Central Group with 60% and PIF with 40%. Selfridges does not produce its own branded fashion collections; its creative output is in retail curation, window display, event and space programming, and buying. The store's distinctive identity is defined by bold visual merchandising, art-commissioned window design, and its history as a cultural retail space. The Birmingham store's architectural disc-covered exterior is the most singular piece of institutional design in the Selfridges portfolio.

## Markets

Selfridges is the second-largest shop in the UK (after Harrods) by floor area, and one of the country's most iconic luxury retail destinations. The Daily Telegraph named it the world's best department store in 2010. The Oxford Street flagship is a tourist destination and cultural landmark; the Birmingham Bullring store (designed by Future Systems, covered in 15,000 spun aluminium discs on a Yves Klein Blue background) is named annually by Retail Week among the 100 stores to visit in the world. Selfridges occupies the top tier of UK department store luxury positioning — curating fashion, beauty, home, food, and cultural programming for a premium consumer. In August 2020, Selfridges partnered with HURR (fashion rental platform) to offer hire of 100 items from 40+ fashion brands.

## Pattern and Surface Design Relevance

Selfridges is the UK's most important luxury multi-brand department store for surface pattern design visibility:

- **Curated multi-brand fashion retail**: As a luxury multi-brand department store, Selfridges stocks hundreds of brands whose primary creative output is surface pattern design — from luxury womenswear prints to designer swimwear, printed accessories, patterned home textiles, and illustrated stationery. The store's buying and curation decisions directly influence which surface designs achieve market visibility in the UK
- **Window display as surface design showcase**: Selfridges' Oxford Street windows have been a primary canvas for fashion and art-direction display since 1909. The windows showcase branded surface design at a major public scale — seasonal fashion prints, patterned displays, and artistic surface treatments are a documented output of the Selfridges window programme. Window photographs have been published in Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, Frame, Creative Review, and WGSN
- **Home department**: Selfridges' home category stocks premium and luxury home textiles, printed accessories, tableware, and interior objects — direct surface pattern design applied to home product format
- **Beauty and fragrance packaging**: The beauty floor stocks luxury and premium brands whose packaging surface design (print, embossing, colour, material) is a core SPD category
- **Architectural surface (Birmingham Bullring)**: The Birmingham store's exterior — 15,000 spun aluminium discs applied to a Yves Klein Blue background — is itself a landmark application of surface pattern/texture to an architectural facade, designed by Future Systems (2003)
- **Fashion rental (HURR partnership, 2020)**: The HURR rental offer extends the public lifecycle of luxury fashion surface design — enabling access to designer prints and patterns through a rental model
