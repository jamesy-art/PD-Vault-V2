# Tiger Pattern

A surface pattern that replicates or references the distinctive stripe markings of the tiger — a high-contrast animal print with a specific character distinct from leopard and zebra prints.

## Overview

Tiger print is a major category within animal print surface pattern, distinguished from other large-cat prints by its stripe structure rather than spot or rosette arrangement. The tiger's orange ground with black stripes — and the knowledge that each animal carries a unique, individual pattern — has made the tiger an enduring reference for bold, energetic fashion and lifestyle prints. Tiger-stripe patterning has also had a significant independent history in military camouflage design.

## Visual Characteristics

The tiger's stripe marking is defined by:
- **Disruptive coloration:** the biological function of tiger stripes is camouflage through disruption of body outline, not background matching
- **Orange/tawny ground with black stripes:** the familiar decorative tiger print colour scheme; belly and inner limbs are white
- **Stripe individuality:** each tiger has a unique stripe pattern, comparable to human fingerprints
- **Pigment depth:** tiger stripe pigmentation extends to the skin level, so the stripe is present even on shaved or closely cropped fur

The biological mechanism producing stripe patterns is described by Turing pattern theory (morphogen reaction-diffusion systems producing self-organising stripe and spot distributions in animal coat markings).

## Pattern Structure

Tiger stripe prints in surface pattern design typically use flowing, irregular bands of varying width on an orange or tawny ground. Unlike zebra stripes (which are more even in width and sharply defined), tiger stripes tend to be more organic, with tapered ends and irregular spacing that follows the body contour of the original animal. All-over scatter repeats are standard for fabric and home textile applications.

## Common Motifs

The tiger pattern is defined entirely by the stripe structure. There are no secondary figurative elements in the base pattern (though tiger-adjacent prints may include the tiger's face or full-body representation as a placement motif). In fashion, the orange-and-black combination is the characteristic palette, though tiger prints are also produced in non-naturalistic colourways.

## Colour and Style

The naturalistic tiger palette — burnt orange or amber ground, deep black stripes, white on inner areas — is the commercial standard. Non-naturalistic versions include:
- **Black and grey tiger stripe** — monochrome, sophisticated
- **Pink and black** — fashion-forward feminine interpretation
- **Gold and black** — luxury positioning
- **White/grey ground with black stripes** — snow tiger reference, cooler aesthetic

## History and Context

Tiger-stripe camouflage is a distinct military pattern tradition with direct relevance to surface pattern design history. Developed by the South Vietnamese Armed Forces and adopted by US Special Forces in the Vietnam War era (c.1962–63), the "tiger stripe" military pattern used narrow green/brown stripes and broader black brush-stroke forms over an olive or khaki ground — 64 stripe elements in total, with interlocking (not overlapping) construction. It was produced in Thailand, Korea, and Okinawa and was derived from the French "Lizard" pattern. In more recent decades, tiger stripe camouflage has continued in use by the Philippine Naval Special Operations Command and other Southeast Asian military units. The fashion and surface pattern industry has drawn on camouflage aesthetics — including tiger stripe — in multiple print revivals. In surface pattern, decorative tiger print has been in continuous fashion use since at least the mid-20th century, most associated with bold glamour and exotic luxury aesthetics in women's fashion.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- **Women's fashion:** dresses, tops, accessories — tiger print is associated with a bold, confident aesthetic
- **Home textiles:** cushions, throws, rugs in luxury or maximalist interior styling
- **Licensing:** tiger print has strong appeal in fashion and lifestyle licensing
- **Swimwear and activewear:** animal print including tiger is a recurring commercial category
