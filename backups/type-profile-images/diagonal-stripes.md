# Diagonal Stripes

Stripe pattern structure where colour bands run at an angle across the design surface rather than horizontally or vertically. The angled line creates a sense of movement, energy, and directionality not present in straight stripe orientations.

## Overview

Diagonal stripes are a foundational stripe variant defined by their angle rather than their colour, width, or spacing. The same striping logic as horizontal and vertical stripes applies — repeating parallel bands of colour — but oriented at a bias, typically 45°. The result is a visually dynamic pattern with an inherent sense of motion and visual flow. As a structure, diagonal stripes are highly versatile: width, colour density, and angle variation each produce substantially different aesthetic registers.

## Visual Characteristics

Parallel colour bands run from one edge of the design field to the other at a consistent angle. The direction of travel creates perceived motion — the eye follows the stripe diagonally across the surface. Bold wide stripes on high-contrast grounds read as energetic and striking. Fine, closely spaced diagonals read as textured and subtle. When combined with shading or texture, diagonal stripes can create depth or three-dimensionality.

Variants include:
- **Multi-coloured**: bands cycling through several colours
- **Solid two-tone**: maximum simplicity and graphic clarity
- **Wavy or undulating diagonal**: softens the hard-edged stripe feel
- **Dotted or broken diagonal**: reduced visual weight, decorative quality

## Pattern Structure

A diagonal stripe repeat tiles by offsetting the stripe across the repeat boundary, maintaining consistent angle. The stripe's angle is defined within the tile — adjusting the tile dimensions changes the perceived angle. Unlike horizontal or vertical stripes, diagonal stripes require the repeat tile to account for the angled band crossing both horizontal and vertical edges simultaneously.

## Common Motifs

- Diagonal colour band (the defining element)
- Bias stripe (fabric/textile terminology for diagonal grain application)
- Speed line (manga/anime convention for indicating motion)

## Colour and Style

The structure is colour-independent; aesthetic register is primarily determined by palette and width combination:

- **Bold multi-colour**: sporting, energetic, pop aesthetic
- **Two-tone high-contrast**: graphic, contemporary
- **Tonal or monochrome**: sophisticated, textured, classic
- **Regimental/classic combination stripe**: heritage fashion; typically muted with structured colour progression

## History and Context

Diagonal stripes have deep roots in heraldic design: the *bend* (top-left to bottom-right diagonal) and *bend sinister* (reversed) are among the oldest charges in European heraldry. In graphic and typographic design, diagonal rules and hatching have been standard visual tools for centuries. In manga and anime, diagonal speed lines are a systematic visual convention for indicating motion, action, or dramatic emphasis. In contemporary fashion and interior design, diagonal stripes remain a perennially active structure, offering more visual interest than vertical or horizontal orientations while sharing their geometric clarity.

## Surface Pattern Uses

- Apparel: sportswear, activewear, fashion (shirts, dresses, accessories)
- Home textiles: cushion covers, throws, rugs, upholstery
- Wallpaper and tile
- Graphic design: packaging, stationery, branded visual identity
- Digital backgrounds and motion graphics
